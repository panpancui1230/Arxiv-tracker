Activation-Efficient Fine-Tuning via Sequence-Compressed Low-Rank Subspaces
Authors: Irene Tenison, Stella Ahn, Miriam Kim, Ebtisam Alshehri, Lalana Kagal
First: 2026-04-03T17:05:33+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-16T15:29:14+00:00
Comments: Accepted to AACL 2026
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) has become the standard approach for adapting large language models (LLMs). However, despite reducing trainable parameter counts, many PEFT methods remain memory-bound by token-level activation intermediates that scale linearly with sequence length, limiting their practicality for long-context and on-device adaptation. Unlike prior memory-efficient PEFT approaches that preserve token-level adapter activations, LARS (Low-memory Activation-Rank Subspace) reduces the sequence-length scaling of stored adapter activation intermediates from O(BSRL) to O(BRL) by performing adaptation within a sequence-level low-rank subspace. Through pooled feature extraction, low-rank subspace modulations, and residual projection, LARS reduces the overall memory footprint by 23% on GPUs and 47.8% on CPUs relative to LoRA at S = 1024, with relative savings increasing with context length. Experiments across reasoning, understanding, long-context, retrieval, and token-level tasks show that LARS maintains competitive performance relative to LoRA while substantially improving memory efficiency. Beyond GPUs, LARS also reduces average CPU power and energy consumption per optimisation step by 22.43% and 22.06%, respectively. These results demonstrate that activation-efficient adaptation provides a practical path toward resource-constrained and on-device fine-tuning of LLMs.

How Finite-Rank Bottlenecks Shape the Low-Rank Adaptation Landscape
Authors: Long Nguyen-Chi, Quynh Nguyen, Thanh Nguyen-Cung, Binh T. Nguyen
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-10-16T11:50:01+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-16T11:50:01+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Low-rank adaptation (LoRA) has become the standard method for parameter-efficient fine-tuning of large pretrained models, yet theoretical explanations for why low-rank updates suffice remain incomplete. Most existing analyses rely on the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) regime, which linearizes the network around the pretrained weights. In this work, we take a different approach that requires no linearization: our key observation is that, once the backbone is frozen, the loss sees each adapted weight matrix only through a finite-rank linear map induced by the frozen activations. Trace-norm regularization then forces the optimizer to pick a low-rank matrix among all matrices that produce the same observed output. This yields global minimizers whose ranks are controlled by the rank of this map, which we call the \emph{bottleneck rank}, rather than the sample size. For multi-head attention, we obtain tighter rank upper bounds by exploiting two additional invariances: row-wise softmax is insensitive to additive row constants in the score matrix, and value updates are observable only after attention weighting and frozen output projection. For the factorized LoRA objective with weight decay, we prove a complementary result on the nonconvex side: after an arbitrarily small generic positive semidefinite perturbation, every first-order stationary point is rank-deficient once the LoRA rank exceeds a threshold determined by the bottleneck rank. Finally, we show that global minimizers of the perturbed objective are near-optimal for the original trace-norm problem, and we provide uniform generalization bounds governed by the spectral structure of the frozen features.

Low-rank adaptation (LoRA) has become the standard method for parameter-efficient fine-tuning of large pretrained models, yet theoretical explanations for why low-rank updates suffice remain incomplete.
Minimax-optimal Scaling Laws in Ridge Regression via Transfer Learning
Authors: Yalda Shabanzadeh, Diyuan Wu, Marco Mondelli
First: 2026-10-16T11:41:22+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-16T11:41:22+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Pretrained models are often reused across downstream tasks, motivating the question of whether this transfer learning procedure can provide a substantial performance improvement, namely, in terms of the scaling law. We address this question in the prototypical setting of high-dimensional regression, where (i) a pretrained \emph{source} model is trained via ridge regression, and (ii) a fine-tuned \emph{target} model is regularized via the $L_2$ distance from the source estimator. First, we establish a deterministic equivalent for the risk of this two-step procedure under both model and covariance shift. Next, by specializing the deterministic equivalent to a shared regression function and commuting power-law covariance spectra, we identify a regime where the resulting scaling law achieves the minimax-optimal exponent. Notably, in this regime, the optimal source regularization depends on source data only, indicating its universality across all targets. Furthermore, we show that the same optimal rate is achieved by performing the fine-tuning step via one-pass stochastic gradient descent. We support our theoretical analysis via experiments on both synthetic settings and language models.

Pretrained models are often reused across downstream tasks, motivating the question of whether this transfer learning procedure can provide a substantial performance improvement, namely, in terms of the scaling law.
FLARE: Physics-Grounded Evaluation of Language-Model Agents for Feeder Restoration with Degraded Observations

Abstract
Restoring service after distribution-grid faults requires decisions from incomplete and conflicting information while respecting electrical constraints. Addressing this need, we introduce FLARE (Feeder-Level Agent Restoration Evaluation), a physics-grounded benchmark for post-isolation feeder restoration. FLARE con- tains 800 AC-feasible cases across five observation settings and allows agents to gather evidence, evaluate switches, and perform restoration actions under fixed operational limits. We evaluate five instruction-tuned models over 78,750 episodes, including topology, distributed-generation, and loading shifts. Mean normalized regret ranges from 0.082 to 0.148. Although every model has zero median regret, P95 regret ranges from 0.814 to 1.000, revealing an important failure tail. Shadow replay shows that 96.4% of 15,706 guard-rejected actions would have caused a physical violation. FLARE enables reproducible evaluation of language-model based restoration support while emphasizing the need for external safety checks.

LongForge: Measuring and Improving Long-Context Instruction Data via Evidence-Assertion Reasoning Graphs
Authors: Ziyang Chen, Junlong Jia, Xing Wu, Tinghao Yu, Songlin Hu
First: 2026-10-16T09:05:30+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-16T09:05:30+00:00
Comments: Work in progress
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Existing quality control for long-context instruction-tuning data is a one-shot judgment inside the generation pipeline that leaves only a scalar, revealing neither which document parts an answer uses nor how many reasoning steps it writes down; the data can thus be neither audited nor controlled. We formalize data quality as a property of the alignment structure among document, question, and answer, and build LongForge, which pairs an analyzer that measures long-context data with a synthesizer that generates it. The analyzer reverse-reconstructs each sample into an evidence-assertion reasoning graph and programmatically computes four dimensions on it: breadth, depth, correctness, and completeness. Every reading traces back to specific evidence spans and reasoning steps, making data auditable. Measuring seven mainstream datasets reveals two previously unquantified defects: breadth barely grows with document length, and depth is generally low, the two being nearly orthogonal. The synthesizer then embeds the analyzer into generation, gating on correctness and completeness and deferring breadth and depth to a training-time mixture over measured values, which makes the training distribution controllable. On two backbones, its two mixtures, LongForge-1 and LongForge-N, outperform the strongest baseline by 2.2-4.7 points on average over six long-context benchmarks, and in a controlled comparison that holds the gate and the candidate pool fixed, raising breadth and depth alone is worth a further 1.16 points, making the structural distribution of training data a lever on downstream quality rather than only a description of it.

HEM-Med: A Hierarchical Agentic Memory Framework for Safe Multi-Center Medication Recommendation
Authors: Min Xiao, Jin Li, Shoujin Wang, Yeguang Zhen, Yishuo Li, Wenpeng Lu

Abstract
Medication recommendation (MR) is critical for clinical decision support. While existing MR methods are mostly designed for single-center learning, where only one independent hospital is involved, multi-center MR with hospitals of different scales has become increasingly demanding in practice. In multi center settings, abundant data from large medical centers can improve recommendation performance for low-resource centers. Despite a few preliminary attempts on this task, two critical gaps remain. Gap 1: Existing multi-center methods fail to disentangle transferable cross-hospital clinical experience from hospital-specific prescribing patterns, limiting clinical feasibility and physician acceptance. Gap 2: They commonly overlook multi label safety constraints of MR, including drug-drug interaction risks and local formulary feasibility, which are essential for safe real-world deployment. To address these gaps, we propose HEM-Med, a novel hierarchical agentic memory framework for multi-center MR. To address Gap 1, we design a disentangled hierarchical memory module that builds patient-level medication context attribution records and disentangles them into cross hospital experience memory and hospital-specific residual memory. To address Gap 2, we introduce a safety-aware medication set generation module, which integrates dual-level experience retrieval with safety-aware set decoding to produce clinically relevant, safer, and locally adaptive medication recommendations. Experiments on a real-world multi-center eICU dataset show that HEM-Med outperforms representative transfer learning, LLM-based, and MR baselines in terms of both recommendation accuracy and safety.

ToPA: Block-wise Toeplitz Adaptation for Expressive and Efficient Fine-Tuning
Authors: Sicong Li, Qianqian Xu, Zhiyong Yang, Zitai Wang, Longtao Huang, Xiaochun Cao, Qingming Huang
First: 2026-10-16T03:38:18+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-16T03:38:18+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) has emerged as a prevalent strategy for adapting large pre-trained models. Among PEFT techniques, reparameterization-based methods, particularly those that employ low-rank or sparsity structures, have attracted significant attention. However, these approaches often underperform full fine-tuning. Specifically, low-rank methods constrain the update rank, which is misaligned with the inherently high-rank nature of full fine-tuning. In contrast, sparsity-based techniques limit the flexibility of the parameter space and compromise connectivity across weights. To overcome these limitations, we explore the use of Toeplitz matrices, whose entries are constant along each diagonal, therefore providing a compact parameterization without imposing explicit rank or sparsity constraints. A straightforward approach is to update weights via a product of Toeplitz matrices. However, similar to RNNs, long multiplicative chains often lead to gradient instability. To address this issue, we propose \textbf{ToPA}, a more refined version of this idea that replaces standard Toeplitz matrices with block-wise Toeplitz, greatly increasing each factor's capacity and shortening the chain, thereby stabilizing training and improving expressiveness. We theoretically show that products of block-wise Toeplitz matrices can approximate arbitrary matrices under shorter chain length, justifying the structural design of ToPA. Furthermore, we demonstrate that ToPA offers greater expressivity than existing low-rank and sparse parameterizations. Empirical evaluations across 21 NLP and CV datasets, spanning 5 model architectures, consistently validate the effectiveness of ToPA.
Summary / 总结
Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) has emerged as a prevalent strategy for adapting large pre-trained models.

Abstract
We extend activation steering to diffusion language models (DLMs) and study a novel problem that arose due to the inference mechanism of DLMs: Modifying a text in-place to manifest a different concept. We propose TimpaTeks, an automatic in-place text modification mechanism using DLMs. Experiments on IMDB movie reviews (sentiment) and a synthetic Cats and Dogs Dataset (arbitrary, more unconventional concept steering) show that TimpaTeks provides a feasible novel mechanism to steer diffusion language model outputs in-place. ShadowFPT: Backdooring Federated Prompt Tuning via Shadow Triggers
Authors: Kun Zhai, Teng Li, Yunhao Feng, Xingjun Ma
First: 2026-10-16T03:22:06+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-16T03:22:06+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Federated Prompt Tuning (FPT) adapts large vision--language models by freezing the pretrained backbone and optimizing only lightweight prompt parameters across clients. Although this design improves communication and parameter efficiency, it creates an overlooked security risk. Since the backbone is shared and frozen, a malicious client can induce backdoor behavior through the representation space while keeping its uploaded prompt updates close to benign ones. We propose \textbf{ShadowFPT}, a targeted backdoor attack that exploits this frozen-backbone attack surface. ShadowFPT first pretrains a learnable \emph{Shadow Trigger} against the frozen CLIP visual encoder, using either auxiliary public data or the malicious client's local data, so that triggered inputs are steered toward the target class in representation space. During federated prompt tuning, the malicious client adapts the trigger under the current global prompt and then optimizes its local prompt on both clean and triggered samples. Only prompt parameters are uploaded to the server, while the trigger and frozen encoders remain local. By shifting most of the attack burden from prompt updates to trigger-induced representation steering, ShadowFPT achieves targeted misclassification while preserving prompt-space stealthiness. Across multiple datasets, aggregation rules, and non-IID federated partitions, ShadowFPT increases the attack success rate from 19.81\% to 90.36\% in our main setting, while maintaining clean accuracy. It remains effective across textual, visual, and joint vision--language prompt tuning. These results identify frozen backbones as stealthy and underexplored backdoor surfaces in federated prompt tuning, suggesting that defenses based only on prompt-update anomaly detection are insufficient.
Summary / 总结
Federated Prompt Tuning (FPT) adapts large vision--language models by freezing the pretrained backbone and optimizing only lightweight prompt parameters across clients.
Pluvio: Assembly Clone Search for Out-of-domain Architectures and Libraries through Transfer Learning and Conditional Variational Information Bottleneck
Authors: Zhiwei Fu, Steven H. H. Ding, Furkan Alaca, Benjamin C. M. Fung, Philippe Charland

Abstract
The practice of code reuse is crucial in software development for a faster and more efficient development lifecycle. In reality, however, code reuse practices lack proper control, resulting in issues such as vulnerability propagation and intellectual property infringements. Assembly clone search, a critical shift-right defence mechanism, has been effective in identifying vulnerable code resulting from reuse in released executables. Recent studies on assembly clone search demonstrate a trend towards using machine learning-based methods to match assembly code variants produced by different toolchains. However, these methods are limited to what they learn from a small number of toolchain variants used in training, rendering them inapplicable to unseen architectures and their corresponding compilation toolchain variants. This paper presents the first study on the problem of assembly clone search with unseen architectures and libraries. We propose incorporating human common knowledge through large-scale pre-trained natural language models, in the form of transfer learning, into current learning-based approaches for assembly clone search. Transfer learning can aid in addressing the limitations of the existing approaches, as it can bring in broader knowledge from human experts in assembly code. We further address the sequence limit issue by proposing a reinforcement learning agent to remove unnecessary and redundant tokens. Coupled with a new Variational Information Bottleneck learning strategy, the proposed system minimizes the reliance on potential indicators of architectures and optimization settings, for a better generalization of unseen architectures. We simulate the unseen architecture clone search scenarios and the experimental results show the effectiveness of the proposed approach against the state-of-the-art solutions.

Refusal Is Not Robustness: Measuring Harmful Reachability in Autoregressive Language Models
Authors: Parv Kapoor, Teresa Fortes, Claudia Mamede, Rui Abreu, Jonathan Aldrich, Eunsuk Kang
First: 2026-10-15T23:45:21+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T23:45:21+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
LLM safety evaluations via jailbreaking typically index on attack success rate (ASR): whether a generated response violates safeguards. However, ASR only evaluates sampled outputs and provides little information on the underlying generation process. Autoregressive language models define distributions over possible outputs, and a sampled refusal can coexist with substantial probability mass on harmful generation paths. We study this hidden structure through bounded continuation analysis (BCA), which models autoregressive generation as a stochastic process over token prefixes and computes the finite-horizon probability that generation reaches a harmful continuation. BCA constructs a bounded continuation tree, labels terminal continuations with a semantic harm evaluator, and estimates harmful reachability using exact probabilistic model checking. BCA complements ASR by measuring how much harmful behavior remains accessible beneath a sampled safe response. This makes it useful for distinguishing models that look identical under measured ASR values, and allocating red-team effort toward regions of the generation process with greater hidden risk. Across four instruction-tuned LLMs and 100 JailbreakBench behaviors, we show that BCA effectively quantifies hidden harmful behavior and provides a useful signal to guide red teaming.

Risk and Refusal: Mechanisms of LLM Responses to Suicidal Ideation
Authors: Drishti Goel, Soorya Ram Shimgekar, Jeongah Lee, Violeta J. Rodriguez, Daniel S. Brown, Ravi Karkar, Koustuv Saha
First: 2026-10-15T22:34:45+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T22:34:45+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Refusal is a common safety mechanism in large language models (LLMs), yet in contexts of suicidal ideation (SI), refusing to engage can itself constitute a safety failure. We evaluate how eight instruction-tuned LLMs respond to SI and self-harm disclosures across three datasets, varying whether models are informed that a disclosure expresses SI and whether they receive a clinician-developed response protocol, and complement this with mechanistic analyses in six open-weight models. Our results reveal a nuanced relationship between refusal and response-level risk. At baseline, elevated risk in model responses is driven primarily by inattention to the disclosure, alongside refusal to engage. Mechanistic analyses suggest that models may refuse SI disclosures through the same direction that mediates their refusals of harmful requests. However, informing models of the SI context, or suppressing refusal directly, increases engagement without reliably lowering response risk, and may raise it. In contrast, the clinician-guided protocol consistently reduces response-level risk while also reducing refusal. Together, these analyses and ablations motivate the need for models that refuse only when warranted and engage in interactionally safe ways when users disclose SI.

Abstract
Uncertainty can measure hallucination risk in retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), but only when retrieved evidence constrains the answer. When evidence is present, unsupported factual claims become less likely; when retrieval coverage is poor, the model can answer from internal knowledge and assign high probability to unsupported claims. We test this across four instruction-tuned models on HotpotQA and SQuAD. After matching faithful and hallucinated answers within word-count bins, length-only classifiers fall near chance, while perplexity reaches 0.81--0.91 AUROC on targeted unsupported edits. Varying retrieval depth shows why the score changes meaning: detection can weaken with only the top-ranked passage, then recover after adding a second passage. A combined uncertainty and semantic-drift score improves calibration, and measurements remain above chance on RAGTruth QA. Results support a conditional view of uncertainty as a useful measure of RAG hallucination risk.

Cultural (In)Competence: Investigating Culture-Specific Stereotype Detection Capabilities of LLMs

Abstract
Stereotypes are subjective constructs that are mediated by the cultural context in which they are viewed. Successfully modeling them thus also requires cultural competence. When using large language models (LLMs) for stereotype-related tasks, such as their detection, it is therefore crucial that models can access relevant cultural knowledge and adjust responses accordingly. Since LLMs have shown disparities in what cultural knowledge is available to them, their capacity regarding stereotypes may also be disparate across languages and cultural contexts (e.g., for non-English languages or for non-US contexts). In this paper, we systematically investigate the effectiveness of LLMs for culture-specific stereotype detection across three factors: (1) the prompting language, (2) the instruction tuning language, and (3) the fine-tuning dataset size. We conduct experiments for US, Dutch, and Chinese cultural contexts. Our results suggest that LLMs do not reliably account for cultural contexts and overpredict the presence of stereotypes. Non-English prompts seem to improve consideration of cultural context and thus lead to more effective predictions. Moreover, we find that additional instruction tuning reduces false-positive rates and that training data size moderates detection effectiveness non-monotonically.

Abstract
Large language models (LLMs) can often tell when they are being tested. This ability, known as evaluation awareness, can lead models to act more cautiously during safety evaluations than in real use. Existing work has studied evaluation awareness almost entirely in English, although models serve users in many languages. Indic languages are spoken by over a billion people, yet evaluation awareness remains almost entirely unstudied in them. We test four instruction-tuned models from three families in five Indic languages alongside English. We release all 47,880 judged responses to support further work. These results suggest that safety evaluations in Indic languages may not reflect how models treat their everyday users.

Abstract
Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning has significantly improved LLMs' mathematical problem-solving capabilities, but distilling such capabilities into smaller models remains challenging due to the capacity mismatch between verbose teachers and compact students. Directly copying teachers' lengthy reasoning chains causes capacity overload, resulting in truncated outputs or repetitive failure. Existing remedies each sacrifice a critical property of CoT: implicit reasoning methods (e.g., compressing reasoning into hidden states) trade away interpretability and verifiability, while heuristic compression strategies (e.g., random step pruning) destroy logical integrity. On GSM8K, BRIDGE enables Qwen2.5-3B to achieve 11.29% accuracy improvement and 27.4% token reduction over the original model, outperforming instruction-tuned variants and distillation baselines. Zero-shot transfer experiments on SVAMP and MATH-500 further confirm the generalization of internalized reasoning. Our code and model checkpoints are publicly available at https://github.com/Applied-Machine-Learning-Lab/SDM2026_BRIDGE and https://huggingface.co/bowen0815/BRIDGE.

CART: Compatible Routing for Multimodal Continual Instruction Tuning
Authors: Ling-Xiang Zhang, Tao Hu, Da-Wei Zhou
First: 2026-10-15T16:11:22+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T16:11:22+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) achieve strong performance through instruction tuning, yet real-world applications often require them to continually acquire new capabilities from sequential tasks, i.e., Multimodal Continual Instruction Tuning (MCIT). In typical MLLMs, a projector is employed to align visual representations with the language model. However, existing methods primarily protect the language model from forgetting while continuously updating the projector across tasks. Such asymmetric adaptation can cause substantial representation drift in the projector, increasing the mismatch between the projector and language model and consequently exacerbating catastrophic forgetting. To address this issue, we propose Compatible Routing (CART), which explicitly coordinates the adaptation of the projector and language model. Specifically, CART maintains task-specific experts for both modules and combines them through weighted mixtures. CART jointly infers their weights by considering both expert relevance and cross-module compatibility, thereby promoting coordinated adaptation between the two modules. To further control model growth, we introduce relevance-guided expert consolidation, which merges experts exhibiting similar relevance profiles across samples within each module. Extensive experiments demonstrate that CART achieves state-of-the-art performance.

Pairwise AUC Optimization Needs Corrective Power: A Unified View
Authors: Jia Chen, Zhiyong Yang, Shilong Bao, Qianqian Xu, Qingming Huang
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-10-15T12:02:54+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T12:02:54+00:00
Comments: 71 pages, including references and appendices. Accepted at NeurIPS 2026
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Direct pairwise AUC-surrogate training is attractive because it trains toward the ranking metric used at test time, especially under class imbalance. This creates a direct alignment between the training objective and the evaluation metric, but such metric alignment alone does not guarantee that stochastic training remains corrective. We study this gap through Corrective Power: whether pairwise mini-batch updates produce stronger and more reliable improvements for more severe positive-negative ranking errors. Our main observation is that direct pairwise training can enter regions where severe errors remain common but no longer produce useful corrective motion. In such regions, even the mini-batch noise that would normally help SGD move along the steepest severe-error correction direction can become much weaker. In this paper, we seek a theoretical explanation for this by exploring the degenerate property of the U-statistics formed from the pairwise gradients therein. Abstract
