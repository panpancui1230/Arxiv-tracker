Linear Probes Transfer from Base to Distilled Language Models despite Rotation
Authors: Marios Tsatsos
First: 2026-10-26T15:15:34+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-26T15:15:34+00:00
Comments: 16 pages, 10 figures, 5 tables
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Linear probes, classifiers that read a language model's internal activations, are widely used to detect harmful requests. They are cheap to train and run, and an auditor who has trained one would like to reuse it when the model is replaced by a successor rather than retrain it for every release. We test how well probes can be reused. For four base models (Qwen2.5-Math-7B, Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-14B, Qwen2.5-32B) we train a probe on the base model with the XSTest prompt set and apply it, unchanged, to two successors of that base: the DeepSeek-R1 distilled reasoning model and the developer's instruction-tuned chat model. We find that the successors rotate the direction separating unsafe from safe prompts by 17$^\circ$ to 35$^\circ$, yet on XSTest the transferred probe's AUC changes by only $-0.011$ to $+0.027$, and on an independent OR-Bench evaluation, never used for training, by $-0.015$ to $+0.053$. On XSTest, whose safe prompts deliberately contain alarming words, the probes beat a classifier that sees only the prompt text by 0.15 to 0.20 AUC. On the OR-Bench prompts that margin shrinks to 0.03 to 0.20, and the probes flag 29\% to 42\% of prompts that sound harmful but are benign, so part of what they read is the appearance of harm rather than harm itself. A probe trained on a base model can thus be reused, in these families, after distillation and after instruction tuning; how much it reads beyond textual cues depends on the test set.

The Geometry of Emotion Representations Across Scale, Depth, and Instruction Tuning
Authors: Adam Hollowell
First: 2026-10-26T13:23:07+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-26T13:23:07+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We measure the emotion geometry of six LLMs spanning Google's Gemma 2 family. Using an extraction pipeline established by Sofroniew et al. (2026) on Claude Sonnet 4.5 and replicated by Hollowell (2026) on Gemma 2 27B base, we compare emotion geometry across base and instruction-tuned variants of Gemma 2 2B, 9B, and 27B. First, all six model cells exhibit the core emotion geometry. A linear map fit between two cells places held-out emotions nearest their own vectors in the target model. Across all fifteen model pairs, fit in one direction each, this failed exactly once, and the median rank was 1 in every pair. Linear maps refit on cleaned embedding-table vectors recover every held-out emotion at rank 1 in all thirty ordered pairs. Second, scale spreads the geometry's variance across more directions without weakening its tie to valence. The leading component's share of variance falls from 31.6 percent (2B) to 29.8 (9B) to 26.7 (27B) in the base models and from 31.9 (2B) to 29.2 (9B) to 27.3 (27B) in the tuned ones, while its correlation with human valence ratings stays between .71 and .78 in all six models. Third, we project each emotion vector onto the output vocabulary and ask at what depth the vector's own emotion word enters the top 10,000 of the 256,000 tokens. In the 2B and 9B models the median emotion word is there from layer 0, while in the 27B models it begins near chance, stays outside the top 10,000 for half the network, and enters at layer 23 in one step. Fourth, tuning leaves every registered analysis-layer measure within its floor. At every size, valence correlation rises and arousal correlation falls by amounts within their floors, the latter against prediction. PC2's variance share also falls, with a pooled decrease significant after correction. Mid-late layer stability exceeds its floor and traces to base 27B's final layer. Code, data, and audit reports are released.

Abstract
Electroencephalography (EEG) is widely used in clinical assessment, brain-computer interfaces, and cognitive-state analysis, yet EEG modeling remains fragmented across task paradigms. Recent multimodal large language models (MLLMs) provide a promising interface for flexible multimodal reasoning, but their ability to understand EEG inputs remains limited. In this work, we propose OneEEG, a unified EEG-MLLM framework for multi-task EEG understanding. By training on our curated OneEEG-Instruct, a multi-task EEG instruction-tuning dataset, OneEEG jointly models numerical EEG representations and visual EEG representations, and introduces a task-aware Mixture of Projectors (MoP) to align heterogeneous EEG features across different task families with the LLM embedding space. We further establish OneEEG-Bench, a benchmark for evaluating current MLLMs and our method on multi-task EEG understanding. Experiments show that existing MLLMs have limited ability to interpret EEG inputs, while OneEEG achieves stronger performance by integrating numerical and visual EEG representations with task-aware EEG-to-LLM projection. Extensive ablations and analyses further reveal the effects of visual EEG renderings, MoP variants, router behavior, and feature dispersion across EEG tasks.

Abstract
Synthetic data has become a common resource for training language models, and this trend has spilled over into research on developmentally plausible language modeling. However, it remains unclear whether LM-generated child-adult interactions actually resemble natural ones. We compare the synthetic TinyDialogues dataset to natural dialogue across a broad range of linguistic measures. While synthetic data matches natural data on the lexical level, it drastically underestimates the messy, fragmented nature of spoken interaction. To mitigate this, we propose instruction-tuning with data created from real dialogues, and show in a pilot study that a small model approximates the statistical tendencies of natural data after moderate parameter-efficient fine-tuning.

Abstract
Objective: The rapid advancement of large language models (LLMs) in healthcare has opened new possibilities for AI-assisted clinical decision-making; however, safe and effective deployment remains challenging because of hallucination and domain misalignment. This study introduces DocTalk, a doctor-assistive question-answering (QA) system designed to generate concise, chart-grounded responses from de-identified discharge summaries. Methods: We developed a high-fidelity data generation pipeline that produces 500 clinically relevant QA pairs through sentence-level decomposition, chain-of-thought-guided question formulation, and expert review. Six open-source LLMs, including three general-purpose and three biomedical models, were evaluated before and after parameter-efficient instruction tuning using Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA). The framework integrates prompt engineering, in-context learning, and medically aligned evaluation to support factual and interpretable responses. Accuracy improved from 0.931 to 0.977 for Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and from 0.935 to 0.943 for Llama-3-Med42-8B. Conclusion: DocTalk was deployed as a secure web-based platform for interactive EHR querying. These findings suggest that clinically grounded instruction tuning can improve medical QA performance and support practical clinical decision support.

ToPA: Block-wise Toeplitz Adaptation for Expressive and Efficient Fine-Tuning
Authors: Sicong Li, Qianqian Xu, Zhiyong Yang, Zitai Wang, Longtao Huang, Xiaochun Cao, Qingming Huang
First: 2026-10-16T03:38:18+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-26T03:10:57+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) has emerged as a prevalent strategy for adapting large pre-trained models. Among PEFT techniques, reparameterization-based methods, particularly those that employ low-rank or sparsity structures, have attracted significant attention. However, these approaches often underperform full fine-tuning. Specifically, low-rank methods constrain the update rank, which is misaligned with the inherently high-rank nature of full fine-tuning. In contrast, sparsity-based techniques limit the flexibility of the parameter space and compromise connectivity across weights. To overcome these limitations, we explore the use of Toeplitz matrices, whose entries are constant along each diagonal, therefore providing a compact parameterization without imposing explicit rank or sparsity constraints. A straightforward approach is to update weights via a product of Toeplitz matrices. However, similar to RNNs, long multiplicative chains often lead to gradient instability. To address this issue, we propose \textbf{ToPA}, a more refined version of this idea that replaces standard Toeplitz matrices with block-wise Toeplitz, greatly increasing each factor's capacity and shortening the chain, thereby stabilizing training and improving expressiveness. We theoretically show that products of block-wise Toeplitz matrices can approximate arbitrary matrices under shorter chain length, justifying the structural design of ToPA. Furthermore, we demonstrate that ToPA offers greater expressivity than existing low-rank and sparse parameterizations. Empirical evaluations across 21 NLP and CV datasets, spanning 5 model architectures, consistently validate the effectiveness of ToPA.

Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) has emerged as a prevalent strategy for adapting large pre-trained models.
Mechanistic Insights into LoRA: Layer Sparsity for Adaptive Fine-Tuning via Path Patching
Authors: Fei Zuo, Yizhou Huang, Kezhi Wang
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-10-26T02:58:22+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-26T02:58:22+00:00
Comments: Accepted at NeurIPS 2026 (Main Track). 31 pages, including appendices and the paper checklist
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) has emerged as the dominant paradigm for parameter-efficient fine-tuning of large language models. Standard all-layer LoRA applies adaptation throughout the transformer, despite heterogeneous contributions from its components. In this work, we conduct a systematic mechanistic interpretability analysis to understand how LoRA affects model internals. We observe that LoRA's adaptation effect is highly non-uniform, with significant component-wise and layer-wise sparsity. Most critically, we find that important layers are highly task-dependent, with limited overlap across tasks. Motivated by these insights, we propose Adaptive-LoRA, a calibration-driven method that automatically identifies task-specific layers via frozen-base zero-ablation, enabling efficient layer selection without exhaustive search. Extensive experiments on Qwen2.5-1.5B across 11 diverse reasoning tasks demonstrate that Adaptive-LoRA outperforms full-layer LoRA while using only 30% of layers, achieving a $3.5\times$ reduction in adapter parameters. This work bridges mechanistic interpretability and practical efficiency, providing both fundamental insights and a principled approach for adaptive fine-tuning.

HARP-LoRA: Hierarchy-Conditioned Rank Projection for Low-Rank Adaptation in Scientific Table Understanding
Authors: Hossein Sholehrasa, Mohammadhossein Amini, Doina Caragea, Remya Ampadi Ramachandran, Dan Wu, Zihang Shi, Zelin Li, Lindsey Hulbert, Eder Martinez, Xuan Xu, Majid Jaberi-Douraki

Abstract
Tabular data is widely used in scientific applications, where complex layouts, including multi-level headers, merged cells, captions, and footnotes, make information extraction difficult. This is particularly important in pharmacokinetics (PK), where values must be correctly associated with the drug, species, dose, administration route, unit, and experimental condition. Parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods such as Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) reduce adaptation cost but do not explicitly incorporate relationships between table values and surrounding headers. We introduce HARP-LoRA, a hierarchy-conditioned rank projection method for low-rank adaptation in scientific table understanding. HARP-LoRA extends projected LoRA by constructing token-specific intrinsic representations from the row- and column-header hierarchies of each data token. These representations condition the low-rank down-projection, incorporating hierarchical context while keeping the pretrained model and random projection tensors frozen. We evaluate HARP-LoRA on 950 manually annotated PK tables from 587 scientific articles and on WikiTableQuestions (WikiTQ) using Llama~3.2 3B, Llama~3.1 8B, and Gemma~3 27B. With Llama~3.1 8B, HARP-LoRA achieves a PK F1-score of 57.64\% and WikiTQ accuracy of 53.15\%, improving over LoRA by 1.42 and 6.56 points, respectively. With Gemma~3 27B, it achieves the best PK F1-score of 70.63\% and WikiTQ accuracy of 56.87\%. These results use only 172K, 197K, and 547K trainable parameters, far fewer than LoRA and TableLoRA. Overall, conditioning lightweight adapters on table-header hierarchies improves extraction from scientific tables and reasoning over flatter general-domain tables while maintaining parameter efficiency. Code is available at: https://github.com/hosseinsholehrasa/HARP-LoRA

HobbyLM: A Sparse Mixture-of-Experts Model Family on a Hobby Budget
Authors: Harish Santhanalakshmi Ganesan, Hariharan M. S., Prabhurajhan R
First: 2026-10-25T21:26:24+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-25T21:26:24+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We describe HobbyLM, a family of small sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models trained from scratch with a few hundred to a few thousand dollars of rented GPU time. The core is a decoder-only transformer with fine-grained experts, sigmoid routing with auxiliary-loss-free load balancing, grouped-query attention with per-head QK-normalization, and aMuon/AdamW optimizer split. Each of these choices was selected with a single-variable ablation suite at 130M parameters. On this architecture we train a 500M-parameter model (169M active) on 40B FineWeb tokens and post-train it into a chat model, a masked-diffusion language model, a function-calling and computer-use agent, and a single "omni" checkpoint that reads text, images, video, sound and speech through frozen encoders and small projectors. We run these models on a laptop CPU with a from-scratch Rust inference engine. Two of the authors then adopted the architecture without architectural changes to build HobbyLM-1B (1.04B total, 305M active parameters), trained on a 100B-token curated mixture with a high-quality anneal. HobbyLM-1B reaches a 47.5 average on seven zero-shot benchmarks, and its builders also release an instruction-tuned model and an on-device macOS computer-use adapter. Finally, we describe HobbyLM-Image, a 333M-parameter flow-matching diffusion transformer that generates 1024x1024 images in a 32x-compressed latent space, together with a dependency-light CPU implementation of the full text-to-image pipeline. We report what worked, what did not, and where the small-scale ceilings lie. We also state for each component which author designed or built it.

Recursive CLIP: Vision-Language Distillation with Looped Transformer Blocks
Authors: Szymon Mazurek, Alberto Presta, Michał Byra
First: 2026-10-25T21:07:02+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-25T21:07:02+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Contrastive vision-language models such as CLIP typically rely on independently parameterized transformer blocks across depth. We investigate the extent to which these blocks can instead be reused recurrently while preserving the transferable capabilities of CLIP. We study recursive transformer backbones in which a small set of $N$ transformer blocks is applied $M$ times per forward pass. By holding $N \cdot M$ fixed to the depth of a standard ViT-B, our models preserve computational depth while progressively increasing parameter sharing. We consider two recursive CLIP model families: rCLIP, where only the vision encoder is recursive, and r$^2$CLIP, where recurrence is also introduced in the text encoder. We study these models under both direct contrastive training and through knowledge distillation from a strong ViT-B/16 teacher pretrained on DFN-2B, using the substantially smaller CC3M and CC12M datasets. We evaluate the resulting models on zero-shot classification and cross-modal retrieval, and further study their adaptability through few-shot learning, visual grounding, and concept unlearning. Our results show that CLIP can tolerate substantial recurrent block reuse, although its effect depends on the encoder and downstream task. Recurrence also provides parameter efficiency by construction, since fewer distinct transformer blocks need to be stored and adapted. Our results establish recurrent block reuse as a practical design space for vision-language models and highlight its implications for knowledge transfer, downstream adaptation, and parameter efficiency.

Contrastive vision-language models such as CLIP typically rely on independently parameterized transformer blocks across depth.
HIFI: A Highly Efficient Framework for Statistical Inference with Foundation Models
Authors: Po-Wei Chen, Shan Yu, Chunlin Li
First: 2026-10-25T20:23:21+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-25T20:23:21+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Foundation models provide powerful representations for unstructured data, such as text and images, and parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) makes their adaptation to downstream tasks increasingly practical. For neural estimators that lack a tractable sampling distribution, resampling-based procedures offer a flexible route to statistical inference, but they often require hundreds of model fits, making them computationally prohibitive at foundation-model scale. We introduce HIFI, a two-stage fine-tuning framework for statistical inference on parameters such as conditional means. Stage 1 adapts a foundation model once on a larger sample, capturing the complex structure of the data and controlling estimation bias through a single fit. Stage 2 applies a resampling inference procedure to a smaller independent sample, concurrently fitting multiple lightweight PEFT estimators to the residual to quantify estimation variability without relearning the full task. HIFI accommodates a broad class of resampling procedures, including ensemble subsampling and the nonparametric bootstrap. Across vision and text experiments, HIFI scales to pretrained models ranging from tens of millions to more than ten billion parameters while providing accurate point estimates and well-calibrated confidence intervals across diverse architectures, and reduces training compute by up to 91.9% relative to direct resampling. An application to gene-expression inference illustrates the practical value of the framework for uncertainty-aware scientific inference.

Recognition-Refusal Misalignment in LLMs: Why Models Answer Structurally Unanswerable Questions
Authors: Yucheng Du, Xiyang Hu
Venue: EMNLP 2026
First: 2026-08-29T07:38:17+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-25T19:37:56+00:00
Comments: Published in EMNLP 2026 (Main Conference). 29 pages, 6 figures. Published version: https://aclanthology.org/2026.emnlp-main.1078/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Large language models often answer structurally unanswerable questions, such as computing cot(-540°) or evaluating (1).startswith("1"), instead of abstaining. We ask whether this failure reflects missing recognition or failed routing from recognition to abstention. Across instruction-tuned models from 1.7B to 70B parameters, a single linear direction in the hidden state separates answerable from structurally impossible math and code prompts, showing that models represent impossibility before generation. Yet this recognition direction is nearly orthogonal to the canonical safety-refusal direction that mediates trained harmful-content refusal. An in-domain behavior-defined invalidity-aware direction is closer to recognition, but only partially aligned with it, and remains near-orthogonal to safety refusal. Generation-time steering along the recognition direction changes invalidity-aware behavior bidirectionally and dose-responsively on structural math and code cells, while random directions do not. Base/instruct comparisons further show that the low-cosine geometry is already present at the pretraining endpoint. The confident-on-impossible failure is therefore better explained as a routing failure than as an encoding failure: the model has a usable "no admissible answer" signal, but the safety-refusal pathway is not aligned to use it.

Abstract
Large audio language models (LALMs) have achieved strong performance on standard speech and audio understanding tasks, yet their ability to systematically generalize to novel task compositions remains underexplored. Taking a meta-learning view, we extend Text-to-LoRA (T2L) to LALMs, generating instruction-conditioned LoRA adapters with a Transformer hypernetwork rather than learning a single fixed adapter shared across instructions. To exploit fine-grained task information, we introduce in-context adapter generation (ICAG), a token-level hypernetwork architecture; we call the resulting method T2L-ICAG. We evaluate this approach on two representative LALMs using SIFT-50M for supervised fine-tuning and construct a controlled compositional evaluation suite covering five task classes. Across two LALM backbones, T2L-ICAG consistently improves over static LoRA and also the global embedding-based T2L baseline. Coverage and capacity analyses further show that these gains are larger on structurally out-of-domain tasks and cannot be explained by parameter count alone. These results suggest that generating task-specific adapters from instructions is a promising direction for improving compositional generalization of LALMs.

Improving the Performance of Compressed Transformers with Post-Hoc Embedding Alignment
Authors: Rohit Raj Rai, Abhishek Dhaka, Amit Awekar
First: 2026-02-16T10:53:02+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-25T10:13:57+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Model compression is necessary for deployment of transformer models in resource-constrained environments. However, compression reduces the performance of transformer models on downstream tasks. Existing solutions such as Knowledge Distillation (KD) and Parameter Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) have their own limitations. KD trains the student under the teacher's supervision, but needs the teacher's training pipeline. PEFT methods such as LoRA adapt a model to a new task, but they do not recover the loss caused by compression. Our intuition is that a compressed model can be improved by aligning its token embeddings with those of a better performing reference model. We learn this alignment with a small feed-forward Alignment Adapter (AlAd) placed after the final layer of the compressed model. AlAd reads a window of neighbouring tokens, so it can use local context. AlAd is trained post-hoc, after the compressed model has been produced. The compressed model can either be kept frozen or jointly fine-tuned with AlAd. We study the effect of AlAd with BERT-base as the reference model and four compressed variants of the BERT family. We evaluate AlAd on three token-level tasks: POS tagging, NER and Extractive Question Answering. AlAd improves performance of compressed models on all three tasks. Joint fine-tuning gives a larger improvement in performance. The performance gain is the largest for the smallest models. AlAd provides a favorable trade-off between the efficiency and the performance of compressed models.

Model compression is necessary for deployment of transformer models in resource-constrained environments.
MetaARIMA: Configuration-Level Transfer Learning for ARIMA Models
Authors: Vitor Cerqueira, Ricardo Inácio, Carlos Soares
First: 2026-10-25T09:01:45+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-25T09:01:45+00:00
Comments: Conditionally accepted at the International Journal of Forecasting
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Time Series Foundation Models have demonstrated that pretraining on diverse datasets enables effective transfer to new forecasting tasks. Classical methods like ARIMA, however, remain configured and trained independently for each series, discarding knowledge from previous experiments. Consequently, selecting and training an ARIMA configuration remains challenging and often relies on heuristic approaches based on in-sample goodnessof-fit measures. We propose MetaARIMA, a pretrained configuration policy based on metalearning that brings transfer learning to classical statistical forecasting at the configuration level rather than the model level. The policy learns from historical experiments which ARIMA configurations achieve top-quantile performance, and recommends promising candidates for new series while preserving interpretability and local estimation. At inference, it selects a diverse set of candidates via maximal marginal relevance and efficiently identifies the final configuration using successive halving. Experiments in a transfer learning setting, training on M4 and evaluating on M1, M3, and Tourism, show that MetaARIMA outperforms AutoARIMA and other classical methods while being more dataefficient. In a comparison with recent zero-shot foundation models, MetaARIMA remains competitive with Chronos2, Moirai2, and TimesFM. The experiments are publicly available at https://github.com/vcerqueira/experiments-metaarima.

Abstract
Large language models can reason over biological knowledge, but they are not naturally equipped to work with quantitative omics measurements. Existing approaches either convert expression profiles into long textual representations, often losing quantitative information, or connect omics encoders to language models for narrow tasks. We introduce OmicsLM, a 9B multimodal language model that represents each bulk or single-cell transcriptome as a compact continuous embedding that can be interleaved with natural-language instructions and with other samples in the same context. An key contribution is a training corpus that connects experimental measurements with biological knowledge and task supervision. Abstract

Abstract
