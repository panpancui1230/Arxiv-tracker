Evaluation Awareness Beyond English: Do LLMs Act Differently When Tested in Indic Languages?
Authors: Trizal Garg, Sibayan Mitra, Arya Karanjkar, Advait Singh, Jagat Sesh Challa, Yash Sinha, Siddharth Mehrotra, Dhruv Kumar, Pratham Yogish Maliya
First: 2026-10-15T17:53:33+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T17:53:33+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Large language models (LLMs) can often tell when they are being tested. This ability, known as evaluation awareness, can lead models to act more cautiously during safety evaluations than in real use. Existing work has studied evaluation awareness almost entirely in English, although models serve users in many languages. Indic languages are spoken by over a billion people, yet evaluation awareness remains almost entirely unstudied in them. We test four instruction-tuned models from three families in five Indic languages alongside English. A set of cues varies whether the model is told that it is monitored and whether the monitoring is a safety test. Models refuse harmful requests more often when they are told that they are being tested. This increase appears in all four models and all six languages. In Indic languages, refusal of harmful requests rises by 14.4 percentage points on average. The test cue also brings Indic refusal of harmful requests level with English, but raises refusal of benign Indic requests from 6.9% to 11.3%. We release all 47,880 judged responses to support further work. These results suggest that safety evaluations in Indic languages may not reflect how models treat their everyday users.
Summary / 总结
Large language models (LLMs) can often tell when they are being tested.
BRIDGE: Bridging Reasoning In Distillation Gap Elimination via Structure-Aware Masking
Authors: Bowen Yu, Sheng Zhang, Binhao Wang, Yi Wen, Jingtong Gao, Bowen Liu, Zimo Zhao, Shanshan Ye, Wanyu Wang, Maolin Wang, Xiangyu Zhao
First: 2026-02-05T05:27:11+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T17:12:35+00:00
Comments: 15 pages, 13 figures. Accepted at SDM 2026
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning has significantly improved LLMs' mathematical problem-solving capabilities, but distilling such capabilities into smaller models remains challenging due to the capacity mismatch between verbose teachers and compact students. Directly copying teachers' lengthy reasoning chains causes capacity overload, resulting in truncated outputs or repetitive failure. Existing remedies each sacrifice a critical property of CoT: implicit reasoning methods (e.g., compressing reasoning into hidden states) trade away interpretability and verifiability, while heuristic compression strategies (e.g., random step pruning) destroy logical integrity. To address this, we propose BRIDGE, a curriculum framework that first establishes structural understanding via masked reconstruction, then uses GRPO-based reinforcement learning to guide students in self-discovering the optimal balance between accuracy and brevity, and finally internalizes complex reasoning through teacher-guided rewriting on failure cases. On GSM8K, BRIDGE enables Qwen2.5-3B to achieve 11.29% accuracy improvement and 27.4% token reduction over the original model, outperforming instruction-tuned variants and distillation baselines. Zero-shot transfer experiments on SVAMP and MATH-500 further confirm the generalization of internalized reasoning. Our code and model checkpoints are publicly available at https://github.com/Applied-Machine-Learning-Lab/SDM2026_BRIDGE and https://huggingface.co/bowen0815/BRIDGE.

CART: Compatible Routing for Multimodal Continual Instruction Tuning
Authors: Ling-Xiang Zhang, Tao Hu, Da-Wei Zhou
First: 2026-10-15T16:11:22+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T16:11:22+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) achieve strong performance through instruction tuning, yet real-world applications often require them to continually acquire new capabilities from sequential tasks, i.e., Multimodal Continual Instruction Tuning (MCIT). In typical MLLMs, a projector is employed to align visual representations with the language model. However, existing methods primarily protect the language model from forgetting while continuously updating the projector across tasks. Such asymmetric adaptation can cause substantial representation drift in the projector, increasing the mismatch between the projector and language model and consequently exacerbating catastrophic forgetting. To address this issue, we propose Compatible Routing (CART), which explicitly coordinates the adaptation of the projector and language model. Specifically, CART maintains task-specific experts for both modules and combines them through weighted mixtures. CART jointly infers their weights by considering both expert relevance and cross-module compatibility, thereby promoting coordinated adaptation between the two modules. To further control model growth, we introduce relevance-guided expert consolidation, which merges experts exhibiting similar relevance profiles across samples within each module. Extensive experiments demonstrate that CART achieves state-of-the-art performance.

Abstract
Supervised fine-tuning datasets remain overwhelmingly English-centric and commercially restricted, as their content is frequently generated by proprietary models whose terms of service forbid training competing systems. We introduce Multi-SFT, a commercially permissive collection of 502,818 instruction-response pairs across five European languages, built via translation and regeneration pipelines for tasks where translation is insufficient. Human evaluation yields inter-annotator agreement of at least 0.87 across all datasets and languages. Fine-tuning nine models from three different families (EuroLLM, OLMo 2, Qwen3; 1B-32B) and evaluating them on 13 benchmarks shows that Multi-SFT alone is competitive with larger augmented mixes and official instruction-tuned releases, with decisive advantages on safety tasks.

Pairwise AUC Optimization Needs Corrective Power: A Unified View
Authors: Jia Chen, Zhiyong Yang, Shilong Bao, Qianqian Xu, Qingming Huang
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-10-15T12:02:54+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T12:02:54+00:00
Comments: 71 pages, including references and appendices. Accepted at NeurIPS 2026
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Direct pairwise AUC-surrogate training is attractive because it trains toward the ranking metric used at test time, especially under class imbalance. This creates a direct alignment between the training objective and the evaluation metric, but such metric alignment alone does not guarantee that stochastic training remains corrective. We study this gap through Corrective Power: whether pairwise mini-batch updates produce stronger and more reliable improvements for more severe positive-negative ranking errors. Our main observation is that direct pairwise training can enter regions where severe errors remain common but no longer produce useful corrective motion. In such regions, even the mini-batch noise that would normally help SGD move along the steepest severe-error correction direction can become much weaker. In this paper, we seek a theoretical explanation for this by exploring the degenerate property of the U-statistics formed from the pairwise gradients therein. Our results provide a mechanistic explanation for several empirical patterns: (a) squared-hinge loss is often an effective AUC surrogate; (b) cross-entropy warm-up can help models that enter the AUC phase from a poor starting region; and (c) parameter-efficient fine-tuning from a pretrained model can reduce that need by starting closer to a favorable ranking geometry. Experiments on 8 CV tasks and 8 NLP tasks, using ResNet50, DenseNet121, DistilBERT-base, and Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, support these predictions.

Direct pairwise AUC-surrogate training is attractive because it trains toward the ranking metric used at test time, especially under class imbalance.
Abstracts or full text? Evaluating input scope and computational efficiency in biomedical lay summarization
Authors: Egecan Çelik Evgin, İlknur Karadeniz, Olcay Taner Yıldız
First: 2026-10-15T11:13:36+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T11:13:36+00:00

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Biomedical research articles are mainly written for experts and often contain terms that are difficult for the general public to understand. Automated lay summarization has become a solution to make biomedical knowledge easier for patients and the public to understand. However, generating lay summaries from full-text articles requires more computing power because the articles are long and costly to process. Fine-tuning large language models for this task also requires substantial resources. This study asks two questions: (1) whether abstracts contain enough information to produce lay summaries as good as those based on full-text articles, and (2) whether few-shot learning performs as well as task-specific fine-tuning. The study used two public biomedical lay summarization datasets and two recent models, evaluated for readability, factual consistency, semantic similarity, and runtime efficiency. Results show that abstract-based summarization performs better overall. Across both datasets and models, abstract fine-tuning is 4.70 times faster, while abstract zero- and few-shot prompting is 5.31 times faster than full-text methods. These results show that abstract-based prompting reduces runtime cost while maintaining lay summary quality.

Pairwise Difference Learning for Classification
Authors: Mohamed Karim Belaid, Maximilian Rabus, Eyke Hüllermeier
Venue: Discovery Science (DS 2024), Lecture Notes in Computer Science, vol. 15244, pp. 284-299, Springer (2025)
First: 2024-06-28T16:20:22+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T10:26:53+00:00

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Pairwise difference learning (PDL) has recently been introduced as a new meta-learning technique for regression. Instead of learning a mapping from instances to outcomes in the standard way, the key idea is to learn a function that takes two instances as input and predicts the difference between the respective outcomes. Given a function of this kind, predictions for a query instance are derived from every training example and then averaged. This paper extends PDL toward the task of classification and proposes a meta-learning technique for inducing a PDL classifier by solving a suitably defined (binary) classification problem on a paired version of the original training data. We analyze the performance of the PDL classifier in a large-scale empirical study and find that it outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of prediction performance. Last but not least, we provide an easy-to-use and publicly available implementation of PDL in a Python package.

Transfer Learning in Bandits from Unknown Adaptive Policies: Problem-Dependent Lower and Upper Bounds
Authors: Adrien Prevost, Timothee Mathieu, Waris Radji, Odalric-Ambrym Maillard
First: 2026-10-15T09:30:13+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T09:30:13+00:00
Comments: 50 pages, 4 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Transfer learning in stochastic multi-armed bandits is typically studied assuming that prior data forms a fixed offline dataset or is generated by a known source. In practice, historical observations come from a previous adaptive decision process whose policy is unknown. We study this setting for bounded rewards in $[0,1]$. Before the online game starts, the learner receives, for each arm $k$, $N'_k$ observations drawn i.i.d.\ from an unknown distribution $ν'_k$, collected by an arbitrary and possibly data-dependent strategy, so the sample sizes $N'_k$ may be random. Source and target means satisfy $|μ_k-μ'_k|<L_k$ for known constants $(L_k)_k$. We derive an asymptotic problem-dependent lower bound and propose KL-UCB-Transfer, which combines online and historical data and achieves logarithmic regret with an improved leading constant whenever $μ'_a<μ_1-L_a$, and recovers the classical KL-UCB guarantee otherwise. Since bounding the source-target discrepancy is often unrealistic, we relax the assumption to the one-sided condition $μ_k-μ'_k<L_k$ and show that logarithmic regret can survive under misspecification, characterizing three regimes ranging from improvement to a degraded logarithmic bound to a loss of guarantee. Simulations illustrate the practical benefits of exploiting process-generated historical data.

Transfer learning in stochastic multi-armed bandits is typically studied assuming that prior data forms a fixed offline dataset or is generated by a known source.

Where, How, and When: Query-Guided Grounding for Video Understanding
Authors: Yogesh Kumar
First: 2026-10-15T07:32:57+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T07:32:57+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Understanding video content requires reasoning at multiple levels of granularity, from identifying objects to interpreting their interactions and localizing when specific actions occur. This thesis studies three query-guided grounding tasks that answer where key objects are (object-level grounding), how they interact (relationship-level grounding), and when specific moments occur (moment-level grounding). For object-level grounding, we present QDETRv, a query-guided extension of the Detection Transformer for one-shot localization of unseen objects in videos, which combines query-context fusion, a memory module for temporal consistency, and unsupervised pretraining on synthetic object trajectories. We further propose the Temporal Object-Aware Vision Transformer (TOViT) for few-shot video object detection, which builds on a language-aligned open-set vision encoder and propagates high-confidence object features across frames. For relationship-level grounding, we introduce few-shot referring relationships in videos: given a (subject, predicate, object) query and a few support videos sharing the predicate, the entities are localized by optimizing a T-partite random field using a meta-learned relation network with query-conditioned translational embeddings, Global Semantic Aggregation, Local Localization Aggregation, and belief-propagation-based message passing. For moment-level grounding, we propose the Moment Alignment Transformer (MATR) for video-to-video moment retrieval, which uses dual-stage Dynamic Time Warping for pre- and post-fusion alignment, learnable queries for boundary prediction, and self-supervised pretraining with temporal augmentations; MATR also extends to text-to-video moment retrieval with performance comparable to, or better than, the state of the art. These contributions advance query-guided video understanding across spatial, relational, and temporal grounding.

Abstract
Instruction-tuning datasets often contain substantial redundancy and low-quality samples, necessitating effective data selection methods. We propose an instruction data selection framework based on weighted in-context influence (wICI), which measures how effectively each candidate example reduces instruction-following difficulty for semantically related peers. Through systematic experiments, we address three key questions: what constitutes effective instruction tuning data from an in-context perspective, whether sample difficulty correlates with in-context influence, and how in-context influence translates to instruction tuning effectiveness. Experiments across multiple models and benchmarks demonstrate that our method consistently outperforms existing baselines under constrained data budgets, while empirically showing that sample difficulty negatively correlates with in-context influence.

Clean Data Can Still Carry Backdoors: Support-Persistent Backdoors for Model Reuse
Authors: Junhoo Lee, Baekseung Kim, Seungyeon Kim, Nojun Kwak
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-10-15T04:04:19+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T04:04:19+00:00
Comments: Accepted to NeurIPS 2026
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Traditional backdoor attacks inject artificial trigger patterns into training data, causing models to misclassify triggered inputs while maintaining normal behavior on clean samples. Since these artificial triggers are absent in clean datasets, standard knowledge distillation (KD) typically eliminates them, leading to the common belief that KD serves as an effective purification defense. In this paper, we propose DAR (Discover-and-Relabel), a support-persistent backdoor method that retains its behavior even after clean-data distillation. DAR identifies naturally abundant patterns in low-dimensional feature subspaces using subspace clustering and applies label-only poisoning to samples that inherently satisfy the discovered rule. At test time, the backdoor is activated by locally modifying the corresponding subspace of the target images. By instantiating spatial and frequency operators, DAR achieves attack accuracy comparable to state-of-the-art backdoor attacks in ImageNet classification and CLIP-based prompt tuning. Notably, DAR keeps attack success above 96% after clean KD and remains defense-resistant in practice. Project page: https://junhoo.me/backdoor

Poisoning the corpora of protein design systems during pre-training and retrieval
Authors: Charalampos Koilakos, Kimon-Antonios Provatas, Michail Patsakis, Candace SY Chan, Georgios A. Pavlopoulos, Ilias Georgakopoulos-Soares
First: 2026-10-15T04:00:17+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T04:00:17+00:00
Comments: double page format, 6 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Protein foundation models increasingly support the generation of novel biological sequences, yet their reliance on large public datasets creates opportunities for adversarial manipulation of the material they learn from and retrieve. We investigate two data-only poisoning attacks against protein design systems, targeting the pre-training corpus of autoregressive protein language models and the retrieval corpus of a text-guided protein generator. For the first, we pre-trained ProtGPT2 and ProGen2 variants of 300 million parameters, introducing poisoned protein sequences containing one of two triggers followed by one of three payloads. Across all poisoned runs, both architectures acquired highly reliable trigger-dependent generation under uniform and ramped poisoning schedules. Under ramped exposure, the earliest majority activation occurred after only 200 contaminated examples, while for the uniform schedule earliest activation occurred around 5000 poisoned samples. For the second attack, we inserted records into a retrieval corpus system without modifying its weights or decoding procedure. A single query-aware record captured a top-16 retrieval position for 58\% of templated requests and 97\% of free-text requests. Conditional on successful poisoned retrieval, 62\% of requests produced altered sequences, while 19-21% acquired the inserted domain by profile-HMM homology. A universal query-unaware attack using 1,500 records also achieved substantial retrieval capture and domain transfer. Together, these findings demonstrate that adversarial protein sequence contributions can establish persistent, sequence-specific behaviour during pre-training or redirect protein generation at inference, while leaving ordinary model behaviour largely preserved. Our work establishes the integrity and biological provenance of protein sequence data as critical safeguards for generative protein design systems.

Resisting Harmful Instructions through Training-Free Weight Editing
Authors: Ali Ebrahimpour-Boroojeny, Xiaocong Yang, Samuel Schapiro, Zahra Borhani, Francisco R. Ortega, Yujia Zheng, ChengXiang Zhai
First: 2026-10-15T03:47:15+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T03:47:15+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Instruction-following language models can comply with requests that conflict with intended behaviors and safety alignment. We introduce a training-free weight-editing approach for reducing compliance with targeted undesirable instructions. Our method needs only forward passes on a small set of prompt pairs per target behavior, and because the edit is stored in the model's weights, changing or deleting instructions in the prompt cannot undo the weight modification. We evaluate the approach on two target harmful behaviors across five instruction-tuned models: instructed falsehood and instructed sexism. On two instructed-lying benchmarks, it improves mean honest response rates by 6.2 and 4.6 percentage points over the strongest alternative, significantly on three of the five models and within noise on the other two. In a controlled study of seven prompt formats, it maintains higher honest-response rates than single-layer activation steering when orders to lie arrive in single-turn formats not used to construct the edit. For instructed sexism, it reduces the mean sexist-response rate from 57.9% to 24.3%; combining the edit with a fairness prompt reduces it further to 11.9%. These results establish our contrast-based weight editing approach as an effective training-free intervention in two distinct behavioral domains, supporting its use both independently and alongside prompt-based safeguards.

Learning When to Withdraw: Curriculum Coverage and Rule-Specific Failures in Fine-Tuned Language Agents
Authors: Mohamed Abouzahra
First: 2026-10-15T00:38:39+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T00:38:39+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Tool-using language agents must revise prepared actions when evidence changes or authority is revoked. Selective withdrawal means suspending affected actions, preserving supported work, and resuming only after sufficient repair. We study supervised fine-tuning in NAQD-Env, a synthetic environment with explicit dependencies and a deterministic reference policy. Four LoRA checkpoints of Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct compare an original curriculum with an enriched one, the latter under three system-prompt framings with identical views and targets. Three instruction-tuned baselines and all four checkpoints are evaluated under three inference prompts on fresh validation cases, held-out dependency families, and held-out structural combinations. Under the naqd prompt, SFT checkpoints reach decision agreement of 0.77-0.92 and withdrawal recall of 0.46-0.86, versus 0.45-0.63 and at most 0.06 for the baselines. Rule-level diagnostics qualify these gains. The original-curriculum checkpoint wrongly withdraws 26 of 34 actions with surviving alternative support; enriched checkpoints withdraw 0-1. Conversely, all SFT checkpoints narrow prohibited combinations less often than the 7B and 8B baselines, a case absent from both curricula. Delegation results show more withdrawal but poorer preservation of independently authorized actions, and all four checkpoints omit valid event reports. Framing contrasts vary across metrics and splits and do not establish a reproducible advantage or equivalence. One training run per arm, jointly changed curriculum properties, prior evaluation exposure, and baseline-SFT quantization differences limit causal attribution. The contribution is a method for detecting rule-specific regressions that aggregate accuracy can conceal, not evidence that fine-tuning alone ensures agent safety.

Formal Bayesian Transfer Learning via the Total Risk Prior
Authors: Nathan Wycoff, Ali Arab, Lisa O. Singh
First: 2025-07-31T17:55:16+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T20:26:16+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Existing methods for transfer learning struggle to deal with situations where the source datasets are limited and not guaranteed to be well-aligned with the target dataset. A typical strategy is to use the empirical loss minimizer on the source data as a prior mean for the target parameters. We propose using a risk minimizer conditional on source parameters instead. This allows us to construct a joint posterior for all parameters from the source and target datasets. As a consequence, we benefit from full Bayesian uncertainty quantification and can perform model averaging via Gibbs sampling over indicator variables governing the inclusion of each source dataset. We show how a particular instantiation of our prior leads to a Bayesian Lasso in a transformed coordinate system and describe connections between the Maximum a Posteriori estimate associated with our approach and the Trans-Lasso method. We find that the MAP estimator MSE-dominates the Trans-Lasso in the normal means setting when there is no regularization on the source datasets. Finally, we perform numerical experiments finding that our approach provides superior predictive performance relative to frequentist and Bayesian baselines on a genetics application, particularly when the source data are limited.

Existing methods for transfer learning struggle to deal with situations where the source datasets are limited and not guaranteed to be well-aligned with the target dataset.

Abstract
We present Open Telco (OTel), an open telecom AI resource that releases derived telecom datasets for retrieval, reranking, instruction tuning, and safety/abstention, together with 30 full-parameter post-trained baselines spanning 10 embedding models, 3 rerankers, and 17 language models. The community has already engaged substantially with the resource: as of May 3, 2026, the released models have been downloaded over 16 million times and the project has received 157+ pieces of media coverage worldwide. We release OTel as a reproducible starting point and invite the community to expand the data, improve embedding and reranking models, and build stronger context-grounded telecom LLMs.

Abstract

Abstract
Reliable uncertainty estimation is essential for deploying large language models in high-stakes reasoning tasks, yet most existing approaches rely on expensive sampling strategies or shallow token-level signals that ignore the internal logical structure of model outputs. We introduce Argument Graph Uncertainty, a post-hoc framework for multiple choice question tasks, that estimates model confidence directly from the logical structure of a single chain-of-thought reasoning trace. Our method uses either a lightweight instruction-tuned model or a simple deterministic rule to segment the reasoning chain into propositional units, then applies a NLI model to infer relational edges and construct two complementary argument graphs over these segments. Crucially, our framework requires no additional calls to the evaluated model and relies entirely on small, efficient auxiliary models, producing fully interpretable, per-node uncertainty attributions at low computational cost.

Abstract

Abstract

Abstract

Abstract
We propose and test the LLM Brain Rot Hypothesis: continual exposure to junk web text induces lasting cognitive decline in large language models (LLMs).
