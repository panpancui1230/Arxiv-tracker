SCDM: Scalable Causal Discovery in Nonlinear Temporal Systems with Meta-Learning
Authors: Jingbo Wang, Kegeng Tang, Zihao Wang, Shaogang Ren
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-10-21T16:31:07+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T16:31:07+00:00
Comments: Accepted at NeurIPS 2026. 44 pages, 6 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Causal discovery from nonlinear multivariate time series is challenging in high-dimensional systems, where the number of candidate directed relations grows quadratically with the number of variables. Existing methods are often limited by target-wise model fitting, repeated conditional testing, or expensive graph-search procedures. We propose SCDM, a shared-parameter meta-learning framework for scalable temporal causal discovery. SCDM treats each target variable as a task while learning a shared temporal predictor and a shared causal-strength matrix across tasks. Each meta-episode updates only a subset of target-variable tasks, allowing the model to accumulate structural evidence across the full system without exhaustive target-wise optimization. After training, a post-training graph readout converts the learned predictor into continuous directed causal scores for threshold-independent evaluation. We also provide a finite-error recovery principle showing that, under fully observed delayed-system assumptions and a positive risk-gap condition, thresholding SCDM scores recovers the graph when statistical, meta-optimization, and readout errors are below the population margin. Experiments on controlled synthetic, neuroimaging-inspired, high-dimensional, and real-world temporal benchmarks demonstrate competitive causal recovery and improved scalability in large temporal systems.

Abstract
This paper describes our submission to the WMT 2026 Multilingual Instruction Shared Task. We adapt Qwen3-8B with LoRA using organiser-provided data, EuroAlpaca, a multilingual instruction dataset created through task-preserving localisation, and CrossEuroAlpaca, a cross-lingual augmentation that assigns different source and target languages to instructions, contexts and responses. Our system targets open-ended question answering (QA-OEG) in ten shared-task languages represented in the auxiliary data. However, these gains are task- and language-specific. Gemma-4 scores improve for QA-OEG and summarisation in the target languages but decline for context-grounded QA there and across all three tasks in the non-target languages.

Unintended Side Effects of Value Training in LLMs
Authors: Siddhesh Pawar, Chen Cecilia Liu, Anna Korhonen, Isabelle Augenstein
First: 2026-10-21T15:58:53+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T15:58:53+00:00

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
As LLMs are increasingly deployed in value-sensitive contexts, aligning them with human values has emerged as a post-training objective in NLP, aimed at instilling additional values and guiding model behaviour. However, current evaluations remain narrow. Because values are interconnected, it is unclear how fine-tuning for specific values alters unrelated capabilities and behaviours. In this work, we adapt four instruction-tuned LLMs across individual Hofstede's value dimensions and evaluate them on both cross-value transfer and broader behaviours. We find that value-specific training produces unintended side effects on untrained values, risk preferences, social cognition, and safety traits. Consequently, we suggest that value alignment after post-training should incorporate broader behavioral evaluations, and we highlight the need to investigate where additional value training might best fit into the pipeline.

Neither Stochastic Parroting nor AGI: LLMs Solve Tasks through Context-Directed Extrapolation from Training Data Priors
Authors: Harish Tayyar Madabushi, Melissa Torgbi, Claire Bonial
First: 2025-05-29T10:31:42+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T14:38:37+00:00
Comments: 14 pages, 1 figure (including appendix)
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
This position paper argues that many behaviours framed as either "stochastic parroting" or "advanced reasoning" are better explained as context-directed extrapolation from training-data priors, and that ML and NLP research communities would benefit from evaluations that distinguish remembering and basic extrapolation from genuine application-level generalisation. Our central contribution is an actionable evaluation checklist and design patterns that make these distinctions testable in practice. Our framework posits that LLMs extrapolate from training data priors while using context to guide the model to the appropriate priors; we call this "context-directed extrapolation." Specifically, this context direction is achieved through examples in base models, leading to in-context learning, while instruction tuning allows analogous task specification via prompts rather than explicit examples. We argue that adopting this more realistic framework and controls for improving evaluation would better calibrate claims about LLM "reasoning," refocus research on how LLM performance interacts with training data and generalisability to out-of-domain tasks, and reduce miscalibration in deployment and policy decisions.

Where Do LLMs Learn to Recommend AI?
Authors: Benaya Trabelsi, Jonathan Shaki, Sarit Kraus
First: 2026-10-21T14:13:52+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T14:13:52+00:00
Comments: 18 pages, 4 figures. Companion paper to arXiv:2601.13749
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Asked what to study, which career to pursue, or what product to build, large language models (LLMs) often recommend AI-related options. We ask where this tendency comes from by comparing models before and after fine-tuning and by varying how questions are asked. First, pretrained models already include explicit AI/ML terms in the recommendations of 27% of their responses, and their released instruction-tuned versions do so in 59%. Second, fine-tuning models ourselves shows that this rise is not inevitable: general instruction tuning leaves the rate unchanged, but removing the 7% of instruction examples that concern AI cuts it by 14 percentage points, below the level of the pretrained models it starts from. Data rewarding helpfulness raise it by 9 points, while data ranked by the preferences of diverse simulated users lower it by 9. Finally, with models held fixed, asking for the "best" option or for what to do "right now" raises the rate by 8 and 18 points, whereas telling the model it is an AI advisor adds 4. Overall, the tendency starts in pretraining, and what models learn afterward, together with how a question is phrased, decides how strongly it appears.

The Stepwise Informativeness Assumption: When Do Entropy Dynamics Track Reasoning Correctness in LLMs?
Authors: Mar Gonzàlez I Català, Haitz Sáez de Ocáriz Borde, George D. Montañez, Pietro Liò
First: 2026-03-11T18:00:18+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T13:26:55+00:00
Comments: 24 pages, 20 figures, 5 tables
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Recent work uses entropy-based signals at multiple representation levels to study reasoning in large language models, but the field remains largely empirical. From a theoretical perspective, reductions in model uncertainty do not necessarily reflect progress toward correctness. In this work, we characterize when such alignment can be expected through the Stepwise Informativeness Assumption (SIA), which requires entropy-reducing reasoning-prefix extensions increase the probability assigned to valid answers in expectation. We show that next-token prediction can transfer answer-relevant information from training data to the model, but does not, by itself, guarantee alignment with correctness. Instead, we propose that this alignment depends on how the training regime couples reasoning traces to correct answers. Furthermore, we hypothesize observable signatures of this phenomenon and characterize its expected failure regimes. We empirically investigate how entropy dynamics relate to correctness across four reasoning benchmarks (GSM8K, ARC, SVAMP, MATH) and diverse open-weight language models spanning base, instruction-tuned, and RL-trained regimes. Our results show that entropy-correctness alignment strengthens with post-training but can weaken when task difficulty exceeds model capabilities.

Abstract
Indoor localization systems are critical for Industry-4.0 applications. Visible Light Communication (VLC)-based systems offer advantages such as immunity to electromagnetic interference, high accuracy, and energy efficiency, but their performance is hindered by environmental variability across deployment areas. This paper presents a transfer learning (TL) framework for VLC-based indoor localization that enhances cross-area robustness while reducing data requirements. Using real-world measurements from four distinct areas of a Bosch manufacturing facility, we show that fine-tuning a deep neural network (DNN) pre-trained on source area data with only 40% of target-area measurements recovers 97% of full-data performance, thereby reducing the amount of target data needed. Overall, the proposed framework provides a scalable and data-efficient pathway for industrial VLC localization, substantially reducing deployment costs and computational burden while maintaining high accuracy in diverse and dynamic environments.

Abstract
Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) enhances generalization by reducing a Max-Sharpness (MaxS). Despite the practical success, we empirically found that the MAxS behind SAM's generalization enhancements face the "Flatness Indicator Problem" (FIP), where SAM only considers the flatness in the direction of gradient ascent, resulting in a next minimization region that is not sufficiently flat. A better Flatness Indicator (FI) would bring a better generalization of neural networks. Because SAM is a greedy search method in nature. In this paper, we propose to utilize the difference between the training loss and the minimum loss over the neighborhood surrounding the current weight, which we denote as Min-Sharpness (MinS). Specially, we combine this FI with SAM into the proposed Bilateral SAM (BSAM) which finds a more flatter minimum than that of SAM. The theoretical analysis proves that BSAM converges to local minima. Extensive experiments demonstrate that BSAM offers superior generalization performance and robustness compared to vanilla SAM across various tasks, i.e., classification, transfer learning, human pose estimation, and network quantization.

What Transfers from Text to Vision? Capability Scaling Laws and Transfer Dynamics for VLMs
Authors: Ziran Li, Qiang Wang, Zhengyu Chen, Shanglin Lei, Borun Chen, Jingang Wang, Xunliang Cai
First: 2026-06-24T13:23:33+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T09:33:38+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Choosing the right large language model (LLM) backbone is the most consequential decision when building a vision-language model (VLM), yet it remains fundamentally unprincipled: compute-based scaling laws fail to generalize across model families, and no framework exists for directly predicting VLM performance before training begins. We propose the Capability-Driven Multimodal Scaling Law, the first cross-family framework that predicts VLM benchmark accuracy from directly observable textual capability. Given a low-dimensional capability score $S$ extracted from LLM textual benchmarks via PCA, we model VLM performance as a function of $S$, with a per-backbone transfer rate and an absorption rate that quantifies data-scaling efficiency. To fit and validate the framework, we train over 150 VLMs on 34 LLMs spanning 7 model families under a strictly controlled recipe. Evaluations on more than 200 textual and 50 multimodal benchmarks show that the law accurately extrapolates transfer rate from models up to 8B parameters to 72B-scale backbones, predicts full VLM training trajectories with high fidelity, and generalizes to entirely held-out model families. Beyond the scaling law, our analysis surfaces actionable insights: certain textual benchmarks negatively correlate with multimodal performance, exposing latent benchmark-gaming behavior; base LLMs outperform instruction-tuned counterparts as VLM backbones due to higher absorption rates and lower data-scaling decay; and different model families occupy distinct positions in the transfer--absorption space. The framework turns backbone selection from costly empirical sweeps into a principled, quantitative decision. Code and data are available at https://github.com/wangq-dev/CDMScaling.

Approximate Solomonoff Induction: On the Robustness of Learned Universal Predictors
Authors: Qian Li, Shang-Hua Teng
First: 2026-10-21T06:44:09+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T06:44:09+00:00
Comments: 24 pages
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Why large language models trained via log-loss minimization over massive and diverse datasets exhibit broad capabilities and generalizable skills is not yet fully understood. Grau-Moya et al. (ICML 2024) offer a conceptual explanation through the lens of meta-learning: if sequence-generating environments $ν$ are drawn from a prior $w_ν$, minimizing population log-loss recovers the corresponding Bayesian mixture predictor $ξ=\sum_νw_νν$. In the idealized limit in which $w$ is a universal prior, the optimal predictor corresponds to Solomonoff universal induction. Practical machine learning algorithms, however, generally produce only \emph{approximately} optimal predictors. This raises a natural question: \emph{Does the prediction performance remain robust under approximation}? In this paper, we first provide a tight bound on the worst-case effect of approximation on prediction error. We show any predictor $ρ$ satisfying $D_{KL}(ξ\|ρ)\leq η$ retains bounded cumulative-expected-squared prediction error. However, while the Bayesian mixture predictor $ξ$ has error $O(\log(1/w_μ))$ in an environment $μ$, we establish a tight $Θ(η/w_μ)$ bound on the excess error of $ρ$ relative to $ξ$. Thus, approximation can substantially degrade performance in environments assigned small prior probability. In sharp contrast, when environments are averaged according to prior $w$, we prove that the excess prediction error is only $O(η)$. Furthermore, we establish a distribution-shift theorem for dominated shifts from a training prior $w$ to a test prior $w'$, interpolating our average-case and worst-case guarantees. Finally, we extend our robustness analysis to a finite-sampling setting relevant to learning universal predictors with neural networks and Transformers, where $D_{KL}(ξ\|ρ)$ could be unbounded. Metropolis-Scale Resilient and Trustworthy Traffic Flow Inference Using Multi-Source Data
Authors: Qishen Zhou, Yifan Zhang, Michail A. Makridis, Anastasios Kouvelas, Yibing Wang, Simon Hu
Venue: Transportation Research Part C: Emerging Technologies, Volume 194, Part B, January 2027, 106083

Comments: The paper has been accepted by Transportation Research Part C: Emerging Technologies

Abstract
Inferring network-wide traffic states from sparse observations with high accuracy and trustworthy uncertainty quantification is essential for intelligent transportation systems, yet it remains challenging due to the underdetermined nature of the problem, multifaceted disturbances in sensing networks, and the inherent conflicts among multiple inference subtasks when modeled jointly. We propose the Task-Aware Attentive Neural Process (TA-ANP), a unified probabilistic framework for resilient and trustworthy global traffic state inference (GTSI) by fusing floating car data (FCD) with sparse fixed-detector measurements. By casting GTSI as a stochastic process, TA-ANP leverages the meta-learning properties of neural processes to adapt rapidly to changes in sensing configurations without retraining. A task-aware multi-query attention module with distinct spatiotemporal inductive biases is introduced to handle three GTSI subtasks jointly ---real-time estimation at unobserved locations, forecasting at both observed and unobserved locations---while mitigating cross-task interference. For uncertainty quantification, we combine neural processes with Monte Carlo Dropout to capture both aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty. To support metropolis-scale evaluation, we construct the Metropolitan Multi-Source Traffic Dataset (MMTD), integrating fixed-loop sensor measurements, FCD statistics, and OpenStreetMap road-network data over an urban network of 26,166 road segments, including 2,371 fixed-sensor-equipped segments used for quantitative evaluation. Experiments across multiple independently generated sensor configurations show that TA-ANP achieves best performance across all subtasks under deterministic and probabilistic metrics. The resulting well-calibrated uncertainties enable more efficient fixed-sensor placement with fewer sensor deployments.

Abstract
Personalized shopping benchmarks ask which item fits the user; we ask how far an agent may commit on that item, and pose learning the individual's auto-purchase line as a task. The boundary is evidence-conditioned and three-valued: whether committing is appropriate depends on the sufficiency of product information (warrant), reversibility, price against the user's norm, and urgency, not only on fit; and the natural output is don't buy, confirm first, or buy automatically. Across sixteen instruction-tuned models, autonomous commitment is misaligned with a human authorization reference in which fifteen annotators, role-playing the delegating principal from a given profile, choose the furthest action they would allow without confirmation: models commit even when fit is low and when their own evidence flag reports the warrant missing. This is overreach. The line is individual: these raters disagree, yet the disagreement is structured and partly predictable from a person's own other labels. A shallow learner on a few dozen of a person's own labels predicts their held-out auto-purchase line at a median of 0.79 by 30-40 labels, outperforming every evaluated prompt$\times$model configuration, including a per-annotator oracle over 80 configurations; the learned boundary does not transfer across domains. We release the counterfactual benchmark, human authorization reference, and annotation protocol after acceptance.

Abstract
Full fine-tuning has achieved remarkable success on reasoning-intensive tasks, but it often requires substantial GPU resource consumption. Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) provides a promising alternative for reducing this cost, yet we observe a substantial performance gap on reasoning-intensive tasks. In this paper, we present three key observations showing that this gap is closely associated with norm-related under-adaptation, where PEFT produces markedly smaller weight-update norms than full fine-tuning. GRAM: Group-wise Rank-Aware Model Merging via Subspace Alignment
Authors: Di Wu, Shuaidong Pan
First: 2026-10-21T02:50:46+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T02:50:46+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Merging different models into a single model is highly desirable for multi-task deployment, particularly in regulated domains like healthcare. In such settings, models are often locally fine-tuned due to strict data privacy policies, necessitating that aggregation operates exclusively on the fine-tuned weights. Existing methods apply a uniform merge across all tasks, ignoring the geometric relationships among their learned subspaces. When task updates occupy misaligned subspaces, projecting them onto a shared low-rank basis discards task-relevant directions, a failure mode named \emph{subspace interference}. Through this study, we show that the per-task projection error of a merged model is governed by the principal-angle alignment between each task and the shared subspace, so that grouping geometrically compatible tasks provably reduces this error within each group. Motivated by this observation, we propose \textbf{Group-wise Rank-Aware Model Merging (GRAM)}, which uses the principal-angle geometry of task vectors to decide which tasks should be merged together and then performs a standard SVD-based merge within each coalition; the pipeline requires only the fine-tuned weights at merge time. With only one additional model ($\nK{=}2$), GRAM significantly mitigates subspace interference and consistently outperforms prior merging methods across language understanding (GLUE with Flan-T5), 8-task CLIP vision, and medical instruction tuning (Qwen2-7B-Instruct with four English/Chinese medical LoRAs).
Summary / 总结
Merging different models into a single model is highly desirable for multi-task deployment, particularly in regulated domains like healthcare.

CSD: Common-Support Distillation for Cross-Tokenizer Knowledge Transfer
Authors: Mingyu Yang, Sharath Turuvekere Sreenivas, Adithyakrishna Venkatesh Hanasoge, Nima Tajbakhsh, Ashwath Aithal, Pavlo Molchanov
First: 2026-10-20T20:18:46+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T20:18:46+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Cross-tokenizer logits distillation enables knowledge transfer across model families, allowing students to learn from stronger or more specialized teachers. However, differences in vocabulary and segmentation make teacher and student next-token distributions difficult to align. Existing logits-based methods are easy to integrate into standard distillation pipelines, but often rely on restrictive heuristics or tokenizer-pair-specific choices. They may restrict supervision to shared vocabulary subsets, require manually selecting different objectives for different tokenizer pairs, or match only the observed chunk and its complement rather than individual alternative continuations. We introduce Common-Support Distillation (CSD), a unified logits-based framework that constructs a shared support between the teacher and student at each aligned input chunk and matches the induced distributions with a single objective. For one-to-one alignments, CSD uses explicit common-token pairs as the shared support. For other alignments, it constructs a virtual support whose probabilities are computed using the autoregressive chain rule. A residual event captures probability mass outside the explicit support, ensuring that both induced distributions remain normalized. Across four teacher-student settings comprising Llama-3.2-1B and Gemma3-1B-PT students with Qwen and Phi teachers, CSD consistently achieves the highest average performance among the evaluated cross-tokenizer methods, outperforming the strongest alternative by up to 1.8 points and surpassing both continued pretraining and same-tokenizer distillation. For Llama-3.1-8B, CSD improves average accuracy over the strongest evaluated baseline by up to 4.2 points for the base student and 1.6 points for the instruction-tuned student.

Combating Catastrophic Forgetting in Continual Domain Adaptation via Knowledge Recasting
Authors: Zijie Liu, Zhen Tan, Charles Fleming, Tianlong Chen
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-10-20T20:16:40+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T20:16:40+00:00
Comments: NeurIPS 2026 Main Conference
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Adapting language models to new domains during post-training risks degrading previously acquired capabilities, a phenomenon commonly known as catastrophic forgetting. Existing mitigation methods largely treat forgetting as a consequence of excessive model drift. They therefore constrain updates by preserving old outputs, replaying old data, or regularizing changes to parameters and representations so that the updated model remains close to its previous state. We take a different view: Rather than pulling the updated model back toward its old state, we translate the new domain into the model's pre-existing knowledge space, making unfamiliar knowledge learnable through familiar procedures. Therefore, we propose Skill Schema Transport (SST), a post-training framework that transports new knowledge into the model's existing skill space. SST abstracts each example type into a skill schema, preserving its essential operations, dependencies, and verification steps while removing domain-specific surface forms. By recasting new examples as instances of reusable procedures, SST turns knowledge update from trajectory memorization into procedural reuse. In continual domain adaptation settings, SST consistently reduces forgetting while improving generalization to related tasks. Across two 3B instruction-tuned backbones, SST improves average final performance on streamed domains by 2.53 to 5.75 points over sequential post-training. It also lowers max-drop forgetting in all evaluated settings, with the largest reduction from 42.41 to 7.10 points on the Qwen-3B MATH stream, while substantially recovering the OOD degradation induced by sequential updates.
Summary / 总结
Adapting language models to new domains during post-training risks degrading previously acquired capabilities, a phenomenon commonly known as catastrophic forgetting.

Abstract
Long-horizon robotic operation requires memory that scales with essential information rather than execution time.
