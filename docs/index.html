Beyond Discrete Tokens: Preserving Numerical Continuity in Medical Vision-Language Models
Authors: Dianye Huang, Jiamei Guo, Yue Zhou, Mingde Li, Yi Xiong, Wei Wang, Nassir Navab, Zhongliang Jiang
First: 2026-10-23T17:40:58+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-23T17:40:58+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Objective: To develop a unified vision-language model for prompt-conditioned quantitative ultrasound measurement that addresses the mismatch between continuous measurement targets and discrete token-based numerical prediction. Methods: This article presents HAD-VLM, a hybrid autoregressive-diffusion framework that combines autoregressive text generation with continuous measurement prediction within a shared backbone. Visual prompt tuning adapts the vision encoder to ultrasound images. A diffusion process predicts a five-component representation comprising caliper endpoint coordinates and measurement length. A post-processing strategy combining the proposed coordinate-length consistency mechanism with ensemble prediction is introduced to refine measurement estimates. We evaluate four echocardiographic measurement tasks on EchoNet-LVH, with additional assessments of medical question answering and X-ray vertebral measurement. Results: HAD-VLM achieves mean absolute errors of 1.36, 2.77, 3.05, and 1.16 mm for IVSd, LVIDd, LVIDs, and LVPWd, respectively, reducing errors by 21.4-37.6\% relative to the Vicuna-based autoregressive baseline. It also outperforms fine-tuned Qwen3-VL and direct MLP regression across all four tasks. Medical question answering performance is preserved on the evaluated dataset. For X-ray vertebral measurement, HAD-VLM reduces mean absolute error from 11.4 to 6.7 pixels. Conclusion: Integrating autoregressive language generation with diffusion-based continuous prediction improves quantitative medical image measurement while retaining prompt-conditioned task flexibility. Significance: HAD-VLM provides a unified approach to combining language interaction with anatomically grounded numerical prediction for quantitative medical image analysis.

Interpretable sparse fine-tuning with TopKLoRA enables faithful circuit discovery
Authors: Marek Masiak, Andrzej Szablewski, Nicola Cancedda, Constantin Venhoff
First: 2026-10-23T16:37:29+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-23T16:37:29+00:00
Comments: 29 pages, 10 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Fine-tuning on datasets of unknown origin can create a backdoor in the trained model when the data includes poisoned examples. Difficult to localise and remove, backdoors can persist through safety training. While parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods capture the full parameter changes introduced during training, they are not easily interpretable. We present the full method of TopKLoRA, extending the preliminary proposal of Masiak et al. (2025), an interpretable-by-design LoRA variant in which only the $k$ largest adapter latents are active at each token. Unlike SAEs, TopKLoRA is trained purely on downstream loss, where optimisation pressure incentivises learning functionally causal latents. We demonstrate this sparse LoRA adapter in a sleeper-agent backdoor case study where we create model organisms by fine-tuning gemma-2-2b, Qwen2.5-1.5B, and Qwen2.5-7B on a poisoned instruction-following dataset. For each organism, we identify and ablate a circuit that is both necessary and sufficient to implement the backdoor. Furthermore, our new fine-tuning method produces sparse circuits that are up to an order of magnitude smaller than a dense LoRA baseline, allowing surgical ablation of the malicious capability without sacrificing instruction-following performance. Finally, we propose a technique to construct model organisms with localised circuits using TopKLoRA latents and gradient-routing, which we use to validate the findings of our circuit search algorithm. We consider these findings as a step toward interpretable-by-design architectures that provide faithful units of analysis without compromising model performance. We share our code: https://github.com/interpretable-finetuning/TopKLoRA.

Singular Geometry of Low-Rank Adaptation
Authors: Kamillo Ferry, Anthea Monod, Keisuke Yano
First: 2026-10-23T16:27:30+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-23T16:27:30+00:00
Comments: 40 pages, 8 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Low-rank adaptation (LoRA) is now a standard approach to parameter-efficient fine-tuning of large neural networks. Its low-rank factorization is inherently non-identifiable, making LoRA a singular statistical model whose learning complexity cannot be described by parameter dimension alone. We study this complexity using the real log canonical threshold (RLCT) from singular learning theory and how it depends on both the low-rank parameterization and the pretrained network. Under a local linearization, LoRA induces a low-rank trace regression model whose Kullback--Leibler geometry is controlled by a forward-backward covariance, constructed from activations and backpropagated sensitivities at the adapted layer. This covariance determines which low-rank perturbations are statistically observable. When it is nondegenerate along the relevant low-rank directions, we recover the classical learning complexity of reduced-rank regression; when its kernel meets these directions, additional statistically indistinguishable parameters change the singular geometry. We characterize this geometry locally through observable tangent directions and a residual singularity, and globally through affine linear sections of determinantal varieties. The global learning complexity need not be determined at a ground-truth parameter, as singularities on another rank stratum can dominate. We also introduce a curvature-corrected multi-temperature estimator of the RLCT. Experiments on multilayer perceptron and transformer architectures reveal strongly layer-dependent forward-backward geometry and show that ambient covariance degeneracy does not imply degeneracy along the low-rank directions relevant to adaptation.

Abstract
The interest in leveraging physics-based inductive bias in deep learning has resulted in recent development of hybrid deep generative models (hybrid-DGMs) that integrate known physics-based mathematical expressions in neural generative models. To identify these hybrid-DGMs requires inferring parameters of the physics-based component along with their neural component. The identifiability of these hybrid-DGMs, however, has not yet been theoretically probed or established. How does the existing theory of the un-identifiability of general DGMs apply to hybrid-DGMs? What may be an effective approach to construct a hybrid-DGM with theoretically-proven identifiability? This paper provides the first theoretical probe into the identifiability of hybrid-DGMs, and presents meta-learning as a novel solution to construct identifiable hybrid-DGMs. On synthetic and real-data benchmarks, we provide strong empirical evidence for the un-identifiability of existing hybrid-DGMs using unconditional priors, and strong identifiability results of the presented meta-formulations of hybrid-DGMs. Code is available at https://github.com/GoForIt-YYB/Meta-Hybrid-VAE.

Enhancing Biomedical Named Entity Recognition via Multiple Programming Languages Instruction Tuning and Ensemble Method

First: 2026-10-02T07:42:44+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-23T11:23:09+00:00

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Instruction tuning has become a common paradigm for applying large language models (LLMs) to biomedical named entity recognition (BioNER). However, existing instruction-tuning approaches still face two key challenges. First, conventional natural-language instructions typically serialize BioNER annotations as flat textual outputs, providing limited structural constraints for typed entity extraction. Second, high-quality biomedical annotations are limited, and learning from a single serialized output form may restrict structural diversity and reduce model robustness. Although external biomedical knowledge can be introduced to alleviate data scarcity, it often requires costly resource construction. To address these challenges, we propose MITE, a Multiple Programming Languages Instruction Tuning and Ensemble method for BioNER. MITE reformulates BioNER as a structure-to-structure generation task by representing both instructions and entity outputs in code-formatted representations. Specifically, each training instance is transformed into multiple programming-language formats, including Python, C++, and Java, while preserving the same underlying entity semantics. These language-specific representations provide structurally diverse supervision without requiring external biomedical knowledge or additional annotations. During inference, MITE aggregates predictions from different code formats through an entity-level voting strategy, reducing language-specific prediction variance and improving robustness. Experiments on three widely used BioNER datasets demonstrate that MITE achieves competitive performance against representative BERT-based and LLM-based baselines, attaining the best results on two benchmarks. Ablation studies further validate the effectiveness of multi-language representations.

MoLS: SNR-Guided Module-Wise Learning-Rate Calibration for Large Language Models
Authors: Ziqing Wen, Zhouyang Liu, Jiahuan Wang, Ping Luo, Junlin Zeng, Li Shen, Dongsheng Li, Tao Sun
First: 2026-05-07T07:32:27+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-23T07:50:02+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Large language models (LLMs) contain functionally distinct modules with heterogeneous gradient statistics. Although Adam(W) adapts updates at the parameter level, selecting relative learning rates across modules often relies on costly manual tuning. We study this problem through gradient signal-to-noise ratio (SNR). Our analysis identifies structural sources of module-wise SNR differences and relates SNR to the attenuation of Adam's normalized mean-gradient component. Based on these findings, we propose \textbf{Module-wise Learning Rate Scaling via SNR (\name)}, a lightweight method that replaces manual multiplier search with one-shot calibration. MoLS estimates module-level SNRs after an initial adaptation period and uses inverse-square-root scaling to determine relative learning-rate multipliers. The multipliers are introduced smoothly during warmup and then held fixed while the global learning-rate schedule continues. Our theoretical results quantify the effect of calibration and its sensitivity to estimation error and relative SNR drift. Extensive experiments across LLM pre-training and fine-tuning show that MoLS reduces perplexity and improves downstream accuracy with minimal computational overhead. It achieves performance comparable to carefully tuned module-specific learning rates and integrates with memory-efficient optimizers and parameter-efficient fine-tuning.

Evidence-Grounded SLM Behavioral Prediction Architecture for Future Assistance Use
Authors: Adeyemi Adeseye, Aisvarya Adeseye, Hannu Tenhunen, Jouni Isoaho
First: 2026-10-23T05:39:31+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-23T05:39:31+00:00
Comments: Accepted for publication at the 2027 International Conference on Artificial Intelligence and Applied Intelligent Systems (AIAIS 2027), to be held in Hong Kong from 22 to 24 January 2027
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Users often exhibit changes in interaction behavior before explicitly seeking assistance, enabling systems to anticipate future assistance use. This is especially important in high-stakes environments, where delayed assistance can have operational, financial, or safety consequences. However, conventional Large Language Models (LLMs) are often unsuitable for continuous on-device behavioral prediction because of their computational and memory demands. Behavioral patterns are also highly individual and device-specific, motivating local processing that preserves privacy and data sovereignty. Small Language Models (SLMs) offer a promising alternative, but their ability to reason reliably over low-level sequential interaction events remains underexplored. This study introduces Evidence-Grounded Local Behavioral Prediction (ELBP), a four-layer architecture combining deterministic interaction processing, dual-view behavioral representation, SLM-based prediction, and evidence verification with abstention. Three instruction-tuned models of 3B parameters or fewer were evaluated using raw, structured, dual-view, and complete ELBP configurations. The best-performing model, Qwen2.5-3B with ELBP, achieved a full-set F1-score of 0.80 and PR-AUC of 0.84, representing a 15.9\% relative improvement over raw-event prompting and 17.6\% over XGBoost, while full-set ECE decreased by 43.2\% relative to raw-event prompting. With evidence verification and abstention, selective F1 increased to 0.84 at 91\% coverage and evidence support reached 96\%. These results show that structured behavioral representation and evidence-verified selective prediction can improve the reliability of behavioral forecasting in SLMs.

Abstract
In this work, we propose a transfer learning algorithm for covariate shift, integrating sample reweighting with representation learning. A key feature of our approach is the use of a lower-dimensional latent space for modeling, in contrast to conventional methods that operate directly in the high-dimensional ambient space. MetaKE: Meta-Learning for Knowledge Editing Toward a Better Accuracy-Editability Trade-off
Authors: Shuxin Liu, Di Gao, Ou Wu
First: 2026-03-13T05:47:00+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-23T01:32:57+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Existing locate-then-edit Knowledge Editing (KE) methods typically decompose editing into two stages: upstream target representation optimization and downstream constrained parameter optimization. The optimization across the two stages is disconnected: upstream applies uniform regularization without observing downstream realization of the planned residual, hindering a refined accuracy-editability trade-off. Since this realization is request-specific and depends on downstream constraints, uniform regularization can over-shrink high-association requests, causing insufficient editing, while it can under-regularize low-association requests, producing over-large planned residuals that reduce downstream editability. To bridge this disconnect, we propose MetaKE (Meta-learning for Knowledge Editing), a new framework that unifies upstream and downstream stages into a bi-level optimization problem. The inner level optimizes parameter updates for the target representation, while the outer level optimizes representation using feedback from downstream constraints, achieving a better semantic accuracy-editability trade-off. To avoid costly multi-layer backpropagation, we introduce a Structural Gradient Proxy to approximate and propagate this feedback. Extensive experiments show that MetaKE outperforms strong baselines, offering a new perspective on KE.

Rethinking Representation Geometry in Supervised Contrastive Learning under Long-Tailed Data
Authors: Joonhyeok Jeong, Jy-yong Sohn, Kibok Lee
First: 2026-10-23T01:30:14+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-23T01:30:14+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Supervised contrastive learning and its balanced variants have demonstrated their effectiveness for representation learning under long-tailed distributions. However, although prior analyses suggest that their optimal representations may exhibit a regular simplex geometry, whether such a geometry is indeed exhibited remains insufficiently understood. In this work, we present a theoretical framework for these objectives that characterizes whether a regular simplex is formed at the optimum. By applying this to representative objectives, we show that these objectives fail to form a regular simplex at the optimum because they remain dependent on the number of samples per class. Motivated by this analysis, we introduce structural balancing that can be applied to contrastive objectives, and propose a fully balanced contrastive objective that provably forms a regular simplex geometry at its optimum. Both theoretical and empirical results demonstrate that the proposed method yields a more balanced representation space and improves performance on long-tailed recognition and transfer learning tasks.

Distributed Sparse Interventions in Language Models
Authors: Maximilian S. Ernst, Lorenz Linhardt, Aaron Peikert, Oliver Eberle
First: 2026-07-08T08:19:11+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T22:16:45+00:00
Comments: 29 pages, 12 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Language models can perform a wide range of tasks at various levels of abstraction. They flexibly infer tasks from context, execute multiple tasks simultaneously, and select among competing goals. Understanding and controlling how models represent these tasks is central to aligning them with human objectives. Researchers can change model behaviour in many ways, but model steering, which manipulates intermediate task representations, links representation to behaviour most directly. Current steering methods typically intervene densely and assume linear effects, e.g., shifting every activation along a global direction in the residual stream. Because such interventions change thousands of neurons at once, they leave researchers to separate relevant from irrelevant neurons. We introduce Distributed Sparse Interventions (DSI), which steers models through a sparse set of individual neurons distributed across layers and attention heads. Finding such a set requires accounting for the selected neurons' interactions, even across layers; DSI achieves that by iteratively refining which neurons to intervene on and how strongly. Across 12 tasks and three instruction-tuned models, DSI activates task behaviour by identifying and intervening on fewer than 0.1% of neurons, outperforming dense steering vectors and often matching 10-shot in-context learning. Because DSI identifies explicit sets of neurons, it connects neuron-level computation to changes in latent representations and, ultimately, to desired outputs, allowing us to study how distributed computational components interact to produce steered behaviour. Our results demonstrate that steering can be viewed not only as a linear shift in representation space, but also as a targeted intervention on model computation, realised by adjusting a small set of interacting neurons whose coordinated activity spans multiple layers and contributes to behaviour.

Language models can perform a wide range of tasks at various levels of abstraction.

Abstract
Critical heat flux (CHF) marks the onset of boiling crisis in light-water reactors, defining safe thermal-hydraulic operating limits. To support Phase II of the OECD/NEA AI/ML CHF benchmark, which introduces spatially varying power profiles, this work compiles and digitizes a broad CHF dataset covering both uniform and non-uniform axial heating conditions. Heating profiles were extracted from technical reports, interpolated onto a consistent axial mesh, validated via energy-balance checks, and encoded in machine-readable formats for benchmark compatibility. Classical CHF correlations exhibit substantial errors under uniform heating and degrade markedly when applied to non-uniform profiles, while modern tabular methods offer improved but still imperfect predictions. A neural network trained solely on uniform data performs well in that regime but fails to generalize to spatially varying scenarios, underscoring the need for models that explicitly incorporate axial power distributions. By providing these curated datasets and baseline modeling results, this study lays the groundwork for advanced transfer-learning strategies, rigorous uncertainty quantification, and design-optimization efforts in the next phase of the CHF benchmark.

Abstract
Code-switched (CS) automatic speech recognition (ASR) remains difficult because real CS speech is scarce and synthetic speech does not fully match it. SQuAT: Spherical Lattice Vector Quantization-Aware Training
Authors: Sebastian Loeschcke, Ivan Koryakovskiy, Tycho F. A. van der Ouderaa, Riccardo Del Chiaro, Mart van Baalen, Paul N. Whatmough, Markus Nagel
First: 2026-10-22T15:14:44+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T15:14:44+00:00

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Vector quantization (VQ), in particular lattice-based methods, offer strong rate-distortion trade-offs for low-bit large language models (LLMs), yet their behavior under quantization-aware training (VQ-QAT) remains poorly understood. We scale lattice VQ-QAT to billion-parameter LLMs and multi-billion-token training regimes, and introduce SQuAT, a shape-gain VQ-QAT approach trained with a separate straight-through estimator (STE) for the shape and gain. SQuAT decomposes weights into direction and magnitude: normalized directions are quantized using an $E_8$-derived spherical codebook, while gains are quantized independently with low-bit scalar quantizers. We show that, unlike a standard STE applied to the full quantizer, which ignores the shape-gain structure, these separate STEs induce tangent-space updates for directions, yielding an implicit form of spherical (Riemannian) optimization. To enable scalability, we develop a distributed exact nearest-neighbor assignment scheme, in which workers quantize disjoint weight blocks and communicate only discrete indices, reducing VQ-QAT training time by 12.8$\times$ and allowing us to scale it to 10B tokens. Across instruction-tuned LLaMA-3.2-1B/3B, Qwen3-4B, and base Qwen3-1.7B, SQuAT consistently outperforms standard VQ-QAT STE under identical quantizers and initialization, and achieves SOTA VQ-QAT accuracy.

Abstract
The growth of time-series datasets and foundation models has made dataset comparison a central part of time-series workflows. Assessing source-target transfer before model adaptation requires similarity estimates that can summarize transfer-related dataset relationships without exhaustive source-target evaluation over every candidate pair. Many metrics have been proposed for measuring similarity between time-series datasets, but they capture different dataset relationships and vary in how well they align with downstream utility across tasks. We evaluate this gap by measuring the correlation between source-target similarity scores and downstream forecasting errors in out-of-distribution and transfer-learning settings. We propose Domain-Integrated Similarity Cost (DISC), a learnable aggregation framework that uses calibration transfer observations to select, weight, and combine similarity metrics for different downstream tasks. By integrating complementary metric values, DISC better distinguishes source-target dataset differences that matter for downstream behavior. After calibration, DISC scores source-target pairs using only their similarity-metric values and yields higher alignment than single-metric references on the reported split. These results show that time-series dataset similarity is task-dependent, and that learned metric integration provides a stronger proxy for downstream transfer behavior than manually selecting one similarity metric.

Beyond Prompt and Parser Engineering: Soft-Prompt Tuning for Automated LLM Evaluation
Authors: Selen Erkan, Bastian Boll, Kristian Kersting, Björn Deiseroth, Letitia Parcalabescu

Abstract
Benchmark scores often misrepresent a large language model's (LLM's) knowledge, because they rely, e.g., on the model's ability to follow specific formatting requirements. This especially penalizes base models that may know the correct answers but lack the ability -- typically introduced in post-training -- to structure them as instructed. To overcome this, we propose an efficient, automated, and architecture-agnostic method for model evaluation based on soft-prompt tuning. By optimizing only 10 soft-prompt vectors (~0.0006% parameters for a 7B model) over a short tuning period, we adapt models to specific benchmark formats, closing gaps in format-following and ensuring that underlying knowledge is accurately reflected in benchmark scores. We evaluate soft-prompt tuning across 7 models and 11 datasets. Our results show that (a) soft-prompt tuning saturates format-following within 150 steps (~1200 samples) while preserving knowledge accuracy, (b) it closely matches strong evaluation-harness baselines on well-studied benchmarks while requiring no manual benchmark-specific prompt or parser design, (c) it improves evaluation signal in settings where baselines are harder to transfer, including long-context datasets and instruction-tuned variants with different output styles, and (d) it produces cleaner outputs that improve agreement between exact match scoring and independent LLM judges. Overall, we propose a lightweight, automated, and flexible protocol based on limited-budget soft-prompt tuning for evaluating LLM knowledge.

Poisoning-Based Backdoors Are Not Cryptographic
Authors: Masahiro Kaneko
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-10-20T11:27:30+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T12:19:07+00:00
Comments: NeurIPS 2026
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Defending against a backdoor requires recovering the verifier that decides whether an input is a trigger, since blocking individual triggers fails against an attacker who can generate new ones. If a verifier could be installed with the asymmetry of cryptographic authentication, where only the attacker can produce triggers and the defender cannot reconstruct the verifier, defense that learns the verifier from observations would be impossible in principle. We show that no such asymmetry can arise when the verifier enters training only through the labels of poisoned data, which covers the dirty-label backdoors that dominate the literature on language models. A training procedure that installs a verifier from poisoned data is itself a learner for that verifier. Hence any verifier that is hard to learn cannot be installed through poisoning, and any verifier that poisoning can install is reconstructible by a white-box defender holding as many labeled trigger observations as the attacker used. Hiding a secret key in the input or the weights does not change this. Experiments on instruction-tuned large language models confirm the dichotomy, with no configuration yielding a backdoor that is both effective for the attacker and irrecoverable for the defender.
Summary / 总结
Defending against a backdoor requires recovering the verifier that decides whether an input is a trigger, since blocking individual triggers fails against an attacker who can generate new ones.

Abstract

Abstract
