An Embarrassingly Simple Data Selection Strategy for Million-Scale Multi-Modal Instruction-Tuning
Authors: Mengyao Lyu, Yan Li, Huasong Zhong, Hui Chen, Jungong Han, Guiguang Ding, Zhenheng Yang
Venue: EMNLP 2026 Oral
First: 2025-03-17T17:11:22+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T14:31:21+00:00
Comments: Accepted to EMNLP 2026 Main Conference (Oral)
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Recent research demonstrates that pretrained large language models (LLMs) require minimal but carefully curated data for instruction-tuning. Building on LLMs, multi-modal models further contend with far larger data volumes, heterogeneous sources, and cross-modal alignment, amplifying both the necessity and the complexity of data selection. However, existing methods rely on deeply extracted embeddings, gradients or narrow quality scores that are time-consuming to compute and limited in expressiveness, frequently failing to surpass a simple random-sampling baseline. To overcome these limitations, we step back from complex embedding spaces and target the fundamental objective of instruction tuning: how and what knowledge should be elicited from the pretrained model and exposed to users. We leverage interaction \underline{S}tyles as a proxy for structural diversity, and decompose data quality into capability-specific \underline{S}cores, yielding an explicit yet \underline{R}ich (SSR) profile for each sample. We further cast the selection as submodular coverage maximization over the capability--style grid, providing an approximation guarantee. SSR consistently delivers superior performance across two distinct architecture stacks, LLaVA-1.5 (Vicuna + CLIP) and LLaVA-OneVision (Qwen2 + SigLIP), two large-scale data pools, six data budget settings, and 14 multi-modal benchmarks. To our knowledge, SSR is the first data selection method validated at million-scale, achieving 99.1% of full-data performance using only 30% of a 2.6M pool. Furthermore, the SSR profile models and selected subsets transfer training-free across data pools and model architectures with capability extensibility.

SimplexLoRA: Dynamic Rank Updating via Alternating Minimization with Simplex Projection
Authors: Igor Shalygin, Grigorii Davydenko, Andrey Veprikov, Aleksey Antonov, Nikolay Bushkov, Stanislav Moiseev, Aleksandr Beznosikov
Venue: EMNLP 2026
First: 2026-10-27T13:24:14+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T13:24:14+00:00
Comments: Accepted to Findings of EMNLP 2026
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
The success of large language models in real-world applications relies on parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods such as Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA), which inserts compact, trainable adapters into selected layers. In this paper, we propose SimplexLoRA, a LoRA variant that adaptively adjusts adapter ranks during fine-tuning based on their learned importance. Unlike standard LoRA, which employs a fixed rank across all layers, SimplexLoRA optimizes per-adapter importance weights on the $n$-scaled simplex during a short warm-up and then freezes them, dynamically allocating capacity where it is most beneficial without increasing the overall parameter budget or inference cost. We evaluate SimplexLoRA across 12 language understanding, summarization, and reasoning tasks using DeBERTaV3, BART-large, Llama 3.1 8B, and Qwen3-8B. Our results show that SimplexLoRA consistently matches or outperforms standard LoRA and recent adaptive baselines, with the largest gains at low ranks. Our code is available at https://github.com/brain-lab-research/SimplexLoRA.

PubMedCausal: A Span-Level Annotated Corpus for Causal Relation Extraction in Biomedical Text
Authors: Ifeoluwa Kunle-John, Josiah Paul, Oluwatosin Agbaakin, Peter Aina, Ikenna Odezuligbo, Sydney Anuyah
Venue: EMNLP 2026
First: 2026-05-27T11:59:17+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T12:00:25+00:00
Comments: Published at EMNLP 2026. 32 pages
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Causal relation extraction (CRE) is central to biomedical text mining, but current resources often conflate causal relations with broader associations, restrict annotation to sentence-level examples, or focus mainly on explicit causal cues. This limits their usefulness for evaluating whether models can recover causal claims as they are actually expressed in biomedical text. We introduce PubMedCausal, a span-level annotated corpus for biomedical CRE built from PubMed abstracts. The corpus contains 30,000 paragraph-level rows, including 3,945 causal rows and 6,491 adjudicated cause--effect pairs. Each causal relation is annotated with full-text cause and effect spans, causality type, and sententiality, enabling evaluation of both causal detection and full-span causal extraction. We benchmark discriminative encoders and open-source generative models across detection and extraction settings. For causal detection, biomedical encoders are strongest, with PubMedBERT reaching an F$_1$ score of 0.7391. For span-level extraction, the best generative baseline is DeepSeek-R1-32B with few-shot prompting, reaching a Cosine Pair F$_1$ of 0.6765. We further test transfer learning by evaluating PubMedCausal-trained encoders on external causal relation datasets, showing that the resource supports cross-dataset evaluation. Our results show that biomedical CRE remains difficult under class imbalance, long causal spans, implicit causality, inter-sentential relations, and prompt sensitivity. Code and Data can be found here: https://github.com/josiahpaul07/PubMedCausal_Exp

On the Sparsity of Direct Preference Optimization: Weight Disentanglement in the NTK Regime
Authors: Kensuke Sasaki, Issei Sato
First: 2026-10-27T11:48:41+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T11:48:41+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) has been widely adopted for aligning language models with human preferences. Recent empirical work has revealed that DPO induces remarkably sparse parameter updates, yet no theoretical explanation for this phenomenon has been offered. We analyze DPO's optimization dynamics under a neural tangent kernel linearization and show that the logistic structure of the DPO loss gives rise to an implicit maximum-margin bias. Building on this observation, we introduce the concept of dual sparsity: the DPO update direction is a sparse linear combination (data-level sparsity, driven by support vectors) of sparse gradient feature vectors (parameter-level sparsity, arising from gradient cancellation between preferred and dispreferred responses). We formalize this structure in our main theorem, bound the number of support pairs by a margin-dependent spectral quantity, and present a framework relating dual sparsity to weight disentanglement---a condition favorable for model merging. Experiments on GPT-2 Small and Medium and on 7--9B instruction-tuned models (Llama-3-8B, Mistral-7B, Gemma-2-9B) with the Anthropic HH-RLHF and UltraFeedback datasets confirm both sources of sparsity, and experiments on GPT-2 show that DPO reduces cross-task interference by up to $35{\times}$ compared to supervised fine-tuning.

Hierarchical Low-Rank Reparameterization for Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning
Authors: Rong Huang, Yi Xiong, Yuan-Yuan Cheng, Xiao-Ming Fu
First: 2026-10-27T11:34:03+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T11:34:03+00:00
Comments: 14 pages, 2 figures, 10 tables, including references and appendices
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods adapt large models by introducing a small number of trainable parameters. However, we find that these parameters still contain redundancy and can be further compressed while maintaining strong task performance. To this end, we propose a novel hierarchical low-rank reparameterization method. Its core idea is to reorganize the trainable parameters of PEFT methods into an approximately square matrix and then express it via a low-rank decomposition, which can be applied recursively. This forms a multi-level structure with factors compressed level by level, drastically reducing the number of trainable parameters. In our experiments, we combine this reparameterization with LoRA, DoRA, MoSA, and RaSA. We evaluate commonsense reasoning on LLaMA-3.1-8B and Qwen2.5-7B, with additional GSM8K math reasoning experiments on LLaMA-3.1-8B. Under comparable trainable-parameter budgets, our method combined with LoRA or DoRA achieves better performance. Furthermore,when the parameter count of the original methods is compressed by approximately an order of magnitude, our method can still maintain competitive task performance and even surpass the corresponding baselines in average accuracy across the eight commonsense reasoning benchmarks.

CaLLM: Continual Adaptation of LLMs via Non-Parametric Adapter Routing
Authors: Bilge Celik, Anna Vettoruzzo, Joaquin Vanschoren
First: 2026-10-27T10:41:56+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T10:41:56+00:00
Comments: Accepted at 5th Conference on Lifelong Learning Agents (CoLLAs), Main Conference Track, 2026
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Continual adaptation of large language models (LLMs) to evolving task distributions while mitigating catastrophic forgetting remains an open problem. Existing strategies often struggle to scale or lack a robust mechanism for zero-shot task-to-expert assignment. We introduce CaLLM, a framework that resolves these tensions by combining metric-based meta-learning with modular parameter isolation via lightweight adapters. First, CaLLM projects each input batch into a task-agnostic embedding space to find its nearest prototype. It then routes the batch to the specific adapter specialized for that data distribution. This formulation provides (i)~controllable cross-task interference by design, (ii)~constant computational complexity regardless of task count, (iii)~seamless integration of novel distributions through prototype expansion, and (iv)~regime agnosticism as the local routing decision enables a unified approach to both online and offline settings. Our experiments demonstrate that this routing mechanism discovers semantically coherent task groupings while facilitating constructive interference between similar tasks, and keeping overhead at a minimum. Across diverse and fine-grained continual learning benchmarks, CaLLM consistently outperforms traditional isolation and replay methods, establishing prototype-based modular routing as a principled and scalable approach to lifelong adaptation of LLMs.
Summary / 总结
Continual adaptation of large language models (LLMs) to evolving task distributions while mitigating catastrophic forgetting remains an open problem.
Manifold meta-learning for reduced-complexity neural system identification
Authors: Marco Forgione, Ankush Chakrabarty, Dario Piga, Matteo Rufolo, Alberto Bemporad
First: 2025-04-16T06:49:56+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T08:51:11+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
System identification has greatly benefited from deep learning techniques, particularly for modeling complex, nonlinear dynamical systems with partially unknown physics where traditional approaches may not be feasible. However, deep learning models often require large datasets and significant computational resources for both training and inference due to their high-dimensional parameterizations. To address this challenge, we propose a meta-learning framework that discovers a low-dimensional manifold within the parameter space of an over-parameterized neural network architecture. This manifold is learned from an abundant meta-dataset of input-output sequences generated by a simulator of the class of systems of interest, and thereafter enables efficient model training while preserving the network's expressive power for the considered system class. Unlike bilevel meta-learning approaches, our method employs an auxiliary neural network to map datasets directly onto the learned manifold, eliminating the need for costly second-order gradient computations during meta-training and generally reducing the number of first-order updates required in inference, which could be expensive for large models. Finally, we derive a probabilistic bound that quantifies the performance gap between training on the learned low-dimensional manifold and the full parameterization. We validate our approach on a family of Bouc-Wen oscillators, which is a well-studied nonlinear system identification benchmark. We demonstrate that we are able to learn accurate models even in small-data scenarios.

Robust LLM Data Selection via Content-Invariant Gradient Attribution
Authors: Jia Gu, Liang Pang, Huawei Shen
First: 2026-10-27T07:12:01+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T07:12:01+00:00
Comments: 16 pages, 8 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Gradient-based data attribution has become an important tool for high-quality data selection in the instruction fine-tuning of large language models (LLMs), as it estimates the contribution of training examples through their influence on model optimization. However, we find that existing global gradient attribution methods suffer from surface-form bias: minor changes to semantically irrelevant details, such as wording or formatting, can produce highly inconsistent attribution scores even when the underlying content remains unchanged. To address this issue, we propose Content-Invariant Gradient Attribution (CIGA), a robust framework for instruction data selection. CIGA first identifies layers whose gradients are both influential for attribution and stable under semantics-preserving surface-form perturbations, thereby reducing formatting noise in gradient features. It then dynamically fuses these attribution signals with semantic representations of the input, combining optimization-level influence with content-level information. Extensive experiments across multiple benchmarks show that CIGA produces more stable rankings under surface-form perturbations while preserving downstream capabilities and improving average performance. CIGA also reduces attribution cost, offering a robust and efficient approach to data selection for LLM instruction tuning.

AnomalyGFM: Graph Foundation Model for Zero/Few-shot Anomaly Detection
Authors: Hezhe Qiao, Chaoxi Niu, Ling Chen, Guansong Pang
First: 2025-02-13T12:10:05+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T05:50:56+00:00
Comments: Accepted by KDD2025
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Graph anomaly detection (GAD) aims to identify abnormal nodes that differ from the majority of the nodes in a graph, which has been attracting significant attention in recent years. Existing generalist graph models have achieved remarkable success in different graph tasks but struggle to generalize to the GAD task. This limitation arises from their difficulty in learning generalized knowledge for capturing the inherently infrequent, irregular and heterogeneous abnormality patterns in graphs from different domains. To address this challenge, we propose AnomalyGFM, a GAD-oriented graph foundation model that supports zero-shot inference and few-shot prompt tuning for GAD in diverse graph datasets. One key insight is that graph-agnostic representations for normal and abnormal classes are required to support effective zero/few-shot GAD across different graphs. Motivated by this, AnomalyGFM is pre-trained to align data-independent, learnable normal and abnormal class prototypes with node representation residuals (i.e., representation deviation of a node from its neighbors). The residual features essentially project the node information into a unified feature space where we can effectively measure the abnormality of nodes from different graphs in a consistent way. This provides a driving force for the learning of graph-agnostic, discriminative prototypes for the normal and abnormal classes, which can be used to enable zero-shot GAD on new graphs, including very large-scale graphs. If there are few-shot labeled normal nodes available in the new graphs, AnomalyGFM can further support prompt tuning to leverage these nodes for better adaptation. Comprehensive experiments on 11 widely-used GAD datasets with real anomalies, demonstrate that AnomalyGFM significantly outperforms state-of-the-art competing methods under both zero- and few-shot GAD settings.

Abstract
Planning effective interventions in biological systems requires treatment-effect models that adapt to unseen biological contexts by identifying their specific underlying mechanisms. Yet single-cell perturbation datasets span only a handful of biological contexts, and existing methods cannot leverage new interventional evidence at inference time to adapt beyond their training data. To meta-learn a perturbation effect estimator, we present MapPFN, a prior-data fitted network (PFN) pre-trained on a synthetic biological prior with causal interventions, decoupling pre-training from limited wet-lab data. Our results show that combining synthetic pre-training with fine-tuning on real single-cell data improves perturbation predictions across biological contexts. LLM Abstention Can Be a Prompt Artifact, in Addition to Genuine Uncertainty
Authors: Zipeng Ling, Shuliang Liu, Yuehao Tang, Junqi Yang, Shenghong Fu, Seonil Son, Chen Huang, Kejia Huang, Yao Wan, Zhichao Hou, Xuming Hu
First: 2025-07-22T03:21:48+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-26T21:52:20+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly trained to abstain from answering questions they are unsure about. However, this ability is often misapplied: in real-world applications, user prompts sometimes contain elements of uncertainty, which lead LLMs to abstain even on problems they are capable of solving. We argue that LLM abstention is not only an expression of genuine uncertainty; it can also be an artifact largely shaped by prompts. We name this phenomenon *Abstention Inflation*. We add "Unknown" as an extra option for LLMs to choose from; experiments show serious accuracy drops on True/False Questions (TFQs). Replacing "Unknown" with an unrelated random word produces a similar effect. We argue that LLMs are trained to imitate the surface pattern of abstention, rather than to express genuine uncertainty. Based on ten experimental settings, we support four claims that form a progressive argument: **(C1)** *Abstention Inflation* can be triggered by the presence of an extra option, not by genuine uncertainty; **(C2)** it makes the models deny they can answer, even when they can; **(C3)** it is a later-layer output override, as the reasoning traces and mid-layer representations preserve correct answers; **(C4)** it is not stochastic noise: it results from various factors, emerges through instruction tuning, is boosted by problems' higher difficulty, and can be mitigated at larger model sizes.
Summary / 总结
Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly trained to abstain from answering questions they are unsure about.

Abstract

Structure, Not Sequence: Tracking and Validating Folding Mechanisms in ESMFold's Trunk with Sparse Autoencoders
Authors: Bohan Liu, Xiaohan Fan, Wenqian Ye, Aidong Zhang

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Structure predictors like ESMFold fold proteins using neural network computation and have transformed structural biology. However, the driving mechanism from amino-acid (AA) sequence to 3D structure is insufficiently understood, and biological meaning remains locked in the dense network. Sparse autoencoders (SAEs) have shown promising results in obtaining interpretable concept features over an overcomplete basis of intermediate protein sequence-model representations. Yet folding models, which predict physical shape from AA sequence semantics, have no such account. We hypothesize that the features carrying the folding computation evolve in the 3D structural regression process. In this paper, we interpret the folding trunk in three steps. First, a pilot analysis on the representation distribution across modules and recycles locates distinct spatial computation in the trunk. Second, the dictionaries expose a division of labor: the single representation carries inherited semantics, and the pair representation computes the pairwise geometry. Third, we interpret the dictionary at scale with a reasoning language model, trace how the concepts compose across blocks by gradient-based attribution, and follow the interpreted code to the model's folding output, verifying where the computation aligns with, or departs from, known biochemistry. Together, the sparse dictionaries we train capture the relational geometry the trunk computes beyond the sequence model, and their concept activations forecast the model's own fold quality, turning the black-box folding trunk into a sparse, interpretable, and scientifically verifiable circuit.

3, 7, 14, 17, 42: Instruction Tuning Installs and Sharpens Number-Choice Heuristics in Language Models
Authors: Samar Ansari

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We measure, without sampling error, the probability a language model assigns to each integer from 1 to N when asked for a random number. For a fixed prompt the model defines one probability vector, which we compute by summing over each integer's scored spellings rather than estimate by sampling, removing the noise that limits earlier studies [1, 4]. We evaluate 18 open-weight model lines at five ranges up to N = 100, under 29 decoding configurations, on two inference stacks; 15 of them have matched base and instruction-tuned checkpoints that both give usable answers. Instruction tuning makes the distribution less uniform in all 15 pairs. It installs one heuristic, round-number avoidance, and sharpens another, endpoint avoidance. Base checkpoints do not avoid round numbers once the endpoints are removed from those sets; tuned checkpoints avoid them, multiples of 10 almost completely. Base checkpoints return the endpoints 1 and N at 0.30 to 0.60 of the interior rate; tuned checkpoints almost never return them. A preference for odd numbers is consistent across tuned checkpoints only at N = 100, where base checkpoints also show it weakly. Endpoint avoidance and the odd preference fit the account Kubovy and Psotka [6] proposed for human number choice; round-number avoidance is not part of that account, and the midpoint avoidance it predicts is essentially absent, with one slight exception at N = 100. Tuning does not install a new favourite integer shared across models: above N = 10 no modal integer is shared by more than 42 percent of tuned conditions, and P (17) rises in 10 of 15 pairs and falls in 5. The concentration is present in the model's own distribution: at temperature 1.0, nucleus truncation at top-p 0.95 adds a median of 0.035 to the total variation distance.

AzeroS: Extending LLM to Speech with Self-Generated Instruction-Free Tuning
Authors: Yiwen Shao, Wei Liu, Jiahong Li, Tianzi Wang, Kun Wei, Meng Yu, Dong Yu

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Extending large language models (LLMs) to the speech domain has recently gained significant attention. A typical approach connects a pretrained LLM with an audio encoder through a projection module and trains the resulting model on large-scale, task-specific instruction-tuning datasets. However, curating such instruction-tuning data for specific requirements is time-consuming, and models trained in this manner often generalize poorly to unseen tasks. In this work, we first formulate that the strongest generalization of a speech-LLM is achieved when it is trained with Self-Generated Instruction-Free Tuning (SIFT), in which supervision signals are generated by a frozen LLM using textual representations of speech as input. Our proposed SIFT paradigm eliminates the need for collecting task-specific question-answer pairs and yields the theoretically best generalization to unseen tasks. Building upon this paradigm, we introduce AZeroS (Auden Zero-instruction-tuned Speech-LLM), which is trained on speech-text pairs derived from publicly available corpora, including approximately 25,000 hours of speech with ASR transcripts and 3,000 hours of speech with paralinguistic labels. Built upon Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, the model updates only two lightweight projection modules (23.8 million parameters each), while keeping both the LLM and audio encoders frozen. Despite the minimal training cost and modest data scale, AZeroS achieves state-of-the-art performance on both semantic and paralinguistic benchmarks, including VoiceBench, AIR-Bench Foundation (Speech), and AIR-Bench Chat (Speech).

Linear Probes Transfer from Base to Distilled Language Models despite Rotation
Authors: Marios Tsatsos
First: 2026-10-26T15:15:34+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-26T15:15:34+00:00
Comments: 16 pages, 10 figures, 5 tables
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Linear probes, classifiers that read a language model's internal activations, are widely used to detect harmful requests. They are cheap to train and run, and an auditor who has trained one would like to reuse it when the model is replaced by a successor rather than retrain it for every release. We test how well probes can be reused. For four base models (Qwen2.5-Math-7B, Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-14B, Qwen2.5-32B) we train a probe on the base model with the XSTest prompt set and apply it, unchanged, to two successors of that base: the DeepSeek-R1 distilled reasoning model and the developer's instruction-tuned chat model. We find that the successors rotate the direction separating unsafe from safe prompts by 17$^\circ$ to 35$^\circ$, yet on XSTest the transferred probe's AUC changes by only $-0.011$ to $+0.027$, and on an independent OR-Bench evaluation, never used for training, by $-0.015$ to $+0.053$. On XSTest, whose safe prompts deliberately contain alarming words, the probes beat a classifier that sees only the prompt text by 0.15 to 0.20 AUC. On the OR-Bench prompts that margin shrinks to 0.03 to 0.20, and the probes flag 29\% to 42\% of prompts that sound harmful but are benign, so part of what they read is the appearance of harm rather than harm itself. A probe trained on a base model can thus be reused, in these families, after distillation and after instruction tuning; how much it reads beyond textual cues depends on the test set.

Abstract
Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) has emerged as a prevalent strategy for adapting large pre-trained models. Among PEFT techniques, reparameterization-based methods, particularly those that employ low-rank or sparsity structures, have attracted significant attention. However, these approaches often underperform full fine-tuning. Specifically, low-rank methods constrain the update rank, which is misaligned with the inherently high-rank nature of full fine-tuning. In contrast, sparsity-based techniques limit the flexibility of the parameter space and compromise connectivity across weights. To overcome these limitations, we explore the use of Toeplitz matrices, whose entries are constant along each diagonal, therefore providing a compact parameterization without imposing explicit rank or sparsity constraints. A straightforward approach is to update weights via a product of Toeplitz matrices. However, similar to RNNs, long multiplicative chains often lead to gradient instability. We theoretically show that products of block-wise Toeplitz matrices can approximate arbitrary matrices under shorter chain length, justifying the structural design of ToPA. Furthermore, we demonstrate that ToPA offers greater expressivity than existing low-rank and sparse parameterizations. Empirical evaluations across 21 NLP and CV datasets, spanning 5 model architectures, consistently validate the effectiveness of ToPA.

Abstract
Contrastive vision-language models such as CLIP typically rely on independently parameterized transformer blocks across depth. We investigate the extent to which these blocks can instead be reused recurrently while preserving the transferable capabilities of CLIP. We study recursive transformer backbones in which a small set of $N$ transformer blocks is applied $M$ times per forward pass. By holding $N \cdot M$ fixed to the depth of a standard ViT-B, our models preserve computational depth while progressively increasing parameter sharing. We consider two recursive CLIP model families: rCLIP, where only the vision encoder is recursive, and r$^2$CLIP, where recurrence is also introduced in the text encoder. Our results show that CLIP can tolerate substantial recurrent block reuse, although its effect depends on the encoder and downstream task. Recurrence also provides parameter efficiency by construction, since fewer distinct transformer blocks need to be stored and adapted. Our results establish recurrent block reuse as a practical design space for vision-language models and highlight its implications for knowledge transfer, downstream adaptation, and parameter efficiency.

Abstract
Foundation models provide powerful representations for unstructured data, such as text and images, and parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) makes their adaptation to downstream tasks increasingly practical. For neural estimators that lack a tractable sampling distribution, resampling-based procedures offer a flexible route to statistical inference, but they often require hundreds of model fits, making them computationally prohibitive at foundation-model scale. We introduce HIFI, a two-stage fine-tuning framework for statistical inference on parameters such as conditional means. An application to gene-expression inference illustrates the practical value of the framework for uncertainty-aware scientific inference.

Abstract
Large language models often answer structurally unanswerable questions, such as computing cot(-540°) or evaluating (1).startswith("1"), instead of abstaining. We ask whether this failure reflects missing recognition or failed routing from recognition to abstention. Across instruction-tuned models from 1.7B to 70B parameters, a single linear direction in the hidden state separates answerable from structurally impossible math and code prompts, showing that models represent impossibility before generation. Yet this recognition direction is nearly orthogonal to the canonical safety-refusal direction that mediates trained harmful-content refusal. Base/instruct comparisons further show that the low-cosine geometry is already present at the pretraining endpoint.
