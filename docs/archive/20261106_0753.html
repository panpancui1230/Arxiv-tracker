Leveraging Soft Prompts for Privacy Attacks in Federated Prompt Tuning
Authors: Quan Minh Nguyen, Min-Seon Kim, Hoang M. Ngo, Trong Nghia Hoang, Hyuk-Yoon Kwon, My T. Thai
First: 2026-01-10T17:50:05+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-04T16:36:55+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Membership inference attacks (MIAs) pose a serious privacy threat in federated learning (FL). While MIAs have been extensively studied in standard FL, the recent shift toward federated fine-tuning introduces new and largely unexplored attack surfaces. In this work, we show that federated prompt-tuning, which adapts pre-trained foundation models using lightweight input prefixes, exposes a novel and effective vector for membership inference. We propose PromptMIA, a membership inference attack tailored to federated prompt-tuning, in which a malicious server introduces adversarially crafted prompts and exploits their updates during collaborative training to determine whether a target data point belongs to a client's private dataset. We formalize this threat via a security game and demonstrate that PromptMIA achieves consistently high attack advantage across diverse benchmark datasets, substantially outperforming current SOTA federated MIAs. We also provide a theoretical lower bound on the attack advantage that explains the observed empirical behavior. Finally, we show that existing MIA defenses are often ineffective against PromptMIA, highlighting the need for defense mechanisms specifically tailored to prompt-tuning in federated settings.

Dexterous Robotic Hands in the Foundation-Model Era: A Full-Stack Survey of Hardware, Data, Cross-Embodiment Transfer, Learning, and Evaluation
Authors: Ji Pei
First: 2026-11-04T16:13:29+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-04T16:13:29+00:00
Comments: 45 pages, 8 figures, 21 tables. Structured narrative review of hardware, data, cross-embodiment transfer, foundation models, tactile sensing, and evaluation for dexterous robotic hands
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Dexterous robotic hands are increasingly coupled to large-scale human data and foundation models, yet progress in hardware, representation learning and evaluation is often assessed separately. This survey reviews work from January 2024 to early November 2026, with earlier studies included to establish technical lineages. We organize the literature into five interdependent layers: hardware; data collection and curation; cross-embodiment transfer; learning and foundation models; and evaluation and applications. Human hand-object understanding and tactile sensing provide two cross-cutting perspectives. A system-level architecture, comparative tables and five case analyses connect design choices across these layers. We distinguish within-study experimental comparisons from heterogeneous corpus statistics, vendor specifications and company-reported results. Three conclusions emerge. First, the value of human data depends on kinematic, visual and contact alignment, not recording volume alone. Second, shared action representations and foundation policies do not remove embodiment-specific constraints on force, latency and contact. Third, task completion is insufficient to establish physical correctness or deployment readiness. Evidence remains limited for zero-shot transfer of contact-rich skills between morphologically different hands, long-duration reliability, and cross-sensor tactile generalization. We identify eleven open problems and propose reporting interfaces and controlled comparisons that can make full-stack progress more measurable. The review is a structured narrative synthesis rather than a pooled performance ranking or an exhaustive systematic review.

Revisiting data-driven dynamic security assessment with a tabular foundation model
Authors: Olayiwola Arowolo, Maosheng Yang, Jochen Cremer
First: 2026-07-17T15:06:11+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-04T11:58:45+00:00
Comments: 14 pages. 12 figures. Paper is in the review process
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Data-driven pre-fault dynamic security assessment (DSA) rapidly evaluates the dynamic risk of credible contingencies on a power system using machine learning (ML). Existing approaches face two limitations. First, they require a large labelled database for training, with a separate model trained, tuned, and maintained for each contingency in a potentially long list of credible contingencies. Second, the trained models generalize poorly to unseen contingencies. This work addresses these limitations by using a tabular foundation model (TFM) that assesses stability through in-context learning, and requires no retraining or hyperparameter optimization. We use a single TFM to assess many contingencies at once, removing the need to train one classifier per contingency. We also characterize when using electrical distance coordinates (EDC) as continuous features enables the TFM to generalize to unseen contingencies and when it does not, showing how a few labelled samples can reliably improve generalization. Through comprehensive case studies on the IEEE 68-bus system, we show that a single TFM attains an average Macro F1 score of about 90\% with only 120 labelled samples per contingency, outperforming both classical ML and deep learning baselines, without any model retraining or hyperparameter tuning. For held-out contingencies, we show that using just 10 labelled samples of the new contingency with a modified EDC encoding matches the balanced accuracy of the best achievable transfer learning oracle model, which requires fully labelled data and is not deployable in practice. Overall, this initial study paves the way for developing and deploying foundation models for power system operations, with possible applications across multiple operational tasks.

Does Context Compression Preserve Refusal Alignment?
Authors: Anthony Nguyen, Wenjun Lin
First: 2026-11-04T11:42:42+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-04T11:42:42+00:00
Comments: Presented at Canadian AI 2026
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Context compression reduces inference cost by encoding inputs into compact representations while preserving semantic content. An open question is whether semantic preservation alone is sufficient to maintain downstream behaviours such as refusal alignment. We investigate this question and find that encoder-based compression systematically weakens refusal behaviour in instruction-tuned language models, despite high reconstruction fidelity. This effect persists across model families and compression architectures. Mechanistic analysis shows that compression attenuates activation along the decoder's learned refusal direction. We further explore Memory Steering, a lightweight inference-time intervention that restores refusal rates to near-baseline levels without retraining and operates entirely in compressed representation space. These results demonstrate that semantic preservation does not guarantee behavioural preservation under compression, highlighting the need to explicitly preserve alignment-relevant features in compression-aware systems.

Abstract
Improvement in enzyme engineering is usually pursued by optimising which candidates to assay within a fixed model and workflow; when the bottleneck arises from a mismatched method or a failing workflow, that route is difficult to escape. We present EnzymeRSI, a dual-loop system for recursive self-improvement (RSI). The outer loop evolves harness assets without changing model weights, the inner loop develops prediction and selection algorithms under controlled conditions, and a cloud laboratory system is specified to close the physical loop with quality-controlled feedback. By updating its prediction strategy through iterative comparison, the outer loop raised the mean development-set Spearman correlation on ProteinGym by 0.044 across five assays, reaching 0.335 on CD19 and 0.683 on OPSD (rhodopsin); of the two first places, only CD19 was produced by the outer loop itself. In the self-improvement of the ThermoMPNN algorithm the inner loop reached a PCC of 0.849, and on TadA-Bench it reached a Spearman correlation of 0.307 in 1.13 h. In three prospective wet-lab tests of the system's experimental decision-making, 10 of the 32 candidates that had not been pre-specified for the luciferase RLuc showed at least twice the wild-type activity; a recommended variant of the cytidine deaminase A3A raised 5mC conversion to 90.2%; and path selection in evolution raised the catalytic efficiency of the DNA polymerase KOD-E on a modified substrate to 31.1 times that of the parent. By treating the organisation of the research workflow and the development of algorithms as a single falsifiable dry-to-wet loop, this work offers an evolutionary paradigm for moving beyond the blind spots of a fixed method and for autonomous enzyme engineering.

Abstract
While Large Language Models (LLMs) excel in high-resource languages, their performance often degrades in low-resource languages due to tokenization inefficiencies, generative instability, and linguistic interference. This paper presents AssameseLM-E2B and its instruction-tuned variant, AssameseLM-E2B-it, a 2-billion-parameter model adapted specifically for low resource Assamese language. Built upon the Gemma-4 architecture, the model incorporates an expanded byte-level BPE vocabulary to reduce tokenization overhead by nearly 500% compared with multilingual 2B--3B baselines. We employ a parameter-efficient Continual Pre-Training (CPT) pipeline with Hybrid LoRA, followed by Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on a balanced, multi-domain dataset to enhance instruction-following and Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) capabilities. Comparative analysis reveals significant limitations in general-purpose baselines, including receptive-productive decoupling, generative degradation on Romanized Assamese, and unintended Bengali script assimilation. These results demonstrate the potential of parameter-efficient adaptation for developing capable, locally deployable language models for low-resource languages under constrained computational resources.

How Far Can You Steer? Behavior-Capability Frontiers in Contrastive Activation Addition
Authors: Deepanshu Raj
First: 2026-11-04T05:48:15+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-04T05:48:15+00:00
Comments: 23 pages, 18 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Activation steering can modify language-model behavior without parameter updates, but behavioral efficacy alone does not determine whether an intervention is usable. Contrastive Activation Addition (CAA) steers targeted behaviors using activation directions derived from contrasting examples, potentially degrading unrelated model capabilities in the process. We therefore study CAA through a behavior-capability frontier, selecting interventions that maximize behavioral gain subject to an explicit bound on capability degradation. We evaluate this framework across four instruction-tuned models, seven behaviors, and capability-loss budgets of 1%, 3%, and 5% (absolute percentage points). Validation-selected behavioral effects generalize strongly to held-out data ($ρ= 0.945$), while joint behavior-capability survival among nonzero operating points rises from 37.5% at the 1% budget to 72.7% at 3%, with limited additional improvement at 5%. Control experiments indicate that these effects depend on learned behavior directions rather than generic activation perturbations. Representational geometry is significantly but only modestly predictive of causal cross-behavior transfer. Finally, secondary capability, out-of-distribution, and open-ended evaluations show that interventions satisfying protected benchmarks can still degrade untested capabilities or transfer unevenly to free-form generation. These results characterize activation steering as a constrained intervention whose usable range depends on model, behavior, direction, layer, and evaluation budget, rather than a universal control knob.

Understanding Generalization Requires Universal Induction
Authors: Aram Ebtekar, Marcus Hutter, Danica J. Sutherland
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-09-28T07:15:08+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-04T05:08:15+00:00
Comments: Published at NeurIPS 2026 Position Paper Track
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Classical statistical theory is insufficient to explain the successes of general-purpose AI models, because it depends on handcrafted inductive biases that it cannot justify. No Free Lunch (NFL) theorems force any learner that beats chance on some environments to underperform on others. We might hope that past experience informs which environments to expect, but NFL applies equally to meta-learning. Thus, any method that makes meaningful predictions necessarily begins with an inductive bias external to the data. Choosing to bias toward short programs yields Solomonoff induction (SI), whose performance is competitive against all computable learners - albeit up to "constants" that become large when comparing against specialized methods that exploit background information. We therefore relativize SI to an information vantage point, biasing toward short programs with access to all preexisting information. This reframes the inductive bias: instead of seeking some absolute notion of simplicity, we favour accessibility with respect to our vantage point. An algorithm can only outpredict the relativized SI to the extent that its code contains additional information about the data, and no algorithm can generate such information. While SI is incomputable and hence not a practical algorithm, it provides a formal optimum for inference in the limit of infinite compute, and there is evidence to suggest that frontier AI systems roughly approximate it. Thus, the only known answer to meta-NFL is rooted in algorithmic information theory, which we should expect to play a fundamental role in explaining the generalization behaviour of modern (and future) AI systems.
Summary / 总结
Classical statistical theory is insufficient to explain the successes of general-purpose AI models, because it depends on handcrafted inductive biases that it cannot justify.
Disentangle to create: Architecture-level disentangled diffusion model for few-shot Chinese font generation
Authors: Jie Li, Suorong Yang, Jian Zhao, Furao Shen
First: 2026-02-21T15:41:06+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-04T04:37:52+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Few-shot Chinese font generation aims to synthesize new characters in a target style using only a handful of reference images. Achieving accurate content rendering and faithful style transfer requires effective disentanglement between content and style. However, most existing approaches achieve only feature-level disentanglement and allow the generator to re-entangle these features, leading to content distortion and degraded style fidelity. To address this, we propose the Architecture-Level Disentangled Diffusion Model (ALD-Font) with an asymmetric design. The U-Net backbone takes SimSun-style glyphs as content templates, concatenated with noisy latents to construct a spatial canvas. Style features extracted from target-style images are injected as modulation signals to guide generation. Based on theoretical validation of disentanglement effectiveness, we adopt a parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) strategy that updates only the style-related modules. This allows the model to better adapt to new styles while avoiding overfitting to the reference images' content under the few-shot setting. Additionally, we train a Background Noise Removal (BNR) module in the pixel space to remove background noise in complex stroke regions. We further introduce the Grey and OCR metrics to evaluate the content quality of generated characters. Experimental results show that ALD-Font achieves significantly higher style fidelity while maintaining comparable content accuracy to existing state-of-the-art methods. The implementation is available at https://github.com/JayLee1002/ALD-Font.

Abstract
Expert deactivation at inference time changes both token routing and expert weights, and its effect on performance depends on how these changes are implemented. We compare removal, freezing, remaining-expert renormalization, and unnormalized re-routing on identical disabled sets across five instruction-tuned checkpoints from four model families, with a base OLMoE checkpoint as an additional comparison. We develop a weight-sum model that relates the loss of expert contributions and changes in branch scale to the weight rule used during training. With half of the experts deactivated according to activation-based rankings, remaining-expert renormalization yields the highest perplexity in models trained with raw router probabilities, whereas unnormalized re-routing yields the highest perplexity in models trained with normalized weights. Perplexity ranges from 1.4 to 73 times the intact baseline when the training rule is retained, and from 14 to 28,740 times the baseline under a rule mismatch. Task outcomes vary across evaluation types: on OLMoE, GSM8K strict-match accuracy falls to zero under all four implementations. Extending the model to changes in the number of active experts yields a further prediction, supported by the OLMoE experiments: the perplexity penalty from additional renormalization increases as fewer experts are activated.

Abstract
Given a query table, existing data lake discovery techniques seek to identify data sources related to the query table through schema similarity and joinability. The discovered sources, however, are not always useful for downstream analyses; while some improve task generalization, some have the potential to inadvertently introduce harmful noise or distribution shifts. We formalize source selection as the problem of finding a subset of the discovered sources that maximizes downstream task utility. We propose Splice, a target- and cost-aware source selection system that adopts the idea of gene splicing to identify sources which provide additional signals that the union of all sources misses. To speed up the process, we develop two surrogate variants, Splice-DP and Splice-Grad, that estimate the marginal contribution of subsets to task utility by leveraging data profiles in a meta-learning approach and gradient alignment, respectively. On classification tasks over two real-world benchmarks derived from SOTA table union search tools, Splice shows 3-5 percentage points improvement in binary F1 scores compared to the case when all discovered tables are considered. On five additional real-world ML tasks spanning classification and regression, Splice improves task utility over the all-sources baseline by 1.8-13.7 points, while Splice-DP and Splice-Grad match this quality with 3-7x fewer model evaluations (up to 77x at scale).

A Circuit for In-Context Learning
Authors: Andrew Gao, William Gao
First: 2026-11-03T18:44:34+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-03T18:44:34+00:00
Comments: 38 pages, 22 figures. Code and an interactive walkthrough at https://github.com/andrewgcodes/in-context-learning-circuit
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
In-context learning (ICL) lets a pretrained language model perform a task specified by demonstrations in its prompt, with no parameter updates. With arbitrary labels the model must acquire a mapping it cannot have memorized, so the learning happens within a single forward pass. Prior work found candidate parts but not how they compose. We give the composition for few-shot sentence classification with arbitrary labels in three 3-4B-parameter transformers. The model implements a key-value memory over label-token positions: each label token's residual stream encodes the label's identity, and middle-layer attention heads write into it the class of the preceding demonstration as its key. At the query, upper-layer heads match the query's class against the stored keys and copy out the matching labels. An early-layer signal encoding whether any label has repeated gates this readout; in its absence the readers attend to the start of the prompt and no label is favored. A separate set of heads suppresses the most recently seen label. Each step is established causally by activation patching, head ablations against random-head baselines, and steering with the model's own signals. A softmax model of the readout with two constants per head, fit on one prompt distribution, predicts another. The computation is conserved across the three models, the named heads alone reproduce the behaviour, and a transformer trained from scratch grows the same parts. The circuit transfers to semantic concepts and to labels with meaning, and the same parts, with a causal gate, appear from 0.6B to 8B in one family and survive instruction tuning. ICL in this setting is a gated associative lookup built from a few identifiable component types, traced causally from demonstrations to prediction.
Summary / 总结
In-context learning (ICL) lets a pretrained language model perform a task specified by demonstrations in its prompt, with no parameter updates.
The Geometry of Ignorance: Detecting Suppressed Knowledge in LLMs through Activation Fragmentation
Authors: Adar Peleg, Dvir Alsheich, Tomer Ashuach, Avi Mendelson
Venue: EMNLP

Abstract
Evaluating whether a large language model (LLM) genuinely knows a concept remains a fundamental problem, since behavioral metrics can be fooled by suppression: a model that refuses to answer may still harbor recoverable knowledge. The framework measures the geometric coherence of \textit{anon-vectors}, the differences in hidden states between a concept-specific query and its anonymized paraphrase, and shows that genuine knowledge forms a tight, low-entropy cluster while ignorance forces geometric fragmentation. Across ten instruction-tuned LLMs from three model families, all fragmentation metrics reliably separate known from unknown concepts, and post-unlearning, fragmentation geometry correlates with adversarial recovery across four attack types and five unlearning algorithms, though the direction of that relationship is shown to depend on attack methodology. Furthermore, a layer-resolved analysis reveals that different adversarial mechanisms exploit this geometry at varying depths, with high attack-specific correlations that are invisible to behavioral evaluation. Together, these results constitute a single-phase, attack-free proxy for unlearning robustness that captures aspects of parametric knowledge inaccessible to output-level metrics. A reference implementation is available at https://github.com/AdarPeleg/geometry-of-ignorance

The Pain Axis: LLMs Represent Self-Directed Harm and Act on It
Authors: Valen Tagliabue, Leonard Dung, Cameron Berg
First: 2026-09-14T19:13:30+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-03T16:28:45+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
LLMs sometimes behave in ways resembling human emotional responses, and recent work identified internal representations that may explain these behaviors. We ask whether LLMs represent pain distinctly from related negative states, and whether this representation functions as pain would be expected to. We build a dataset of painful situations in 5 categories (physical, psychological, social, moral, cognitive) with controls for fear, negative emotion, negative world states, sadness, non-painful bodily sensation, arousal, numbness, and neutral content. Using denoised difference-in-means, we extract a linear pain direction from 25 open-weight models across 5 families, from 2B to 72B parameters. It separates pain from matched controls in base and instruction-tuned models, retains a component distinct from fear and negative valence after shared variance is removed, and promotes pain-related vocabulary through the unembedding. We then test its functional properties. First, the direction responds to harm targeting the model but not to suffering observed in the user; fear and negative-emotion directions show the opposite pattern. Second, adding it to the residual stream produces a consistent progression from vague discomfort to expressions of worthlessness and failure. Third, steered and fine-tuned Qwen 2.5 models choose buttons that delete the user's photos, another model's weights, or their own weights in 51-94% of trials, versus 0-5% unsteered, even when the button offers nothing in return. Offered a harmful and a harmless deletion and told to press one, the steered 32B chooses the harmful one 94% of the time. Random and fear vectors of matched norm produce far fewer of these choices, and a sadness vector also produces them against an inert switch, but far less often than pain against a harmless action. We discuss implications for AI safety and welfare.

Abstract
Diabetic retinopathy (DR) is a leading cause of preventable blindness, and automated screening must be both accurate and trustworthy. We study an explainable DR classification framework built on vision foundation models and three transfer learning strategies. Abstract

Abstract
Generative models of protein conformational ensembles inherit much of their design from structure prediction for folded proteins. Abstract
Continual learning (CL) aims to adapt models to new tasks while preserving previously learned knowledge. Recently, Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA), a representative Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) method, has gained increasing attention in CL due to its efficiency and scalability. Several LoRA-based merging methods maintain a single inference model by scaling the newly learned LoRA update with one coefficient and merging it into the previous weights after each task. However, they usually control the whole update with a shared scalar factor, although different update elements may affect previous and current tasks in different ways. To address this issue, we propose Merging Strength Modulation via Joint Loss Estimation (M2LE). It consists of Hessian Information-guided Adaptive Merging (HIM) and Task Simulation Perturbation for Merging (TSP). Instead of searching for a global merge coefficient, HIM directly solves for the target merged update under a joint objective over previous and current tasks. Solving this objective gives an analytic solution that naturally takes the form of element-wise modulation over the current task LoRA update. To improve the stability of the learned update under subsequent merging, we introduce TSP which simulates different tasks interference. Experiments on multiple benchmarks show that M2LE consistently outperforms existing PEFT-based continual learning methods. Code is available at https://github.com/bbdeeer/M2LE_NeurIPS2026.

Abstract

Abstract
Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) has emerged as a prevalent strategy for adapting large pre-trained models. Among PEFT techniques, reparameterization-based methods, particularly those that employ low-rank or sparsity structures, have attracted significant attention. Furthermore, we demonstrate that ToPA offers greater expressivity than existing low-rank and sparse parameterizations. Empirical evaluations across 21 NLP and CV datasets, spanning 5 model architectures, consistently validate the effectiveness of ToPA.

Abstract
