Generative Latent Priors for Interpreting and Steering Protein Language Models
Authors: Daniel Hinjos, Lucía Urcelay
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-11-06T18:06:39+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-06T18:06:39+00:00
Comments: Accepted at the Interpretability for Discovery (Interp4Discovery) Workshop at the 40th Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS 2026)
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Dominant methods for interpreting large models impose strong structural assumptions. Generative Latent Priors (GLP) are a complementary approach: a diffusion model that learns the distribution of a frozen model's activations directly, imposing no assumptions. We port GLP to ESM-2 activations (UniRef50) and evaluate it at two host scales (ESM-2-8M and -650M), both as a lens for protein representations and as a prior for editing them. Generation quality scales smoothly with depth and compute. As a lens, single GLP "meta-neurons" are the strongest probes of discrete Swiss-Prot concepts at both hosts, beating InterPLM's released SAE features, and lead on continuous properties at 8M though not at 650M; falsification tests show this is mostly concentration, not new information. As an editing prior, the on-manifold correction makes DiffMean steering of three biochemical properties effective and restores on-manifoldness, but only in isolation: re-embedding the sequences actually produced after several rounds of decoding, the structural plausibility is compromised. This is mainly reproduced in additional SAE-based clamping structural steering experiments, where only Helix motif succeeds. The naturalness failure is traced to the rewriting coverage and the decoder rather than the prior, highlighting what the method must clear before it can serve for both discovery-by-editing and discovery-by-exploration. Trained GLPs are released on huggingface.co/collections/HPAI-BSC/glprot.

Breaking the Privacy Promise: Gradient Inversion Attacks on Federated Low-Rank Adapted Vision Transformers
Authors: Ritik K. Badiya, Siladittya Manna, Ashmit Sinha, M Yashwanth, Anirban Chakraborty
Venue: WACV 2027
First: 2026-11-06T16:02:20+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-06T16:02:20+00:00
Comments: Accepted at WACV 2027
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) has been increasingly adopted to fine-tune pretrained models with low computational cost and is therefore also preferred in the federated learning paradigm to reduce computational and communication overhead. In this work, we investigate the sparsely explored problem of the risk of private data leakage in LoRA-adapted Vision Transformers (ViTs), where low-rank updates weaken the gradient signal available to a server with access to individual client updates. We show that quality of reconstruction is limited by two coupled ambiguities: reduced information in LoRA gradients and missing spatial cues from frozen positional embeddings. To address this, we propose a spatially anchored generative inversion framework that incorporates server-prescribed trainable positional embeddings into ViT blocks during fine-tuning at clients. The resulting positional gradients act as layer-wise spatial anchors, providing additional positional cues, while the generative prior keeps reconstructions on a realistic image manifold, reducing duplicate-data and patch-permutation artifacts, {which produce similar gradients as those produced when the target client images are used}. Adding our proposed spatial anchoring to GIAS improves PSNR by 10.14 dB, SSIM by 0.45, and LPIPS by 0.098 on CIFAR-10, as well as PSNR gains of 7.83 and 11.74 dB on high-resolution CelebA and CARS datasets, respectively, in the FedSGD setting. Such gains in reconstructed image quality metrics are also maintained at higher batch sizes and in FedAvg settings. These results indicate that LoRA-based PEFT does not, by itself, prevent data leakage in FL, and carefully incorporated positional embeddings can help in exposing private information.

Knowledge Graph-based Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Schema Matching
Authors: Chuangtao Ma, Sriom Chakrabarti, Arijit Khan, Bálint Molnár
First: 2025-01-15T09:32:37+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-06T14:56:30+00:00

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Traditional similarity-based schema matching methods fail to resolve semantic ambiguities and conflicts in complex, domain-specific mappings due to a lack of commonsense and domain-specific knowledge. In parallel, large language models (LLMs) without sufficient background knowledge tend to hallucinate, further complicating schema matching. To overcome these limitations, we introduce KG-RAG4SM, a Knowledge Graph-based Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Schema Matching. This method integrates novel retrieval mechanisms, including LLM-based, vector-based, graph traversal-based, and hybrid techniques with advanced reranking schemes to extract the most relevant subgraphs from large external KGs. Our approach achieves superior results without requiring re-training, as evidenced by improvements of 39.79% in precision and 30.56% in F1 score over LLM-based state-of-the-art (SOTA) methods (e.g., Jellyfish-8B) on the MIMIC dataset, and enhancements of 69.20% in precision and 21.99% in F1 score over pre-trained language model (PLM)-based SOTA methods (e.g., SMAT) on the Synthea dataset. Additionally, KG-RAG4SM scales effectively across KGs from commonsense KGs to domain-specific ones, while its instruction-tuning knowledge exclusion component ensures truthful performance in end-to-end schema matching tasks despite the suboptimal relevance of KG context. Our empirical analysis and case studies from real-world scenarios show that the hallucination problem in LLMs for schema matching is well mitigated by our solution.

Discovering Update Rules for Opponent Shaping in General-Sum Games
Authors: Hong Jun Lee, Ziyan Wang, Jun Wang, Stefanos Leonardos, Yali Du
First: 2026-11-05T17:23:34+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-06T12:51:37+00:00
Comments: v2: new title, revised Section 3.2
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
In general-sum games, self-interested learners can reach outcomes that are poor for everyone, which has motivated methods that shape the opponent's learning. Methods like Learning with Opponent-Learning Awareness (LOLA), which differentiates through the opponent's learning process, and Model-Free Opponent Shaping (M-FOS), which uses model-free optimisation methods for long-horizon opponent shaping, have enabled mutual cooperation and exploitation of learners. However, these methods require access to the opponent's parameters, policy, rewards or learning rule during deployment. In single-agent reinforcement learning, update rules discovered by meta-learning have achieved state-of-the-art results (e.g. DiscoRL). These rules are separate from the policy they train and read only the agent's own experience. We hereby extend this framework to general-sum settings by introducing Discovered Update Rule for Opponent Shaping (DUOS). To the best of our knowledge, this is the first method to discover an update rule for an agent in general-sum games. DUOS meta-trains the rule to maximise the agent's expected return over its lifetime, and updates an inner network using the agent's own experience. We show that our discovered rule exhibits behaviour consistent with opponent shaping without reading opponent policies or parameters, and ranks 1st in normalised mean score and 1st in self-play among tournament entrants.

Contrastive Distribution Matching for Amortized Sequential Monte Carlo in Discrete Diffusion
Authors: Jaihoon Kim, Taehoon Yoon, Prin Phunyaphibarn, Seungjun Kim, Morteza Mardani, Minhyuk Sung
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-05-22T08:06:52+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-06T11:51:06+00:00
Comments: Project Page: https://cdm-smc.github.io/ (NeurIPS 2026)
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Discrete diffusion models have emerged as powerful frameworks for generating structured categorical data. However, efficiently sampling from reward-tilted distributions remains a fundamental challenge. While Twisted Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC) offers asymptotic exactness for this task, estimating the optimal twist function in discrete state spaces necessitates costly Monte Carlo approximations, resulting a severe computational bottleneck at inference. To overcome this limitation, we introduce Contrastive Distribution Matching (CDM), a novel framework that amortizes the cost of SMC inference by learning a parameterized twist function via positive and negative samples. For efficient training, we reformulate the gradient estimator to leverage the closed-form forward kernels of discrete diffusion models. In practice, evaluating our learned twist function incurs less than 5% additional computational overhead compared to a single forward pass of the base model. Through extensive empirical evaluations, we demonstrate that CDM consistently outperforms existing baselines under matched wall-clock time. We validate the effectiveness and versatility of our approach across a diverse range of applications, including toxic text generation, regulatory DNA sequence design, protein designability, and diffusion large language model alignment.

Discrete diffusion models have emerged as powerful frameworks for generating structured categorical data.
Do We Still Need Humans in the Loop? Human vs. LLM Annotation in Active Learning for TikTok Hate Speech Detection
Authors: Ahmad Dawar Hakimi, Lea Hirlimann, Isabelle Augenstein, Hinrich Schütze
First: 2026-04-15T14:10:58+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-06T09:11:55+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Annotating data remains a costly bottleneck for supervised NLP. Active learning (AL) reduces the number of human labels needed by selecting only the most informative instances, while instruction-tuned LLMs attack the same bottleneck from the other side, making labels cheap enough to annotate entire corpora. This raises two questions: can LLM labels replace human labels within the AL loop, and does AL remain necessary when entire corpora can be cheaply labeled? We investigate both by training supervised hate speech classifiers on a new dataset of 278K German political TikTok comments, comparing human and LLM annotation under matched conditions. LLM annotation at scale outperforms human-supervised classifiers at roughly one-tenth the cost, for both a closed-source (GPT-5.2) and an open-weight (Qwen3.5-122B-A10B) LLM, and the advantage is robust under soft-label evaluation. It hinges on the annotation interface: only a two-question decomposition mirroring the human annotation task unlocks it. AL provides no reliable advantage over random sampling in our prefiltered pool. Error structure depends on the LLM: only GPT-5.2 matches the human FP/FN balance, while other variants over-flag border-control and economic-competition discourse. Humans remain essential as evaluators; for training labels, the question shifts to which LLM, which interface, and what shape of pool.

Annotating data remains a costly bottleneck for supervised NLP.
PixelBench: Can LLMs Understand and Generate Images Pixel by Pixel?
Authors: Haoquan Zhang, Feiyang Liu, Juanxi Tian, Wanhua Li, Haodong Duan, Weiyang Liu
First: 2026-11-02T05:10:12+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-06T06:52:20+00:00
Comments: Technical report, 38 pages, 7 figures. Project page: https://haoquanzhang.github.io/pixelbench
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Large language models (LLMs) can generate visual assets through text-based formats such as SVG and CAD, but these symbolic representations describe images using high-level primitives rather than explicit raster values. We ask whether LLMs can instead generate pixel images directly through their existing vocabulary, without a learned image tokenizer or decoder. We introduce PixelBench, a benchmark for text-to-pixel (T2P) and image-to-pixel (I2P) generation across multiple resolutions, together with a complementary pixel-understanding evaluation. Models generate an RGB color palette followed by a grid of indices specifying the palette color of each output pixel. In T2P, the strongest models produce recognizable images directly from text. In I2P, the strongest models outperform palette-matched downsampling and specialized pixelization algorithms at low resolutions. Pixel-understanding performance correlates strongly with generation performance. A resolution-scaling study shows that pixel-generation performance often improves initially before declining at high resolutions. Finally, we introduce Pixel Reconstruction Tuning (PRT), which augments visual instruction tuning with caption-palette-grid trajectories. We post-train Molmo2-4B from its pretraining checkpoint by mixing general instruction data with our curated PRT data, improving performance across 16 visual-centric benchmarks, indicating that explicit pixel reconstruction can provide useful auxiliary supervision.

When Can You Trust Neural Critics? Auditing Mutual Information Estimates of Higher-Order Epistasis in Protein Language Models
Authors: Maryam Rahimimovassagh, Waldemar Karwowski, Niloofar Yousefi
First: 2026-11-06T06:20:28+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-06T06:20:28+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Mutual information (MI) estimation is used to probe what neural representations encode, including in protein language models, but variational MI estimators have variance that scales at least exponentially in the true MI. We audit a prior neural-MI study of order-resolved epistasis in ESM-2 on the four-site GB1 landscape. Run-to-run differences show no systematic bias and a magnitude comparable to ordinary cross-seed retraining variability, and the evaluation-pairing scheme shifts scores substantially. At the original dimensionality, two of three alternative estimator families stabilize the estimate across seeds and one does not. In a synthetic calibration at the representation dimensionalities of two other protein language models (a Gaussian channel with known MI; no model representations), all three families recover materially less signal than required, despite correct signal ordering and stable seeds. A control critic given the true signal direction, which also reduces its input to one dimension, recovers 93.3% and 98.7% of the analytic MI at gain 1.5 (at 5,120 and 4,096 dimensions), so the signal is recoverable with privileged information, but the control does not identify why blind estimation fails. We package the checks into a reusable diagnostic protocol and conclude that even a stabilized estimator is not currently sufficient for order-resolved dependence claims made under blind search at this dimensionality and sample size.

Beyond Truncation? Entailment-Licensed Elision and the Limits of Semantic Preservation in LLM Decoding
Authors: Mahmoud Alyosify, Mirna Imbabi, Eman Elkhamisi
First: 2026-11-06T04:39:25+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-06T04:39:25+00:00
Comments: 8 pages, 3 figures, 2 tables
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Instruction-tuned large language models (LLMs) answer at greater length than a request needs. We ask whether a training-free controller inside the decoding loop can remove that surplus while preserving content, judged against the simplest possible baseline: truncating the model's own answer to the same length. An entropy-gated logits processor removes 25.5-34.9% of tokens on MT-Bench development questions and 19.9-34.2% on a sealed test split within pre-registered BERTScore non-inferiority margins, yet we prove and confirm by audit that its output is always a prefix of the unmodified answer: the saving is truncation. A survey of 23 training-free mechanism families finds none that selects content better than truncation without oracle labels. We therefore introduce Entailment-Licensed In-Decoding Elision (ELIDE), which, at each sentence boundary, withholds a span from the reader only when a natural-language inference model judges it entailed by the text already shown, while leaving the model's generation unchanged. ELIDE beats matched-length truncation on three model families, but reading 936 of its elisions on 670 public prompts shows that 59% harm the answer, a failure that no automatic measure detects. Scoping the licence to its discourse unit reduces the harmful share to about one elision in ten on two fresh tests.

Enabling Approximate Joint Sampling in Diffusion LMs
Authors: Parikshit Bansal, Sujay Sanghavi
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2025-09-25T21:23:26+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-05T23:41:05+00:00
Comments: Accepted to NeurIPS 2026
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
In autoregressive language models, each token is sampled by conditioning on all the past tokens; the overall string has thus been sampled from the correct underlying joint distribution represented by the model. In contrast, masked diffusion language models generate text by unmasking tokens out of order and potentially in parallel. Generating an overall string sampled from the correct underlying joint distribution would (again) require exactly one token unmasking in every full-model forward pass. The more tokens unmasked in parallel, the further away the string is from the true joint; this can be seen in the resulting drop in accuracy (but, increase in speed). In this paper we devise a way to {\em approximately} sample multiple tokens from the joint distribution in a single full-model forward pass; we do so by developing a new lightweight single-layer ``sampler" on top of an existing large diffusion LM. One forward pass of the full model can now be followed by multiple forward passes of only this sampler layer, to yield multiple unmasked tokens. Our sampler is trained to mimic exact joint sampling from the (frozen) full model. We show the effectiveness of our approximate joint sampling for both pretrained-only (Dream-7B-Base, Llada-7B-Base) and instruction-tuned (Dream-7B-Instruct, Dream-7B-Coder) models on language modeling and math \& coding tasks. When four tokens are unmasked for each full-model denoising step, our sampling algorithm achieves a MAUVE score of 0.87 (vs marginal baseline of 0.31) with respect to the true joint distribution.

Abstract
Selecting small, effective instruction-tuning datasets requires identifying which aspects of the original data to retain. We introduce Follow the Features (FtF), a data-selection method that uses sparse autoencoder (SAE) features to characterize training data and guide subset construction. FtF ranks features using dataset-level activation statistics, scores samples by their expression of highly ranked features, and incorporates feature resampling to encourage diversity. The LLM-SAE pair used for selection is independent of the model being fine-tuned, enabling reuse across target models. We evaluate FtF on two model-dataset settings at a training budget of 100 samples with a fixed sequence length of 256 tokens. FtF achieves performance competitive with GRAPE and S2L, with relative rankings varying across settings. On Qwen 3 8B trained on Alpaca, FtF exceeds full-dataset training by 1.59 percentage points in the harmonic mean of an AlpacaEval-based win rate and Open LLM Leaderboard performance, although full-dataset training retains a higher Leaderboard score. These results support SAE feature statistics as a useful signal for data-efficient instruction tuning.

Abstract
Geospatial foundation models (GFMs) are reshaping remote sensing by shifting the field from task-specific modeling toward general-purpose geospatial intelligence. Mapping the damage to buildings stands to benefit substantially from this shift, as GFMs pretrained on large-scale Earth observation data provide generalizable geospatial representations that can facilitate effective responses to sudden-onset disasters. Although parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) facilitates GFM adaptation, parameter efficiency does not necessarily translate into label efficiency. Building damage mapping remains challenging due to scarce pre-existing labeled data or limited time for annotation. When weak supervision is introduced to alleviate the labeling bottleneck, we identify the difficulty of learning sufficiently discriminative representations as a key challenge for label-efficient GFM adaptation. To address this challenge, we propose a Positive-Unlabeled GFM Adaptation (PUMA) framework that requires annotations for only a small number of damaged buildings. Extensive experiments on disaster benchmark datasets from around the world demonstrated the effectiveness and robustness of PUMA. A real-world application to the 2025 Los Angeles wildfires further highlighted its potential to accurately map damage under highly limited supervision. More broadly, these findings suggest that weakly supervised PEFT offers a label-efficient pathway from GFMs to real-world applications.

Abstract

Abstract
Accurate coding of tumor diagnoses with ICD-10-GM and ICD-O-3 is essential for structured cancer documentation in Germany. Smaller open-weight LLMs are appealing for privacy-preserving automation but often struggle with coding accuracy in German-language contexts. This study investigates whether instruction-based fine-tuning on public datasets improves the coding accuracy of open-weight LLMs for German tumor diagnosis texts. The evaluation uses coded diagnoses from the local tumor documentation system as test data. In a systematic data quality assessment, the upper limit for ICD-10 coding performance was estimated at 60-79% for exact and 81-94% for partial (three-character codes only) derivation. Eight open-weight models from the Qwen, Llama, and Mistral families (7-70 B parameters) were fine-tuned. ICD-10-GM accuracy rose from 1.4-24% to 41-58%, and partial accuracy from 31-74% to 73-83%. Malformed code outputs dropped to 0% for all models. Tumor-diagnosis recognition reached 99%. Accuracy correlated positively with model size, but gaps between small and large models narrowed after fine-tuning. The reasoning mode in Qwen3 generally yielded a lower performance than fine-tuning and was over 100 times slower. Our findings highlight the potential of leveraging public catalogues to build instruction datasets that improve LLMs in medical documentation tasks. The complete training dataset and the best-performing checkpoints of the fine-tuned models are available from https://huggingface.co/datasets/stefan-m-lenz/ICDOPS-QA-2024.

Abstract
Black-box optimization (BBO) addresses problems where objectives are accessible only through costly queries without gradients or explicit structure. Classical derivative-free methods---line search, direct search, and model-based solvers such as Bayesian optimization---form the backbone of BBO, yet often struggle in high-dimensional, noisy, or mixed-integer settings. Recent advances use machine learning (ML) and reinforcement learning (RL) to enhance BBO: ML provides expressive surrogates, adaptive updates, meta-learning-based algorithm selection, and generative models, while the RL--BBO interface includes dynamic operator configuration, robust policy search, and meta-optimization across tasks. This paper surveys these developments, covering representative algorithms such as the modular model-based optimization framework (mlrMBO), zeroth-order adaptive momentum methods (ZO-AdaMM), automated BBO (ABBO), distributed block-wise optimization (DiBB), partition-based Bayesian optimization (SPBOpt), the transformer-based optimizer (B2Opt), diffusion-model-based BBO, surrogate-assisted RL for differential evolution (Surr-RLDE), robust BBO (RBO), coordinate-ascent MORE with step size adaptation (CAS-MORE), log-barrier stochastic gradient descent (LB-SGD), policy improvement with black-box (PIBB), and offline Q-learning with Mamba backbones (Q-Mamba). We further review benchmark efforts such as the NeurIPS 2020 BBO Challenge and the MetaBox framework. A controlled numerical study further confirms that ML-based candidate screening can improve the evaluation-budget performance of direct search and differential evolution, while the benefit remains solver-dependent.

Abstract
Bayesian optimization for Hyperparameter Optimization (HPO) and Combined Algorithm Selection and Hyperparameter Optimization (CASH) often relies on explicit hyperparameter encodings as surrogate inputs. In high-dimensional search spaces, however, such representations can make it difficult for standard surrogates to learn useful performance models. We instead study an alternative meta-learning representation that describes each configuration directly as its historical performance across prior tasks. This representation can be paired directly with standard surrogate models. Our scope is the iterative selection component of AutoML, deciding which configuration to evaluate next from a fixed candidate set, rather than a complete framework with features such as runtime estimation, cost-aware budgeting and ensembling. We further apply this representation to maximal marginal relevance (MMR), a diversity-based re-ranking criterion from recommender systems, as a posterior-free acquisition rule. Abstract
