Newton-Based Methods for Bilevel Optimization Beyond Lower-Level Strong Convexity
Authors: Youran Dong, Junfeng Yang, Wei Yao, Jin Zhang
First: 2026-10-19T17:58:34+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T17:58:34+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Bilevel optimization provides a framework for machine learning tasks such as hyperparameter optimization, data reweighting, and meta-learning. Existing Newton-based bilevel methods typically rely on the classical hypergradient formula, which is generally unavailable when lower-level solutions are nonunique and the lower-level Hessian is singular. We develop two Newton-based methods beyond lower-level strong convexity by exploiting curvature shared by the lower-level and multiplier updates. Newton-based GALET handles potentially singular lower-level curvature under a positive spectral-gap condition, exploiting its range structure to avoid the squared-Hessian multiplier system used in GALET. Newton-based BAMM uses a strongly convex aggregation model when the lower-level objective is convex and the upper-level objective is strongly convex in the lower-level variable. We establish convergence guarantees and improved complexity bounds relative to GALET and sl-BAMM under the corresponding assumptions. We further introduce GNBO, a generalized Newton-based framework that captures the shared-curvature structure of the proposed methods and several existing Newton and quasi-Newton bilevel methods. Experiments on synthetic problems, data hyper-cleaning, and GPT-2 data reweighting evaluate the proposed methods across different bilevel learning tasks.

Scaling Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning for Brain Encoding
Authors: Zhuofan Josh Ying, Guanglong Sun, Nolan Ho, Kieran Liu, Rui Yang, Andrew Chen, Nikolaus Kriegeskorte, Pony Ma, Hossein Adeli
First: 2026-10-19T17:46:08+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T17:46:08+00:00
Comments: 38 pages, 13 figures, 20 tables. Code available at https://github.com/zfying/brain-lora
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Brain encoding models aim to predict neural responses to stimuli. The most widely used approach fits readouts from frozen pretrained backbones. Using neural data to train large models from scratch or fully fine-tune them after task-pretraining has proven difficult because neural data is costly and limited. We show that parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) enables us to effectively adapt very large pretrained backbones for brain encoding with a small number of trainable parameters. To make it computationally practical, we exploit the backbone's causal structure to predict multiple timepoints in a single pass instead of using sliding windows, significantly reducing feature-extraction cost. This enables efficient fine-tuning of multimodal backbones with up to 104B parameters, the largest model adapted for brain encoding to our knowledge. Trained with a low-rank adapter (LoRA) and a lightweight readout, our approach consistently improves brain encoding performance across nine diverse multimodal backbones. On the same Qwen3-Omni backbone as the prior state-of-the-art (SoTA), our method BrainLoRA achieves the best published performance on the Algonauts 2025 Challenge. We also show the adapted multimodal backbones remain interpretable: modality attribution recovers the canonical sensory topography that prior work often obtains by construction from separate unimodal encoders, and neural adaptation increases the contribution of audio inputs and cross-modal mixing within the backbone. To scale out the number of adapted models, we develop a multi-LoRA kernel that accelerates joint training of multiple LoRAs on a shared backbone. This enables an ensemble of 200+ adapted models that ranks first on the public Algonauts post-challenge leaderboard at the time of writing. Together, our results highlight PEFT as an efficient and powerful paradigm for brain encoding.

TokenGate: Adapting LLMs for Fast and Memory-Efficient Prefill with Learned Token Pruning
Authors: Liana Patel, Rishabh Ranjan, Ion Stoica, Matei Zaharia, Carlos Guestrin
First: 2026-10-19T17:15:53+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T17:15:53+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Expert human readers process large inputs (e.g., code repositories and long documents) by skimming to closely read only what matters. On the other hand, language models process all input tokens through each model layer, resulting in significant processing costs for long-context inputs. We introduce TokenGate, a small, drop-in component that extends existing trained models to enable end-to-end learnable token pruning over long-context inputs for fast and memory-efficient prefill. To enable end-to-end learning despite the non-differentiable top-K operator for token pruning, we introduce token-gated attention, a mechanism inspired by Mixture-of-Experts that re-weights attention logits using token importance scores. Across diverse tasks, TokenGate achieves $1.4$-$11.3\times$ faster time-to-first token (TTFT) and $1.5$-$3.4\times$ lower peak memory at no accuracy degradation for prefill in transformers. Compared to the best-performing prior methods for token-pruning, TokenGate gives up to $6.4\times$ faster prefill and $2\times$ lower peak memory. TokenGate natively extends both standard transformers (e.g., Qwen3) and hybrid-attention models (e.g., Qwen3.5) with simple lightweight adaptation comparable to standard parameter-efficient fine-tuning. We make our code available at: https://github.com/tokengate-models/TokenGate.

recAMPLIFY: Large-Scale Pretraining of Recursive Protein Language Models
Authors: Saurav Jha, Qianli Liao, Robert Vernon, Sarath Chandar, Quentin Fournier
First: 2026-10-19T17:12:55+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T17:12:55+00:00
Comments: Models: https://huggingface.co/chandar-lab/recamplify Code: https://github.com/chandar-lab/AMPLIFY/tree/recamplify
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Protein language models (pLMs) are powerful tools for protein annotation and mutation-effect prediction, but progress has largely depended on scaling model size and data, making them expensive and prone to memorization. Test-time scaling offers a complementary path, but methods developed for autoregressive LLMs do not transfer directly: masked encoders lack a generation loop, and amino acids do not verbalize in a reasoning language. Instead, we adapt recursive pretraining to masked pLMs, where a weight-tied Transformer core refines residue-aligned hidden states over an adjustable number of cycles. We instantiate this recipe as recAMPLIFY, a pLM with 155M unique parameters trained on the same 2T tokens as AMPLIFY-350M. Despite 56% fewer parameters, it improves CASP contact recovery and three out of four supervised tasks, notably raising fold accuracy by 22.5%. Because optimal depth for recAMPLIFY varies across tasks and assays, we then introduce Gated Mixture Recurrence (GMR), a framework that decodes each protein from a probability-weighted mixture of hidden states visited during recursion. GMR employs a learned residual gate at scalar, low-rank, or per-dimension resolution to control how much of each cycle's proposed update is accepted into the state. This unifies halting and gating into a single adaptive computation objective while recovering PonderNet-style halting as a special case without gates. Layered on top of recAMPLIFY, GMR further reduces expected layer depth by up to 37%, although the accuracy trade-off varies by task. Mechanistic analyses show that residual gating controls recurrence stability beyond the trained depth and that halt depth tracks biological properties.

The Effectiveness and Durability of Pretraining Data Filtering in Protein Language Models
Authors: Aaron Maiwald, Roderick McNeill, Jaeyoung Lee, Matthew A. Clarke, Lennart Justen, Alejandro Acelas, Ivo Andrews, Giles Harper-Donnelly, Adam Moore, Sophie Rose, Oliver M. Crook, Felix Jackson
First: 2026-10-19T16:08:59+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T16:08:59+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Pretraining filtering is one of the most common safeguards for open-weight language models. Here we study its effectiveness, durability and scalability using the ESM-2 family of protein language models. We find that filtering viral proteins from the pretraining data meaningfully degrades masked language modelling, mutation effect prediction and long-range contact prediction on viral proteins, while largely preserving performance on non-viral proteins. These effects are concentrated among proteins from human-infecting viruses, and grow as we scale models from 8M to 3B parameters. As models increase in size, we find that stringent filtering becomes more effective. However, fine-tuning on sequences from the removed viral families restores the lost performance. Although the cost to recover performance increases with model scale, even for the largest models it costs about 80 USD of H100 time. This work establishes pretraining filtering as moderately effective at selectively degrading performance on viral proteins, but not durable against fine-tuning with modest computational resources and access to relevant data.

REATS: LLM Reasoning-based Ensemble Learning for Adaptive Time Series Forecasting
Authors: Xu Zhang, Chang Xu, Hui Sun, Nan Ma, Zijian Zhang, Peng Wang, Wei Wang, Li Zhao
First: 2026-08-10T19:04:46+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T14:52:03+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Due to the diversity of real-world time series, no single forecasting model consistently dominates across all samples. Ensemble learning addresses this by combining complementary model strengths, yet existing methods rely on fixed rules or black-box models based solely on numerical inputs, failing to leverage LLM reasoning for interpretable weighting decisions. We propose REATS, which leverages LLM reasoning capabilities as an intelligent ensemble router that jointly processes textual temporal pattern descriptions and numerical features to produce interpretable, sample-adaptive ensemble weights through chain-of-thought reasoning. To enable effective LLM-based ensembling, we study its key design choices and propose: (i) a structured input pipeline that transforms raw time series into hybrid textual--numerical representations with fixed token cost, enabling rule-based chain-of-thought construction without API dependency, augmented with retrieved similar-sample priors; (ii) a diverse multi-row weight supervision scheme coupled with a token-efficient percentage-table format that reduces numerical complexity and mitigates LLM hallucinations; and (iii) a two-stage fine-tuning framework combining SFT with GRPO, where a reciprocal reward mapping transforms the continuous unbounded MSE gap into bounded signals with amplified near-oracle sensitivity, addressing the uniform sensitivity and outlier-dominated advantage compression inherent in naive reward designs for regression-based GRPO. Experiments on eight benchmarks demonstrate that REATS outperforms competitive ensemble baselines while providing natural language explanations and demonstrating strong transfer learning and out-of-domain generalization to unseen candidate models.

An Empirical Study of Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning for Mixture-of-Experts LLMs
Authors: Minglei Li, Peng Ye, Haonan He, Jingqi Ye, Qingyu Yang, Tao Chen
First: 2026-10-19T13:28:03+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T13:28:03+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods are critical for adapting large models under resource-constrained settings. However, as mixture-of-experts (MoE) architectures, which offer efficient inference at massive parameter scales, gain increasing prevalence in the community, a critical gap arising from the lack of systematic recipes for applying PEFT to MoE models. To narrow this gap, we conduct a large-scale systematic study that investigates a set of essential design recipes, including which modules should be adapted, which granularity should be used to adapt MoE modules, which PEFT method to use, and to what extent should the load-balancing objective be used, across three MoE backbones on downstream tasks and general knowledge tasks. Our experiments yield several key insights. First, adapting MoE experts alone achieves the highest downstream performance with competitive general capability retention under a comparable parameter budget. Second, native expert-weight updates offer a better downstream--retention trade-off than adapters that bypass experts or entire MoE modules. Third, LoRA and DoRA together define the strongest operating region for high downstream accuracy among the evaluated PEFT methods. Fourth, no nonzero load-balancing coefficient Pareto-dominates the zero baseline. We finally evaluate a weight-level redesign of MoE$^2$-LoRA on Qwen3. Moving its module-level bypass pool into native expert weights improves all four upstream endpoints by 2.4 to 17.9 points with 17% fewer trainable parameters and a 3.2-point average downstream cost.

SOS-LoRA: Static Orthogonal-Subspace Low-Rank Adaptation with Fixed Multi-Scale Scaling
Authors: Yupeng Chang, Yuan Wu, Yi Chang
First: 2026-06-27T03:25:36+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T13:14:03+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) is a widely used parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) method for large language models. Under a fixed rank budget, LoRA parameterizes each adapted weight through a single low-dimensional input-side pathway, which may couple heterogeneous behaviors through shared input directions and induce interference during optimization. We propose Static Orthogonal Subspace LoRA (SOS-LoRA), a drop-in extension that reparameterizes a rank-rtot update as a sum of K static (always-on, non-routed) low-rank experts. SOS-LoRA (i) decomposes the total rank across experts, (ii) applies a fixed multi-scale scaling scheme to encourage scale-separated optimization dynamics, and (iii) promotes diverse input-side directions via cross-expert orthogonal initialization and a lightweight regularizer. SOS-LoRA remains fully mergeable, adding no inference-time parameters or latency after merging. Experiments on reasoning and knowledge-intensive benchmarks (Llama 2/3), encoder-based NLU (GLUE), and math reasoning (GSM8K/MATH) show consistent gains over matched-budget LoRA baselines and recent variants. Code is available at https://github.com/llm172/sos-lora.

Shaping the Document, Not the Model: Optimized Suffixes for Context Following
Authors: Gia-Bao Dinh Ho, Sekh Mainul Islam, Masahiro Kaneko, Timothy Baldwin, Isabelle Augenstein
First: 2026-10-19T12:01:22+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T12:01:22+00:00
Comments: 19 pages, 5 figures, 18 tables. Project page: https://context-gcg.github.io
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
When a user supplies a document and wants the answer drawn from it, a language model is expected to follow it even when it contradicts its parametric knowledge. Prior work has focused on making the model rely on context by steering activations, contrasting logits during decoding, or instructing it in plain language. We ask whether the document alone, with no instruction written into it and no access to the model at inference time, is enough. We optimize a document suffix by discrete token search, starting from the answer to a single question and extending the objective to a set of passages, to multi-hop questions that follow from the planted fact, and to later turns of a conversation. On counterfactual passages that contradict parametric knowledge of the model, the suffix flips nearly all items the model resists across three instruction-tuned model families, in most settings outperforms the decoding baseline and the strongest instruction we could write, and matches activation steering. Controls show that the suffix largely acts through the passage rather than injecting the answer, and a single suffix per model transfers to unseen passages. Each extended objective carries the effect to its target, so the objective, not only the document, sets how far the effect reaches.

Conditional information Retrieval for Longitudinal Survey Harmonisation
Authors: Wing Yan Li, Zeqiang Wang, Michelle Lin, Jon Johnson, Suparna De
First: 2026-10-19T11:03:31+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T11:03:31+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Identifying conceptually equivalent questions across different surveys is a challenging information retrieval task where surface-level textual similarity is insufficient. Two questions may be lexically similar yet assess entirely different sociological sub-concepts, or lexically different yet conceptually equivalent. We introduce a conditional information retrieval framework that addresses this by decomposing the task into two steps: concept extraction, which identifies the underlying sociological concept of a survey question using a fine-tuned generative model, and concept-aware retrieval, which augments question representations with the extracted concept as an explicit conditioning signal for finer-grained alignment. To reflect the realistic data scarcity conditions of specialised social science annotation, we use CLOSER as a case study. Our framework consistently outperforms baselines by 2-3 points in both MAP and Recall. We further analyse fine-tuning strategies including SFT, DPO, and dynamic DPO, finding that SFT delivers the most stable gains while dynamic DPO interacts differently with instruction-tuned versus distilled reasoning backbones. Finally, we demonstrate that a model fine-tuned on a general social science thesaurus (ELSST) transfers robustly to task-specific survey data, offering a practical solution under data scarcity.

Residual-Guided Adaptive Tucker Rank Allocation for Parameter-Efficient MCI Conversion Prediction
Authors: Guanghua He, Hancan Zhu, Gaohang Yu, An Zhang
First: 2026-10-19T06:33:23+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T06:33:23+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Allocating limited trainable capacity across convolutional layers is a central challenge in parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) of pretrained three-dimensional (3D) networks. We propose Residual-Guided Adaptive Tucker Rank Allocation, which jointly selects input- and output-channel ranks under a fixed adapter-parameter budget while preserving the spatial kernel modes. To guide rank selection, we learn task-specific proxy updates from downstream training data and approximate them using candidate Tucker rank pairs. A perturbation analysis bounds the output discrepancy between the fixed proxy network and its Tucker approximation in terms of layerwise reconstruction residuals, motivating residual-based allocation. We normalize each squared residual by the corresponding proxy-update energy to compare relative approximation errors across layers. Dynamic programming minimizes their sum under a global budget constraint that accounts for both channel factors and the Tucker core. This yields a globally optimal allocation over the specified candidate rank pairs. We evaluate the method on the Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI) cohort for predicting conversion from mild cognitive impairment to Alzheimer's disease within 36 months, using hippocampal segmentation pretraining. Across five random seeds, the proposed method achieves a mean area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) of 0.7706, compared with 0.7239 for fixed-ratio Tucker adaptation and 0.7430 for full fine-tuning. The total number of trainable parameters is limited to approximately 0.618 million, accounting for only 1.45% of the full model. Adaptive Tucker rank allocation thus provides an effective approach to parameter-efficient transfer learning in 3D medical imaging.

Abstract
Protein language models produce rich per-residue representations, but the sequence embeddings obtained by mean-pooling them are never trained to place related proteins near one another, so the geometry that retrieval, clustering and nearest-neighbor transfer depend on is never optimized. We present ProtSent, a contrastive post-training framework that supervises a protein language model with three types of biological relation at once, Pfam family co-membership, AlphaFold DB structural clusters and STRING interactions, and with deep mutational scanning fitness ordering as an optional fourth. It needs no structure at training or inference, trains on corpora filtered at 40% sequence identity against the benchmark test sets they most resemble, and is applied to ESM-2 (35M and 150M parameters) and ESM-C (300M). Post-training makes structural family membership recoverable from the frozen embedding without a trained readout: on SCOPe-40, the adjusted Rand index of the 35M model rises from 0.054 (near chance) to 0.507, and family retrieval gains +0.185, +0.190 and +0.424 Recall@1 over the three frozen backbones, with paired bootstrap intervals excluding zero. On the CATH midnight-zone benchmark, homologous-superfamily accuracy rises from 40.7% to 56.7% at 35M and from 43.3% to 62.7% at 150M, more than the 12 points a CATH-supervised model gains over its own base. Against a maximally sensitive HMMER search, alignment keeps the best single hit at both ESM-2 scales, but the embedding leads at ranking depth (MAP 0.705 against 0.607 at 150M) and returns a ranking for every query, including those where alignment finds none. Under a trained linear probe, however, the same embeddings fall below the untuned backbone, as do structure-distilled embeddings against theirs, so the gain in neighborhood structure comes at a cost in linear-probe accuracy. Abstract
Federated learning (FL) is a distributed paradigm for Internet of Things (IoT) edge networks. However, deploying Vision-Language Models (VLMs) over wireless edge networks presents distinct challenges: large model parameters increase uplink transmission latency over bandwidth-constrained fading channels, and non-independent and identically distributed (Non-IID) data exacerbates client drift. Existing feature alignment solutions often introduce auxiliary computations that consume time slots allocated for wireless parameter transmission, increasing the likelihood of straggler dropouts under system delay constraints. To address these issues, we propose DPGR-GCAM, a communication-efficient federated domain generalization framework. By employing a dual-parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) architecture, we design a channel-aware tiered transmission mechanism that adaptively truncates payloads to maximize node survival rates under fading conditions. GCAM operates within the low-dimensional feature manifold to dynamically resolve multi-objective optimization conflicts with minimal edge computation latency, thereby reducing computation-induced network outages. Experiments demonstrate that DPGR-GCAM improves cross-domain generalization and sustains convergence under network fading scenarios with a 10\% client participation rate.

Abstract
Public leaderboards rank large language models (LLMs) in general, but an engineer has to choose a model for a specific application, architecture and cost. This paper presents a method for application-specific capability profiling of LLMs and its realization as a containerized testbed. The method treats each application as a unit of extension: an Oracle-to-PostgreSQL migration agent, a documentation-grounded chatbot or an enterprise-support automation agent runs in its own container behind a small contract, brings its own dataset, evaluator and owned attributes, and is onboarded together with its dashboards. Measurements accumulate in a versioned, sparse metamodel of every model. The method separates the model under test from judges and agents, freezes and hashes plans, refuses to compare values from different contexts, diagnoses the instrument before the system, and applies a null-agent check and a noise rule whose operating characteristics are quantified by simulation. In the evaluated automation conditions, model choices produced larger differences on selected observable outcomes than framework choices, although the experimental design does not establish a general causal ranking of these factors. The experiments also show that instruction tuning is deployment-specific and that system-reported success can exceed independently observed success: of 216 migration units checked against a real Oracle database, 108 were demonstrably correct. These measurements establish application-specific observations, not a universal model ranking.

Abstract
State Space Models (SSMs) offer an efficient alternative to Transformers for sequence modeling, yet conditioning pre-trained SSMs for iterative generation typically operates outside the recurrent operator, through input injection or activation modulation. While such mechanisms expose the model to conditioning information, they leave the underlying temporal dynamics fixed. We introduce MaRK (Markov-adapted Recurrent Kernels), a dynamic operator-conditioning framework that maps context vectors directly into bounded modulations of a frozen SSM's recurrence ($A$), read-in ($B$), read-out ($C$), skip ($D$), and discretization ($Δ$) parameters. Viewed through the lens of LPV-SSM systems, MaRK induces a context-indexed family of Markov parameter sequences, allowing each diffusion timestep to reshape the model's input-output memory kernel. We instantiate MaRK on a frozen 111M-parameter Hydra SSM backbone and study three adapter geometries: Hypernet, Chebyshev polynomial, and Discrete Cosine Transform kernels. Since these adapters modify the Markov parameter sequence through low-rank auxiliary maps on the frozen backbone, parameter-efficient fine-tuning arises as a structural consequence of the adaptation mechanism itself, requiring only 6.3--11M trainable auxiliary parameters to transition from a bidirectional objective to an iterative diffusion regime. The bounded recurrence parameterization further yields an analytic Affine Quadratic Stability certificate for the modulated recurrence. Through synthetic LPV recovery experiments and Markov-operator diagnostics, we show that MaRK recovers coordinate-invariant temporal operators under matched assumptions and produces distinct, stable timestep-conditioned memory profiles. Empirically, the Chebyshev variant yields the strongest performance, achieving an average validation loss of 2.55, followed by the DCT (2.59) and Hypernet (3.77) geometries.

Abstract
Vision-language (VL) models are typically built by fine-tuning a language model to attend to visual features in service of a Visual Question-Answering objective. We ask whether this alignment changes how language models represent text---especially on tasks where visualization would help, like geometry questions posed in words. We distinguish weak multimodality (the model can answer questions about an input image) from strong multimodality (the model's text representations themselves become vision-aligned, improving performance on text-only tasks with latent visual structure). To test for strong multimodality, we construct a task of shape-counting problems with paired equivalent text and image specifications. This task is trivial when the image is provided, but requires visualization when specified in text as line coordinates. We compare instruction-tuned Qwen-2.5 and Mistral models against their vision-aligned counterparts on the text variant of the task. We find that the multimodal models do no better in this setting, despite strong performance on the image version of the task. Probing experiments corroborate this: vision-aligned decoder embeddings carry no more visual structure than their text-only counterparts. Finally, we provide an optimization objective that distills in-context visual embeddings into text embeddings. This alignment induces gains on the text variant of the task without explicit task supervision, even on samples more complex than seen during alignment. This suggests that VQA-style alignment leaves available cross-modal transfer untapped.
