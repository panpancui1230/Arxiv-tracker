Aggregation of Published Non-Uniform Axial Power Data for Phase II of the OECD/NEA AI/ML Critical Heat Flux Benchmark
Authors: Reece Bourisaw, Reid McCants, Jean-Marie Le Corre, Anna Iskhakova, Arsen S. Iskhakov
First: 2025-06-18T16:01:44+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T16:44:18+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Critical heat flux (CHF) marks the onset of boiling crisis in light-water reactors, defining safe thermal-hydraulic operating limits. To support Phase II of the OECD/NEA AI/ML CHF benchmark, which introduces spatially varying power profiles, this work compiles and digitizes a broad CHF dataset covering both uniform and non-uniform axial heating conditions. Heating profiles were extracted from technical reports, interpolated onto a consistent axial mesh, validated via energy-balance checks, and encoded in machine-readable formats for benchmark compatibility. Classical CHF correlations exhibit substantial errors under uniform heating and degrade markedly when applied to non-uniform profiles, while modern tabular methods offer improved but still imperfect predictions. A neural network trained solely on uniform data performs well in that regime but fails to generalize to spatially varying scenarios, underscoring the need for models that explicitly incorporate axial power distributions. By providing these curated datasets and baseline modeling results, this study lays the groundwork for advanced transfer-learning strategies, rigorous uncertainty quantification, and design-optimization efforts in the next phase of the CHF benchmark.

Authors: Fadel Thior, Ahmer Tabassum, Haania Siddiqui, Hanan Aldarmaki, Thamar Solorio

Abstract
Code-switched (CS) automatic speech recognition (ASR) remains difficult because real CS speech is scarce and synthetic speech does not fully match it. We investigate whether synthetic CS speech is more useful for learning an adaptation-friendly initialization than for ordinary continual fine-tuning. We propose SyRA-CS, a pair-specific synthetic-to-real adaptation framework based on Reptile meta-learning. For each language pair and pretrained model, we meta-train on the corresponding synthetic CS-FLEURS subset and then adapt using one hour of labeled real target speech. We evaluate four architectural settings: Encoder-CTC, FastConformer-RNN-T, Attention Encoder-Decoder (AED) and Speech-LLM. Experiments cover on Arabic-, French-, German-, and Hindi-English, against synthetic-only transfer, real-only fine-tuning, and continual fine-tuning. SyRA-CS improves over continual fine-tuning on WER in all 16 model-pair comparisons and on CER in 15 of 16, with relative macro WER reductions of 17.7% for XLS-R and 5.3-5.8% for the other three models. The gains hold across the tested models but vary in size across architectures, language pairs and metrics.

SQuAT: Spherical Lattice Vector Quantization-Aware Training
Authors: Sebastian Loeschcke, Ivan Koryakovskiy, Tycho F. A. van der Ouderaa, Riccardo Del Chiaro, Mart van Baalen, Paul N. Whatmough, Markus Nagel
First: 2026-10-22T15:14:44+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T15:14:44+00:00

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Vector quantization (VQ), in particular lattice-based methods, offer strong rate-distortion trade-offs for low-bit large language models (LLMs), yet their behavior under quantization-aware training (VQ-QAT) remains poorly understood. We scale lattice VQ-QAT to billion-parameter LLMs and multi-billion-token training regimes, and introduce SQuAT, a shape-gain VQ-QAT approach trained with a separate straight-through estimator (STE) for the shape and gain. SQuAT decomposes weights into direction and magnitude: normalized directions are quantized using an $E_8$-derived spherical codebook, while gains are quantized independently with low-bit scalar quantizers. We show that, unlike a standard STE applied to the full quantizer, which ignores the shape-gain structure, these separate STEs induce tangent-space updates for directions, yielding an implicit form of spherical (Riemannian) optimization. To enable scalability, we develop a distributed exact nearest-neighbor assignment scheme, in which workers quantize disjoint weight blocks and communicate only discrete indices, reducing VQ-QAT training time by 12.8$\times$ and allowing us to scale it to 10B tokens. Across instruction-tuned LLaMA-3.2-1B/3B, Qwen3-4B, and base Qwen3-1.7B, SQuAT consistently outperforms standard VQ-QAT STE under identical quantizers and initialization, and achieves SOTA VQ-QAT accuracy.

DISC: Domain-Integrated Similarity Cost for Time-Series Dataset
Authors: Yen-Ku Liu, Hongjie Chen, Franck Dernoncourt, Ryan Rossi
First: 2026-10-22T13:52:09+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T13:52:09+00:00

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
The growth of time-series datasets and foundation models has made dataset comparison a central part of time-series workflows. Assessing source-target transfer before model adaptation requires similarity estimates that can summarize transfer-related dataset relationships without exhaustive source-target evaluation over every candidate pair. Many metrics have been proposed for measuring similarity between time-series datasets, but they capture different dataset relationships and vary in how well they align with downstream utility across tasks. We evaluate this gap by measuring the correlation between source-target similarity scores and downstream forecasting errors in out-of-distribution and transfer-learning settings. We propose Domain-Integrated Similarity Cost (DISC), a learnable aggregation framework that uses calibration transfer observations to select, weight, and combine similarity metrics for different downstream tasks. By integrating complementary metric values, DISC better distinguishes source-target dataset differences that matter for downstream behavior. After calibration, DISC scores source-target pairs using only their similarity-metric values and yields higher alignment than single-metric references on the reported split. These results show that time-series dataset similarity is task-dependent, and that learned metric integration provides a stronger proxy for downstream transfer behavior than manually selecting one similarity metric.

Authors: Selen Erkan, Bastian Boll, Kristian Kersting, Björn Deiseroth, Letitia Parcalabescu

Abstract
Benchmark scores often misrepresent a large language model's (LLM's) knowledge, because they rely, e.g., on the model's ability to follow specific formatting requirements. This especially penalizes base models that may know the correct answers but lack the ability -- typically introduced in post-training -- to structure them as instructed. To overcome this, we propose an efficient, automated, and architecture-agnostic method for model evaluation based on soft-prompt tuning. By optimizing only 10 soft-prompt vectors (~0.0006% parameters for a 7B model) over a short tuning period, we adapt models to specific benchmark formats, closing gaps in format-following and ensuring that underlying knowledge is accurately reflected in benchmark scores. We evaluate soft-prompt tuning across 7 models and 11 datasets. Our results show that (a) soft-prompt tuning saturates format-following within 150 steps (~1200 samples) while preserving knowledge accuracy, (b) it closely matches strong evaluation-harness baselines on well-studied benchmarks while requiring no manual benchmark-specific prompt or parser design, (c) it improves evaluation signal in settings where baselines are harder to transfer, including long-context datasets and instruction-tuned variants with different output styles, and (d) it produces cleaner outputs that improve agreement between exact match scoring and independent LLM judges. Overall, we propose a lightweight, automated, and flexible protocol based on limited-budget soft-prompt tuning for evaluating LLM knowledge.

Poisoning-Based Backdoors Are Not Cryptographic
Authors: Masahiro Kaneko
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-10-20T11:27:30+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T12:19:07+00:00
Comments: NeurIPS 2026
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Defending against a backdoor requires recovering the verifier that decides whether an input is a trigger, since blocking individual triggers fails against an attacker who can generate new ones. If a verifier could be installed with the asymmetry of cryptographic authentication, where only the attacker can produce triggers and the defender cannot reconstruct the verifier, defense that learns the verifier from observations would be impossible in principle. We show that no such asymmetry can arise when the verifier enters training only through the labels of poisoned data, which covers the dirty-label backdoors that dominate the literature on language models. A training procedure that installs a verifier from poisoned data is itself a learner for that verifier. Hence any verifier that is hard to learn cannot be installed through poisoning, and any verifier that poisoning can install is reconstructible by a white-box defender holding as many labeled trigger observations as the attacker used. Hiding a secret key in the input or the weights does not change this. Experiments on instruction-tuned large language models confirm the dichotomy, with no configuration yielding a backdoor that is both effective for the attacker and irrecoverable for the defender.

Abstract
While autonomous vehicles have achieved reliable performance within specific operating regions, their deployment to new cities remains costly and slow. A key bottleneck is the need to collect many human demonstration trajectories when adapting driving policies to new cities that differ from those seen in training in terms of road geometry, traffic rules, and interaction patterns. In this paper, we show that self-play multi-agent reinforcement learning can adapt a driving policy to a substantially different target city using only the map and meta-information, without requiring any human demonstrations from that city. We introduce NO data Map-based self-play for Autonomous Driving (NOMAD), which enables policy adaptation in a simulator constructed based on the target-city map. Using a simple reward function, NOMAD substantially improves both task success rate and trajectory realism in target cities, demonstrating an effective and scalable alternative to data-intensive city-transfer methods. Few-shot Classification as Multi-instance Verification: Effective Backbone-agnostic Transfer across Domains
Authors: Xin Xu, Eibe Frank, Geoffrey Holmes
First: 2025-07-01T03:34:20+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T11:16:36+00:00

Abstract
We investigate cross-domain few-shot learning under the constraint that fine-tuning of backbones (i.e., feature extractors) is impossible or infeasible---a scenario that is increasingly common in practical use cases---and show how representing few-shot classification as a series of multiple instance verification (MIV) tasks enables competitive classification performance using the static embeddings produced by frozen, "black-box" backbones. More specifically, based on this novel representation, we introduce a new approach to few-shot domain adaptation named the "MIV-head": a classification head that is backbone-agnostic and computationally efficient. We show that the core components of the MIV-head, when trained on few-shot data from a target domain, collectively yield strong performance on test data from that domain. Importantly, they do so without fine-tuning the backbone, requiring only the "meta-testing" phase. In experiments under various settings on an extension of the Meta-Dataset benchmark for cross-domain few-shot image classification, using representative off-the-shelf convolutional neural network and vision transformer backbones pretrained on ImageNet1K, we demonstrate that the MIV-head achieves highly competitive accuracy when compared to state-of-the-art "adapter" (or "partial fine-tuning") methods applied to the same backbones, while incurring substantially lower adaptation cost. Moreover, compared to our approach, well-known "classification head" approaches achieve a substantially lower level of accuracy. We share our code at: https://github.com/xxweka/MIV-head.

Authors: Ruchira V Bhat, Rahul Bhowmick, Avinash Singh, Krishna Kumar Sabapathy

Abstract
The preparation of quantum Gibbs states is a fundamental challenge in quantum computing, essential for applications ranging from modeling open quantum systems to quantum machine learning. Building on the Meta-Variational Quantum Eigensolver framework proposed by Cervera-Lierta et al. and a problem-driven ansatz design, we introduce two meta-learning algorithms: Meta-Variational Quantum Thermalizer (Meta-VQT) and Neural Network Meta-VQT (NN-Meta VQT) for efficient thermal state preparation of parametrized Hamiltonians on Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum (NISQ) devices. Meta-VQT utilizes a fully quantum ansatz, while NN Meta-VQT integrates a quantum--classical hybrid architecture. Both use collective optimization over a finite set of training parameter values to prepare approximate Gibbs states at held-out parameter values within the sampled parameter domain. We validate our methods on Transverse Field Ising Models (TFIM) with up to eight qubits and on a two-qubit Heisenberg model with all field terms. Within these finite-size benchmarks, the trained models accurately prepare thermal states at test points inside the training interval. For the six and eight qubit cases of TFIM, meta-learned parameters when combined with appropriately designed ansatz serve as warm-start initializations, significantly outperforming random initializations in the optimization tasks. Furthermore, a 3 qubit Kitaev ring example showcases our algorithm's effectiveness across finite-temperature crossover regimes. Finally, we apply our algorithms to train a Quantum Boltzmann Machine (QBM) on a 2-qubit Heisenberg model with all field terms, achieving enhanced training efficiency, improved Gibbs state accuracy, and a 30-fold runtime speedup over existing techniques such as variational quantum imaginary time (VarQITE)-based QBM highlighting the practicality of meta-algorithm-based QBMs.

Survival is the Only Reward: Sustainable Self-Training Through Environment-Mediated Selection
Authors: Jennifer Dodgson, Alfath Daryl Alhajir, Michael Joedhitya, Akira Rafhael Janson Pattirane, Surender Suresh Kumar, Joseph Lim, C. H. Peh, Adith Ramdas, Steven Zhang Zhexu
First: 2026-01-18T08:35:56+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T07:26:55+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Self-training systems often degenerate due to the lack of an external criterion for judging data quality, leading to reward hacking and semantic drift. This paper provides a proof-of-concept system architecture for stable self-training under sparse external feedback and bounded memory, and empirically characterises its learning dynamics and failure modes. We introduce a self-training architecture in which learning is mediated exclusively by environmental viability, rather than by reward, objective functions, or externally defined fitness criteria. Candidate behaviours are executed under real resource constraints, and only those whose environmental effects both persist and preserve the possibility of future interaction are propagated. The environment does not provide semantic feedback, dense rewards, or task-specific supervision; selection operates solely through differential survival of behaviours as world-altering events, making proxy optimisation impossible and rendering reward-hacking evolutionarily unstable. Analysis of semantic dynamics shows that improvement arises primarily through the persistence of effective and repeatable strategies under a regime of consolidation and pruning, a paradigm we refer to as negative-space learning (NSL), and that models develop meta-learning strategies (such as deliberate experimental failure in order to elicit informative error messages) without explicit instruction. This work establishes that environment-grounded selection enables sustainable open-ended self-improvement, offering a viable path toward more robust and generalisable autonomous systems without reliance on human-curated data or complex reward shaping.

Abstract
Large language models have been reported to encode the harmfulness of a request separately from their decision to refuse it, and latent safety monitors such as Latent Guard build on this report by classifying prompts as harmful from the internal activations of the model, with the aim of catching harmful intent even when the model complies. Whether these representations give a signal of harm that survives distribution shift and is independent of model behaviour is unclear. We test this on out-of-distribution prompts made by rewriting existing harmful and benign requests with surface rewording, persuasive framing and jailbreak-style wrappers. Across 15 instruction-tuned LLMs and 38 activation-based readers, we compare harm detection with that of TF-IDF and sentence-embedding classifiers, with independently trained safety classifiers, and with the refusal of each model. Activations are read at the last token of the user instruction, where the model has read the whole request, and at the last chat-template token before the reply, where the planned response can be represented. Because models disagree about which prompts to refuse, we can separate a signal of the harmfulness of the prompt from a signal of the refusal decision of the model. At the end of the instruction, the representation is largely independent of the refusal behaviour of each model but degrades under distribution shift, so that a TF-IDF classifier ranks harm better than the original Latent Guard in every model we test, and linear and MLP probes do about as well as sentence-embedding classifiers trained on the same examples. The representation just before generation detects more, catching 44 percent of harmful prompts at a matched false-alarm rate against 32 percent for the refusal of the model, but that gain follows the refusal decision itself, and part of it comes from refusals that string-matching evaluation misses.

Authors: Anthony Givans, Michael Crawshaw, Mingrui Liu

Abstract
Transformer models built on the attention mechanism have become a central building block in modern deep learning, yet softmax attention remains a major bottleneck for long-context workloads. While FlashAttention makes the forward and first backward passes I/O-efficient, it does not support backward-over-backward (BoB), which enables exact differentiation through the backward pass for applications such as second-order optimization, test-time training, gradient-based memory, and meta-learning. Existing BoB implementations either materialize large intermediate tensors or exhaust GPU memory at long sequence lengths. We present FlashBoB, an exact, I/O-efficient algorithm for BoB in softmax attention that keeps computation within on-chip tiles and avoids all $N \times N$ intermediate tensors, where $N$ is the sequence length. The key insight is a hierarchical affine structure in the softmax double backward: two row-wise scalars determine all outputs through affine transformations. This yields a two-pass schedule with bounded on-chip static random-access memory (SRAM) usage and minimal off-chip high-bandwidth memory (HBM) traffic. FlashBoB achieves $Θ(N^2 d^2/M)$ HBM traffic ($d$ is the head dimension and $M$ is the memory size) and, within the standard FlashAttention-style score-recomputation model, matches the inherited large-cache lower bound for exact forward attention. Empirically, it scales exact attention BoB to $N=262\text{K}$ on a single A100 80GB GPU, where prior PyTorch exact baselines fail by $N=16\text{K}$, and is up to $6.3\times$ faster than FlashBack. These results make exact second-order attention practical at long-context sequence lengths where prior implementations cannot run efficiently.

A Transformer-based Approach for Real-time Multi-agent Path Planning with Varying Initial/Final Conditions
Authors: Qian Zhang, Shanqing Liu, Jerome Darbon, George Karniadakis
First: 2026-10-21T19:48:21+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T19:48:21+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We address the problem of real-time multi-agent path planning under prescribed initial and final configurations, formulated as a high-dimensional optimal control problem subject to inter-agent collision avoidance and obstacle constraints. Classical solvers based on the Hamilton--Jacobi--Bellman equation or Pontryagin's maximum principle (PMP) become computationally intractable as the number of agents grows, due to the curse of dimensionality and the non-convexity introduced by interaction constraints. We propose OCFormer, a transformer-based neural network that parameterizes the inverse of a coordinate transformation mapping a simple, analytically tractable latent space, in which agents evolve independently under a decoupled Hamiltonian, to the physical space of the coupled, interacting system. The model is trained by minimizing a physics-informed loss comprising the Hamiltonian ODE residual from the PMP and a boundary matching term, without requiring pre-computed trajectory data. The self-attention mechanism endows OCFormer with permutation equivariance over agents, a structural symmetry that prior symplectic network approaches do not natively enforce. When trained on a distribution of boundary conditions, the amortized OCFormer produces near-optimal trajectories for unseen initial and final configurations in a single forward pass ($\sim$220ms), enabling real-time inference without retraining. We further introduce a lightweight transfer learning framework that adapts a pretrained OCFormer to out-of-distribution perturbations in seconds via L-BFGS fine-tuning. Numerical experiments on two- and three-dimensional multi-agent navigation with up to 128 agents demonstrate the scalability and efficiency of the proposed approach.

Abstract
Majority-vote self-consistency (SC) lowers per-problem accuracy on most GPQA Diamond problems for small instruction-tuned models: on 56.6% of problems for Qwen2.5-7B and 65.7% for Llama-3-8B. An obvious fix is a verifier-free confidence gate that decides which problems to vote on. We report that the most natural version of that fix also fails, and we separate three signal failures that v1 treated as one. The token-entropy gate fails for a measurement reason: averaged over a chain of 602 tokens, entropy mostly measures fluency. The chain average sits 0.0005 nats from the non-answer average and 0.1695 nats from the answer token. So we measure confidence at the answer token itself. On Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct-Turbo (198 problems, 64 samples each), a sample that disagrees with its problem's plurality still emits its answer at a median margin of at least 20.4 nats, and at least 75.5% of such samples exceed 10 nats. Both quantities were pre-registered and tested once on 69 problems that no exploratory analysis had seen; both passed. The unit matters. Pooled across the benchmark, the margin does separate correct from incorrect samples: the fraction above 10 nats differs by +0.0586 [+0.0160, +0.0994]. Within a problem, paired, it does not: -0.0142 [-0.0501, +0.0215]. Token log-probabilities do carry information, but not for the within-question choice that self-consistency makes. The agreement gate's failure has no mechanism we can identify, and we leave it open. The new claims rest on one model: a registered replication on a second model was sampled but could not be evaluated, and we report that instead. Separately, three reasoning models could not be evaluated on hosted serverless inference at a small budget, each for a different measured reason. All three are downloadable, so this is a limit of metered APIs, not of what can be known.

Abstract
Reverse target fishing identifies protein targets for a query molecule and is central to drug discovery and repurposing. Discriminative approaches adapted from virtual screening face sparse, long-tailed associations and missing-not-at-random measurements: treating unrecorded pairs as negatives can suppress genuine targets. We reformulate the task as generative retrieval and introduce GenFish. Residual vector quantization converts frozen protein language model features into shared semantic identifiers. Given a molecular fingerprint, GenFish autoregressively models codes conditioned on the query and preceding code, ranking proteins by identifier likelihood without explicitly labeling unrecorded pairs as negatives. Abstract

Abstract
This position paper argues that many behaviours framed as either "stochastic parroting" or "advanced reasoning" are better explained as context-directed extrapolation from training-data priors, and that ML and NLP research communities would benefit from evaluations that distinguish remembering and basic extrapolation from genuine application-level generalisation. Abstract
